## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. `saññā` and `maññati` are not the same lemma

`saññā` is a feminine noun commonly translated according to context as perception, recognition, idea, or conception.

`maññati` is a finite verb meaning, according to context, to think, suppose, deem, imagine, regard, or conceive.

They are not inflectional variants of one another.

Related forms such as `sañjānāti` belong to the same perception/recognition word-family as `saññā`, but `sañjānāti` is a verb and must not be silently treated as the noun `saññā` in morphology claims.

### 2. Snp 4.9 — exact `saññā` forms are context-sensitive

Snp 4.9 contains several `saññā`-family forms:

- `aṇumpi saññaṁ`: `saññaṁ` is accusative singular of `saññā`;
- `saññāvirattassa`: the whole expression is a compound built on `saññā` plus `viratta`; do not mislabel the first member itself as an independently inflected genitive noun;
- `saññañca`: sandhi for `saññaṁ + ca`, again accusative singular plus "and".

The final verse contrasts one detached from `saññā` with those who have taken hold of `saññā` and view and then move through the world in conflict.

**Guardrail:** the verse does not by itself prove that all perception is pathological. It contrasts detachment/non-grasping with taking hold.

### 3. Snp 4.9 — `maññāmahaṁ` and `maññati`

Snp 4.9 contains:

- `maññāmahaṁ`, analyzable as `maññāmi ahaṁ`: first-person singular, "I think / I suppose";
- `yo maññati so vivadetha tena`: `maññati` is third-person singular present.

The immediately preceding line gives the comparison field:

- equal;
- superior / special;
- inferior.

Therefore, in this exact passage, `maññati` is directly connected to comparative self-positioning and dispute.

**Text-attested limit:** this local contextual force must not be promoted into the universal dictionary definition "maññati always means egoic self-positioning." Elsewhere the verb can have broader ordinary senses such as think, deem, or suppose.

### 4. MN 1 — perceiving is followed by conceiving, but the verbs remain distinct

MN 1 repeatedly uses a structure such as:

- one perceives/recognizes an object: `sañjānāti`;
- having perceived it: `saññatvā`;
- one then conceives it / in it / from it: forms of `maññati`;
- one conceives "it is mine";
- one delights in it.

This is strong early-discourse evidence that perception/recognition and conceiving/appropriating are distinguishable operations in the passage.

It is especially important that `sañjānāti` and `maññati` are not synonyms there.

### 5. SN 35.31 — conceiving can take multiple grammatical relations to experience

SN 35.31 gives a repeated pattern for eye, forms, eye-consciousness, contact, and feeling:

- not conceiving the item itself;
- not conceiving in it;
- not conceiving from it;
- not conceiving "it is mine".

This supports the view that `maññati` can organize experience through several relational constructions, including explicit appropriation.

**Guardrail:** the exact Pāli syntax must control interpretation. A modern umbrella term such as "identity construction" is synthesis, not a lexical translation of every instance.

### 6. SN 22.64 — conceiving the aggregates is bondage

SN 22.64 states that when one conceives form, feeling, perception, formations, or consciousness, one is bound by Māra; when one does not conceive them, one is free from Māra.

This gives direct early-text support for treating `maññati` as spiritually problematic in a specific aggregate-appropriation context.

It does not redefine ordinary thinking in every context as bondage.

### 7. MN 18 — perception, thought, and papañca are distinguished

MN 18 gives the sequence:

- contact;
- feeling;
- what one feels, one perceives/recognizes;
- what one perceives, one thinks about;
- what one thinks about, one proliferates.

Therefore:

- perception is not identical to thought;
- thought is not identical to papañca;
- papañca is not identical to all cognition.

The sequence uses the verb `sañjānāti`, related to the `saññā` family, but morphology must still be preserved.

### 8. Snp 4.11 — `saññā` is explicitly linked with `papañcasaṅkhā`

Snp 4.11 says:

`saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`

At minimum, the verse directly presents `saññā` as the source/origin condition in relation to `papañcasaṅkhā`.

The surrounding stanza uses compounds such as:

- `saññasaññī`;
- `visaññasaññī`;
- `asaññī`;
- `vibhūtasaññī`.

These are compound/adjectival forms built around perception, not simple repetitions of the bare noun `saññā`.

**Critical guardrail:** `papañcasaṅkhā` in Snp 4.11 and `papañcasaññāsaṅkhā` in MN 18 must not be silently treated as morphologically identical compounds.

Their relation is an important open lexical question.

### 9. Snp 4.11 — dispute is present in the same discourse, but the whole discourse is not one simple linear chain

Snp 4.11 investigates quarrels and disputes through a series including:

- what is held dear;
- desire;
- pleasant/unpleasant;
- contact;
- name-and-form;
- appropriation;
- perception-related structures.

This makes the discourse highly relevant to conflict-generation.

But it does not license the simplified universal formula:

`saññā -> maññati -> papañca -> dispute`

as if that exact chain were stated in the text.

That chain remains synthesis unless each link is separately sourced.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Perception is not yet self-positioning

The strongest cross-text distinction is:

**`saññā / sañjānāti` identifies, recognizes, or takes up features of experience; `maññati` can then organize what is experienced through interpretive/appropriative relations, including "mine" and comparative self-positioning in specific passages.**

This formulation synthesizes MN 1, SN 35.31, Snp 4.9, and related texts.

No single passage gives this as a universal technical definition.

### B. Self-positioning is directly text-attested in Snp 4.9, but "identity" is still an analytic umbrella

Snp 4.9 directly links `maññati` with the positions:

- equal;
- superior;
- inferior;

and then with dispute.

Calling the broader pattern "identity construction" or "self-positioning" is a useful analytic label, but the early verse itself speaks through comparison and conceiving rather than a modern theory of identity.

### C. `maññati`, `māna`, and `asmimāna` must stay distinct

Snp 4.9 later uses `māna`, while SN 22.89 distinguishes residual `asmimāna` from explicit self-view.

Therefore:

- `maññati` is not simply the noun `māna`;
- `māna` is not automatically identical to `asmimāna`;
- all three can participate in self-referential structures, but they must not be collapsed lexically.

### D. `saññā` is not the enemy

Several early texts use skillful perceptions deliberately, and SN 22 treats `saññā` as one aggregate to be understood rather than as a synonym for defilement.

Thus Snp 4.11's `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā` cannot safely be expanded to:

"all perception necessarily causes harmful proliferation."

A more defensible synthesis is:

**perception supplies distinctions/recognitions that can become material for proliferative construction, but the ethical and liberative issue depends on what follows, how it is grasped, and whether the process is understood and released.**

This is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS.

### E. MN 18 and Snp 4.11 converge but are not identical maps

MN 18 gives an explicit process:

feeling -> perceiving -> thinking -> proliferating -> papañca-related categories assail the person across past/future/present.

Snp 4.11 gives a different poetic investigation of dispute and includes the statement that `papañcasaṅkhā` have `saññā` as source.

A162 treats the texts as mutually illuminating, not as interchangeable formulations.

### F. A126/A144 refinement

A162 preserves prior A126/A144 insight but sharpens the lexical boundary:

- `saññā` != autobiographical memory;
- `saññā` != papañca;
- `sañjānāti` != `maññati`;
- `maññati` != `māna`;
- `maññati` != all thinking;
- `papañca` != all thinking;
- self-positioning and papañca may interact in synthesis but are not synonyms.

### Mil 3.3.10 — perception / `saññā`

Milindapañha characterizes `saññā` by recognition, using recognition of colors as its example.

This is useful later support for the distinction:

`saññā` can function as recognition without itself being equivalent to self-positioning or papañca.

### Mil 3.7.15 — perception, reasoning, and soul

Milindapañha also distinguishes perception/recognition from reasoning/discrimination and rejects the need for an inner soul that performs seeing/hearing.

Role in A162:

- later clarification of functional distinction;
- useful objection-handling after A161;
- no override of MN 1, MN 18, SN 22, SN 35, or Aṭṭhakavagga.

### Effect on interpretation

Milindapañha did **not** change A162's primary interpretation.

It strengthens the functional reading of `saññā` as recognition and supports keeping perception distinct from a permanent self.
