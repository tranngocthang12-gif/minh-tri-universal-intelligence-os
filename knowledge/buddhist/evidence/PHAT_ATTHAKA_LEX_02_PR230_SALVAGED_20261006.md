TEXT_LOCATIONS:
### saññā
- **Snp 4.5 Paramaṭṭhaka, Sn 802:** `pakappitā natthi aṇūpi saññā` in the seen/heard/sensed context. `saññā` is nominative singular feminine; `pakappitā` agrees with it and is semantically crucial: the line concerns fashioned/constructed saññā, not a bare assertion that all perception is absent.
- **Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya, Sn 841:** `nāddakkhi aṇumpi saññaṃ`. Here `saññaṃ` is accusative singular. Translation ranges around “perception/notion/conception”.
- **Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya, Sn 847:** `saññāvirattassa ... saññañca diṭṭhiñca ye aggahesuṃ ... ghaṭṭayantā`. The first is a compound involving saññā + viratta; `saññañca` is accusative singular + `ca`. The verse directly contrasts detachment/dispassion toward saññā with people who grasp saññā and view and then go about clashing.
- **Snp 4.11 Kalahavivāda, Sn 874:** `na saññasaññī na visaññasaññī / nopi asaññī na vibhūtasaññī ... saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`. This contains derived/compound `saññin` forms, not four independent occurrences of the bare noun. The final compound directly assigns saññā as `nidāna` of `papañcasaṅkhā`; this audit records that relation only and does not define or audit papañca.
- **Snp 4.12 Cūḷaviyūha, Sn 892:** `aññatra saññāya niccāni loke`. `saññāya` is an oblique singular ā-stem form (formally syncretic across several singular cases); `aññatra` can govern instrumental/ablative/genitive. `niccāni` is neuter plural and agrees with `saccāni`, not morphologically with feminine `saññā`.

### maññati family
- **Snp 4.5 Paramaṭṭhaka, Sn 799:** `hīno na maññetha visesi vāpi`; `maññetha` is an optative middle 3sg form in the standard paradigm. It occurs with self-ranking as inferior/superior, adjacent to the prohibition on taking oneself as equal.
- **Snp 4.6 Jarā, Sn 806:** `yaṃ puriso mamidanti maññati`; present 3sg `maññati` directly takes the quoted possessive judgment “this is mine”.
- **Snp 4.6 Jarā, Sn 813:** `dhono na hi tena maññati / yadidaṃ diṭṭhasutaṃ mutesu vā`; the purified/sage does not `maññati` in regard to the seen/heard/sensed. The root verse itself does not spell out the full fourfold technical expansion later supplied by Mahāniddesa.
- **Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya, Sn 840:** editions vary between forms such as `maññe-m-ahaṃ` / `maññāmahaṃ`; in context this is ordinary first-person “I think/suppose” language. This is an important counterexample to treating every form of the lemma as technical self-reification.
- **Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya, Sn 842:** `samo visesī uda vā nihīno / yo maññati so vivadetha tena`. This directly links conceiving/regarding oneself equal, superior, or inferior with dispute.
- **Snp 4.10 Purābheda, Sn 855:** `na loke maññate samaṃ / na visesī na nīceyyo`; `maññate` is present middle 3sg and again occurs in the equal/superior/inferior comparison field.
- **Snp 4.13 Mahāvyūha, Sn 911–912:** `... uggahaṇanti maññe` occurs twice. `maññe` is morphologically available in more than one slot of the paradigm; context and translations commonly take it idiomatically as first-person “I think/suppose”. It should not be silently forced into the technical “self-conceiving” sense.
- **Snp 4.14 Tuvaṭaka, Sn 918:** `seyyo na tena maññeyya / nīceyyo atha vāpi sarikkho ... nātumānaṃ vikappayaṃ tiṭṭhe`. `maññeyya` is optative 3sg; the verse strongly coordinates comparison with construction/imagining of self, but the exact semantic contribution of `vikappayaṃ` is separate and should not be collapsed into the verb `maññeyya`.
- **Snp 4.14 Tuvaṭaka, Sn 931:** `... nāññaṃ atimaññe`. This is a prefixed `ati-maññati` family form in a context of not looking down on / despising another on account of life, wisdom, or observance. Prefixation changes the contextual sense; it is not a bare synonym of every `maññati` occurrence.

### Morphology traps explicitly excluded
- Snp 4.13 `saññamena` in `sīluttamā saññamenāhu suddhiṃ` is from `saññama/saṃyama` “restraint”, not the noun `saññā` “perception/notion”.
- Snp 4.7 `samaññāto` is from the designation/name family `samaññā/samaññāta`, not an occurrence of bare `saññā`.
- `saññī`, `asaññī`, `visaññasaññī`, and `vibhūtasaññī` are derived/compound forms and must not be counted as if they were all bare-noun tokens.
- `maññanā` is a nominal derivative often used in wider early-text analysis; a finite `maññati` occurrence does not automatically attest the noun `maññanā` in that Aṭṭhakavagga verse.
- `māna`, `vidhā`, `vikappa`, and `maññati` can cluster in the same semantic field but are not lexical synonyms.

LEXICAL_FINDINGS:
1. **`saññā` is context-sensitive.** “Perception”, “recognition”, “notion”, “conception”, and sometimes “idea” can each capture part of its range; no one English/Vietnamese gloss should be promoted to the absolute meaning across Aṭṭhakavagga.
2. **Sn 802 is specifically modified saññā.** `pakappitā saññā` supports a constructive/fashioned cognitive reading in that verse. It does not safely establish “an awakened person has no perception at all”.
3. **Sn 847 distinguishes saññā from the act of grasping it.** The verse's problem is not lexically identical to the mere existence of saññā: it contrasts `saññāviratta` and grasping (`aggahesuṃ`) saññā/view.
4. **Sn 874 gives a direct source relation but not a complete psychology.** `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā` is direct textual evidence of a relation between saññā and `papañcasaṅkhā`. This task does not infer a full definition of papañca, nor does it insert `maññati` into that line.
5. **Sn 892 places saññā in an epistemic/disputational truth context.** But the grammar is sufficiently compressed that competing translations exist; it is not safe to claim the verse simply says “truth is nothing but subjective perception”.
6. **`maññati` has both ordinary and marked uses.** Sn 840/911–912 show “think/suppose” type uses, while Sn 806, 842, 855 and 918 show strongly marked appropriation or self-comparison contexts.
7. **Possessive conceiving is directly attested.** Sn 806's `mam idaṃ` (“this is mine”) is unusually clear evidence that `maññati` can participate in mine-making/appropriation.
8. **Self-ranking is directly attested.** Sn 799, 842, 855 and 918 place forms of `maññati` inside equal/superior/inferior comparison structures. Sn 842 further links that self-positioning directly to dispute.
9. **Identity is broader than the lemma.** In Sn 918, self-language and `vikappayaṃ` are adjacent to `maññeyya`, so a self-construction reading is locally strong. But the modern analytical noun “identity” is not the lexical meaning of `maññati` in every occurrence.
10. **The Aṭṭhakavagga does not present one formula “saññā → maññati → papañca → identity → dispute”.** It supplies important pieces of that wider analytical model in different verses. Treating the whole chain as one direct Aṭṭhakavagga teaching would conflate separate passages and terms.

TEXT_ATTESTED:
- Sn 802: `pakappitā saññā` occurs directly; the modifier “fashioned/constructed” is textually present.
- Sn 841 and 847: saññā appears in the Māgaṇḍiya exchange; Sn 847 directly connects grasped saññā + view with clashing in the world.
- Sn 874: the verse directly states `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`; only that relation is attested here.
- Sn 892: `saññāya` occurs inside the discussion of many truth-claims; exact syntactic/translation force remains partly open.
- Sn 806: a person `maññati` “this is mine”.
- Sn 842: one who `maññati` oneself equal/superior/inferior would dispute on that basis.
- Sn 799, 855, 918: further direct self-comparison uses of the `maññati` family.
- Sn 840 and Sn 911–912 demonstrate that forms of the same verbal family can also function as ordinary “think/suppose” language rather than a technical identity term.
- Sn 931 shows prefixed `atimaññe` in a “do not look down on another” context; prefix and construction matter.

CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
- The project-level model that combines **MN 18 perception → thought/proliferative process**, **MN 1 conceiving/appropriation**, **SN 22.89 ‘I am’**, and Aṭṭhakavagga comparison/dispute into an “identity-story decomposition” is a legitimate analytic synthesis when clearly labelled, but it is not a single Aṭṭhakavagga lexical formula.
- A126 remains defensible only if its “identity story decomposition” component stays CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS. Its cited early texts attest perception, conceiving, and “I am” material separately; the integrated modern model is synthesis.
- A144 is correctly guarded when Aṭṭhakavagga is called a **stress-test/supporting conflict context**, not a direct lexical definition of papañca. This audit does not promote Aṭṭhakavagga dispute verses into a papañca definition.
- “`maññati` = MN 1 technical `maññanā` in every Aṭṭhakavagga occurrence” is a cross-text import, not direct passage-level attestation.
- “`saññā` = autobiographical memory” or “`saññā` = identity-story categorization” is not direct Aṭṭhakavagga attestation. The earlier project guardrail `saññā != autobiographical memory one-to-one` is supported by this audit.

OVERCLAIMS_FOUND:
1. **OVERCLAIM:** “`saññā` means memory.” It has a wider perceptual/recognitional/notional range; autobiographical memory is not a one-to-one lexical definition.
2. **OVERCLAIM:** “`saññā` always means concept/conceptual construction.” Sn 802 strongly supports a constructed-notion reading because of `pakappitā`, but Sn 874's percipient/unpercipient compound field preserves a perceptual-awareness dimension.
3. **OVERCLAIM:** “Sn 802 says the sage has no perception whatsoever.” The grammar says no even-minimal **`pakappitā` saññā** in the cited domain; dropping the modifier changes the claim.
4. **OVERCLAIM:** “Sn 847 says ‘notion I am’ in the Pāli.” The root verse has `saññā`, not an explicit compound `asmisaññā`; translations inserting “I am” are interpretive.
5. **OVERCLAIM:** “Sn 874 proves perception causes all identity, dispute, or every phenomenon called papañca.” The verse directly gives a saññā–`papañcasaṅkhā` nidāna relation; broader definitions require separate evidence.
6. **OVERCLAIM:** “Sn 892 proves truth is merely perception.” The compressed `aññatra saññāya niccāni loke` construction admits translation debate and should not carry that metaphysical conclusion unaided.
7. **OVERCLAIM:** “`maññati` means identity/self-reification.” Its base lexical range is think/suppose/deem/regard; selfing is strongly contextual in certain complements/constructions.
8. **OVERCLAIM:** “every `maññati` occurrence is the MN 1 technical conceiving pattern.” Sn 840 and Sn 911–912 are counterexamples to an automatic technical reading.
9. **OVERCLAIM:** “`maññati`, `māna`, and `vidhā` are synonyms.” Sn 842 itself keeps them formally distinct while putting them in one comparison field.
10. **OVERCLAIM:** “`atimaññati` is just the same lemma sense as `maññati`.” The prefix `ati-` changes the contextual force toward despising/looking down on.
11. **OVERCLAIM:** “Aṭṭhakavagga directly teaches the complete chain saññā → maññati → papañca → identity → dispute.” No single audited passage states that chain.

COUNTER_READINGS:
- **Against a purely neutral/sensory reading of saññā:** Sn 802's `pakappitā saññā`, Sn 847's grasping of saññā, and Sn 892's truth-claim context show constructive/epistemic uses that are not captured by “raw sensory perception” alone.
- **Against a purely conceptual reading of saññā:** Sn 874 explicitly works with percipient/non-percipient derived forms and modes of awareness; translating every occurrence simply as “concept” is too narrow.
- **Against “saññā itself is the problem”:** Sn 847 makes grasping/dispassion a key distinction; the text does not lexically equate saññā with grasping.
- **Against a weak generic reading of maññati as only ‘thinking’:** Sn 806 (“this is mine”), Sn 842 and Sn 918 (equal/superior/inferior and self-construction context) show that it can carry marked appropriative/self-positioning force.
- **Against total technicalization of maññati:** Sn 840 and Sn 911–912 show ordinary “I think/suppose” type usage. Context, complement, and morphology must determine whether “conceiving” is technical or ordinary.
- **Against a direct papañca-identity formula:** Sn 874 gives saññā as nidāna of `papañcasaṅkhā`; Sn 842 separately gives comparative `maññati` leading to dispute. The text does not join those into one explicit causal sentence.

UNCERTAINTIES:
- **Sn 802 `pakappitā saññā`:** “constructed perception”, “fashioned notion”, “conceived idea”, and similar translations capture different aspects. The modifier is secure; the best single target-language gloss is not.
- **Sn 841 `aṇumpi saññaṃ`:** “perception”, “notion”, and “conception” remain plausible contextual renderings; do not force the same translation used at Sn 802.
- **Sn 847 `saññāviratta`:** “dispassionate toward perception”, “detached from notions”, etc. vary. Translation as explicitly “notion ‘I am’” adds content not present in the bare compound.
- **Sn 874 compounds:** `saññasaññī`, `visaññasaññī`, `asaññī`, `vibhūtasaññī` are difficult and translation-sensitive. Mahāniddesa gives specific exegetical identifications, but those are later explanations and are not silently promoted to the root verse.
- **Sn 892 `saññāya`:** the form is case-syncretic; `aññatra` can govern more than one case. `niccāni` grammatically agrees with plural neuter `saccāni`. Readings like “perceptions of permanence” have exegetical support but are not established merely by surface morphology.
- **Sn 840:** textual traditions differ between `maññe-m-ahaṃ`-type and `maññāmahaṃ`-type readings; both support an ordinary first-person judging/thinking function, but exact form-level analysis should retain the variant.
- **Sn 911–912 `maññe`:** the form is morphologically ambiguous across the `maññati` paradigm. Context commonly yields “I think/suppose”; do not use these lines as technical self-conceiving evidence without further argument.
- **Sn 918:** `maññeyya` is clear optative; the neighboring `nātumānaṃ vikappayaṃ` strengthens a self-construction reading, but `vikappa` has its own lexical audit burden and must not be merged into `maññati`.
- **Edition numbering:** some digital editions use internal numbering shifted from PTS/Sutta Nipāta numbering. This record uses standard Snp/PTS verse numbers stated above and keeps sutta IDs as the primary locator.
- This is not a full concordance and makes no exhaustiveness claim for every derivative/compound form.
