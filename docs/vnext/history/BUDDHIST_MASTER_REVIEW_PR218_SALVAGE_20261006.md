## Salvaged durable principles

The following process ideas remain useful and are retained as non-law architecture guidance:

1. **Learning must not outrun source assurance indefinitely.** Continued conceptual learning may proceed, but material source debt must remain visible and be reduced.
2. **Use complementary work lanes, not one endless checkpoint stream.** A healthy Buddhist-learning program combines:
   - continuing understanding/synthesis;
   - exact discourse/source assurance;
   - lexical and parallel-text stress testing.
3. **Repeated themes require consolidation.** Repetition is useful only if later passes record what was added, corrected, narrowed, or superseded.
4. **Build navigational synthesis after sufficient evidence accumulates.** Large bodies of checkpoints should gain concept hubs/claim matrices or equivalent generated navigation rather than forcing users to traverse chronology.
5. **Phase maturity is evidence-based, not checkpoint-count-based.** More A-numbers alone do not prove deeper understanding.
6. **Queue state belongs only in the canonical task registry.** Historical plans must not become a second live scheduler.
