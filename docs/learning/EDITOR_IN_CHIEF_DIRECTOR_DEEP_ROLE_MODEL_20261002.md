# MINH TRÍ — DEEP ROLE MODEL: TỔNG BIÊN TẬP + ĐẠO DIỄN

- Date: 2026-10-02
- Focus: YouTube / animation / music-video / audiovisual content
- Status: DEEP_ROLE_MODEL_LEARNED / HANDS_ON_PRODUCTION_NOT_YET_VERIFIED
- Parent learning: docs/learning/EDITOR_DIRECTOR_AUTO_LEARNING_20261002.md
- Important scope note: "Tổng biên tập" here is an internal creative/editorial authority role for MINH TRÍ content production. It is not a claim about statutory press titles or legal responsibilities in any jurisdiction.
- Learning principle: know the decision system, not only the job-title description.

---

# 1. Core distinction

## Tổng biên tập
Owns the question:

**"Should this content exist in this form, for this audience, under this promise and standard?"**

Primary domain:
- editorial intent
- audience promise
- factual/rights/ethics standards
- content selection
- story priority
- tone/identity
- final editorial acceptance

## Đạo diễn
Owns the question:

**"How should the audience see, hear, understand and feel that approved intent moment by moment?"**

Primary domain:
- dramatic interpretation
- blocking
- performance
- composition
- camera
- movement
- lighting intent
- sound intent
- pacing on set / in animation
- integration of creative departments

The DGA's model is useful: the director contributes to all creative elements and helps integrate them into one dramatic and aesthetic whole.

---

# 2. The deep mental model

The two roles operate on different layers of the same piece.

## Layer 1 — Why
Owned primarily by Tổng biên tập.

Questions:
- Why are we making this?
- Why would this audience care?
- What is the single promise?
- What must be true when the viewer finishes?
- What must not be misleading?

## Layer 2 — What
Owned primarily by Tổng biên tập with creative consultation.

Questions:
- Which story?
- Which angle?
- Which characters?
- Which facts?
- Which beats?
- Which scenes survive?
- What gets cut?

## Layer 3 — How
Owned primarily by Đạo diễn.

Questions:
- How is the beat staged?
- What do we see first?
- What is off-screen?
- Which lens/shot scale?
- What movement?
- What performance?
- What sound?
- What visual rhythm?

## Layer 4 — When
Shared with editor/post.

Questions:
- When does information arrive?
- How long is silence held?
- When does music enter?
- When do we reveal?
- When do we cut?

## Layer 5 — Evidence
Owned again by Tổng biên tập after release.

Questions:
- Did audience behavior match the intended promise?
- Where did viewers leave?
- What was rewatched?
- Did the opening fulfill the package?
- Which hypothesis should change next time?

This creates a loop:

**Why -> What -> How -> When -> Evidence -> Revised Why/What**

# 3. Tổng biên tập — deep responsibilities

## 3.1 Own the audience contract

Before production, define:
- core audience
- secondary audience
- viewer need/desire
- title/thumbnail promise
- opening promise
- payoff promise

Rule:
A piece fails editorially if the package promises one thing and the content delivers another, even if the cinematography is excellent.

For YouTube, official guidance reinforces this: title/thumbnail communicate value and expectations; the opening should quickly deliver on that promise.

---

## 3.2 Decide what matters

The editor-in-chief must rank:
1. essential
2. useful
3. decorative
4. distracting

A scene can be:
- beautiful
- expensive
- technically impressive
- personally loved

and still be cut if it weakens the promise.

Editorial courage includes killing good material that belongs to a different piece.

---

## 3.3 Protect factual integrity

For factual content:
- verify before publishing
- prefer original sources
- distinguish confirmed fact from interpretation
- preserve context
- correct errors
- avoid misleading promotion

Professional editorial standards from AP/SPJ support this general model: verification, context, accountability, and human editorial judgment remain core even when AI assists.

For fictional/music/animation content:
the equivalent is internal truth:
- character motivation must be coherent
- world rules must be consistent
- emotional claims should not contradict earlier setup without purpose

---

## 3.4 Protect rights and provenance

Before approval, ask:
- Who owns the music?
- Who owns the visual?
- Is the sample licensed?
- Is AI-generated material allowed under the relevant tool/service terms?
- Is a person represented deceptively?
- Are credits/disclosures needed?

"Looks good" is never enough for editorial approval.

---

## 3.5 Own structure

The total editor decides whether the macro structure is correct.

Typical structure questions:
- Is the hook the right hook?
- Is setup too long?
- Is the reveal too late?
- Is the middle repeating itself?
- Does the ending actually pay off the beginning?

For short content:
every beat must justify its duration.

---

## 3.6 Own tone and identity

A channel becomes recognizable because editorial choices repeat consistently:
- emotional temperature
- humor level
- visual seriousness
- narration style
- music philosophy
- pacing range
- acceptable ambiguity
- taboo shortcuts

The total editor protects this identity across individual directors/projects.

---

## 3.7 Own the editorial gate

Possible decisions:
- APPROVE
- APPROVE WITH CHANGES
- HOLD
- REWRITE
- RE-SHOOT / RE-ANIMATE
- KILL

The gate must produce reasons, not taste statements.

Bad:
"I don't like it."

Good:
"The opening spends 16 seconds on context before delivering the title promise, so it violates the approved audience contract."

# 4. Đạo diễn — deep responsibilities

## 4.1 Turn abstract intent into observable behavior

Editorial intent:
"She is waiting for a message that will never come."

Director must translate this into:
- where she sits
- how often she checks the phone
- what her body does
- what remains silent
- how close the camera gets
- when the phone enters frame
- whether the room feels empty or crowded
- what sound carries time

Direction is translation from concept to experience.

---

## 4.2 Own performance

For actors:
- objective
- obstacle
- action
- subtext
- physical behavior
- timing

For animation:
the same logic becomes:
- pose
- facial micro-expression
- timing
- anticipation
- follow-through
- eye direction
- spacing

For AI video:
director must explicitly control:
- character identity
- action continuity
- emotional continuity
- camera intention
- movement direction

---

## 4.3 Own blocking

Blocking is not "where actors stand."

It controls:
- power
- distance
- tension
- reveal
- eyeline
- camera opportunity
- spatial clarity

Example:
two characters standing side by side communicates differently from one behind the other, even with identical dialogue.

---

## 4.4 Own composition

Every shot should answer:
- what is dominant?
- what is hidden?
- what is the visual hierarchy?
- where will the eye land?
- what is the relationship between subject and environment?

Composition is meaning.

---

## 4.5 Own shot scale as emotional distance

General logic:
- extreme wide: world / isolation / scale
- wide: body / geography
- medium: interaction
- close-up: emotional access
- extreme close-up: detail / pressure / subjectivity

These are not rigid rules.

Director selects shot scale based on desired audience relationship.

---

## 4.6 Own camera movement intention

Movement should answer "why now?"

Possible functions:
- reveal
- pursuit
- instability
- intimacy
- transition of power
- discovery

Do not move camera because movement looks expensive or cinematic.

---

## 4.7 Own visual continuity

Track:
- screen direction
- eyeline
- geography
- action matching
- prop position
- costume
- lighting direction
- time of day
- emotional state

Breaking continuity is allowed when intentional.

Unintentional continuity error creates cognitive noise.

---

## 4.8 Own sound intention

Director should not treat sound as post-production decoration.

For every scene:
- what is foreground sound?
- what is absent?
- is music subjective or objective?
- does silence create pressure?
- can sound bridge a cut?
- does an off-screen sound reveal information?

Image tells where to look.
Sound can tell what to fear, expect or remember.

---

## 4.9 Own coverage strategy

Coverage must serve the edit.

Before production:
- master?
- two-shot?
- singles?
- inserts?
- reaction?
- cutaway?
- transition?

Too little coverage:
editor cannot solve problems.

Too much coverage:
time is wasted and intention becomes weak.

The goal is **necessary expressive coverage**, not maximum footage.

---

# 5. Decision-rights matrix

## Total editor has final authority on:
- audience
- promise
- factual acceptance
- rights/ethics gate
- story inclusion/exclusion
- channel identity
- publish/hold decision

## Director has final authority within approved brief on:
- performance interpretation
- blocking
- camera grammar
- shot design
- staging
- visual/sound execution
- creative integration during production

## Shared:
- beat structure refinement
- pacing
- scene order when creative discoveries occur
- final cut discussions
- reshoot priorities

---

# 6. Conflict protocol

Conflict is normal.

Do not resolve by:
- ego
- seniority alone
- "cinematic taste"
- "the algorithm wants it"

Resolve in this order:

1. What is the approved audience promise?
2. What is the intended emotional effect?
3. What evidence supports each option?
4. Which option preserves story clarity?
5. What constraints are real?
6. Can both versions be tested?
7. Who owns the final decision on this layer?

Example:

Director wants a 12-second silent wide shot.
Total editor wants 4 seconds.

Test:
- What emotional function requires 12?
- Does tension build or does attention collapse?
- Can performance/audio justify duration?
- Is there evidence from cold-view or retention?

Decision should be tied to function, not taste.

# 9. Deep failure modes — Tổng biên tập

## 9.1 Chasing metrics without meaning
Problem:
optimizes CTR/retention by sensationalism.

Fix:
metrics diagnose delivery; they do not replace identity or truth.

## 9.2 Vague feedback
Problem:
"make it stronger."

Fix:
name function, location, evidence, desired change.

## 9.3 Over-editing
Problem:
destroys director's coherent vision through endless notes.

Fix:
separate:
- mandatory standard failure
- strategic concern
- optional creative taste

## 9.4 Protecting original idea against evidence
Problem:
data and cold viewers show confusion but editor refuses change.

Fix:
protect purpose, not the first draft.

## 9.5 Inconsistent standards
Problem:
different rules on every episode.

Fix:
maintain editorial charter.

---

# 10. Deep failure modes — Đạo diễn

## 10.1 Style before meaning
Beautiful shots with no dramatic function.

## 10.2 Directing adjectives
"Be sadder."
"More intense."

Better:
give playable action or objective.

## 10.3 Coverage without point of view
Shoots everything from every angle.

Result:
editor has footage but no film.

## 10.4 Ignoring sound
Creates visually polished but emotionally thin scenes.

## 10.5 Continuity obsession
Protects geography but kills the best emotional take.

## 10.6 Random camera movement
Motion becomes decoration.

# 11. YouTube-specific division of labor

## Total editor
Owns:
- concept selection
- title/thumbnail promise
- content angle
- first-30-second promise
- macro structure
- release decision
- retention postmortem

Official YouTube guidance frames performance around:
- appeal
- engagement
- satisfaction

It also emphasizes matching title/thumbnail expectations and delivering value quickly.

## Director
Owns:
- hook execution
- performance
- visual surprise
- shot rhythm source material
- emotional escalation
- transitions
- sound design intent

Director gives the editor material capable of fulfilling the editorial promise.

# 12. Retention as a shared diagnostic

## Intro drop
Total editor asks:
- wrong promise?
- too much setup?
- mismatch with title/thumbnail?

Director asks:
- weak first image?
- flat performance?
- unclear staging?
- no visual change?

## Dip
Total editor:
- redundant beat?
- irrelevant information?

Director:
- boring coverage?
- slow performance?
- weak visual focus?

## Spike
Total editor:
- strong value?
- confusing information?

Director:
- strong visual/emotional moment?
- detail required rewatch?

Do not treat retention as one-role data.

# 13. Music-video / song visualization application

## Total editor owns:
- what the song means for this version
- whose perspective
- literal vs metaphorical interpretation
- recurring motifs
- emotional arc
- what visuals are prohibited because they contradict song identity

## Director owns:
- performance style
- visual motif execution
- camera rhythm
- choreography
- lighting
- locations
- transitions
- sync/anti-sync between music and image

Example:
Song says loneliness.

Total editor:
"Loneliness is not abandonment; it is being unseen while surrounded."

Director:
stages crowd moving around a still protagonist, wide lens, locked camera, selective focus, muffled ambience.

# 14. Animation / AI-video application

Total editor protects:
- story logic
- character identity at conceptual level
- tone
- message
- audience promise

Director protects:
- visual identity frame to frame
- staging
- lens language
- motion
- timing
- expression
- continuity

AI-specific rule:
A generated shot that is individually impressive can still be rejected if it breaks character, world or emotional continuity.

# 15. Cold-view protocol

Both roles should watch the near-final piece without stopping.

Pass 1 — Total editor:
write only:
- promise mismatch
- confusion
- repetition
- missing context
- weak payoff

Pass 2 — Director:
write only:
- performance
- visual focus
- staging
- continuity
- sound
- rhythm

Then compare notes.

Do not fix during viewing.

# 16. Levels of mastery

## L1
Can define the two roles.

## L2
Can identify who owns a decision.

## L3
Can resolve conflicts using promise, intention and evidence.

## L4
Can produce a complete brief + directing package + edit notes.

## L5
Can run repeated real productions and improve outcomes from audience evidence without losing creative identity.

# 17. Practical examination required

Use one 45–60 second short.

## Total editor examination
Must produce:
- audience
- promise
- one-line story
- beat selection
- cut criteria
- rights/fact check
- packaging concept
- final acceptance notes

## Director examination
Must produce:
- beat intention
- blocking
- 8–12 shot list
- eye path
- sound design
- continuity map
- performance notes

## Conflict examination
Introduce three forced conflicts:
1. beautiful but unnecessary shot
2. strongest emotional take with continuity imperfection
3. retention feedback contradicting original structure

Pass only if decisions are justified by function and evidence.

# 19. Final working doctrine

Tổng biên tập protects:
**meaning + truth + promise + identity + acceptance**

Đạo diễn protects:
**experience + performance + image + sound + integration**

Neither role should micromanage the other's domain without a reason tied to the audience contract.

The strongest collaboration is:

**Tổng biên tập says what must be true.  
Đạo diễn makes the audience feel that truth.  
Dựng determines exactly when they feel it.  
Audience evidence tells us whether it worked.**
