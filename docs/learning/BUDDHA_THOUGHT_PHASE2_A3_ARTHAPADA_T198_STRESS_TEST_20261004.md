## Research question

Which parts of the Aṭṭhakavagga view/conflict architecture are independently preserved outside the Pāli recension, especially in T198 佛說義足經 (Arthapada/Yi Zu Jing), and where must lexical or verse-level equivalence remain open?

## 1. T198 corpus status

T198 佛說義足經, translated by Zhi Qian, is an old Chinese Arthapada recension corresponding to the Pāli Aṭṭhakavagga.

Secure structural facts:
- it contains sixteen discourse units corresponding to the sixteen-discourse architecture of Aṭṭhakavagga;
- the order is not identical throughout: the Chinese order diverges especially in the later portion;
- the text contains older verse material plus prose framing/expansion.

Therefore T198 is a strong **transmission-independent structural witness**, but not a mechanically identical copy of the Pāli text.

## 2. Māgandiya is securely cross-attested

The Chinese T198 contains a **摩因提女經** unit corresponding to the Māgandiya material.

Independent Chinese and Pāli traditions preserve the broad architecture:
- Māgandiya questions the Buddha about the view/practice on which he takes his stand;
- the answer resists being identified with a grasped doctrinal position;
- purification is not secured by simply possessing a view/learning/practice;
- peace is associated with non-grasping and non-contention.

This is reinforced by SĀ 551, which explicitly quotes and explains a verse from the Yipin/Arthapada Māgandiya section and analyzes non-contention through abandoning craving, clinging, views and related attachment toward the aggregates.

**Status:** CROSS-TRADITION STRONG at structural level.

## 3. SĀ 551 is especially important

SĀ 551 preserves a tradition in which Mahākaccāna interprets an Arthapada/Māgandiya verse.

The explanation links:
- attachment to form/feeling/perception/formations/consciousness,
- desire/lust/delight/craving,
- clinging,
- view/standpoint,
- latent tendencies,
with continued conflict.

It also explains “not contending with the world” by contrasting aggressive doctrinal challenge with non-participation in that status-combat pattern.

This gives independent support to the A2 synthesis:
the anti-dispute material is not merely anti-cognition; it is anti-attachment + anti-status-combat.

## 4. Pasūra / Cūḷaviyūha / Mahāviyūha / Tuvaṭaka: corpus-level support is stronger than exact verse-level support in this pass

The T198 corpus as a whole confirms that an Arthapada collection with sixteen units circulated outside the Pāli transmission.

However, in this pass:
- exact T198 chapter correspondence for every Pāli unit Sn 4.8, 4.12, 4.13 and 4.14 was not independently established line by line;
- exact Chinese equivalents for Pāli technical expressions such as “truth is one,” papañca-saṅkhā, mantā asmīti, and every “only this is true” formulation remain unverified.

Therefore:
- **CORPUS PARALLEL:** strong;
- **THEMATIC PARALLEL:** probable-to-strong depending on unit;
- **LEXICAL IDENTITY:** not established.

This is an important methodological boundary.

## 5. Why lexical non-equivalence matters

Old Chinese translations:
- can compress Indic terms,
- expand through prose framing,
- choose Chinese conceptual vocabulary that is not one-to-one with Pāli,
- reflect a different source dialect/recension.

Therefore a Chinese phrase that resembles:
- view,
- desire,
- attachment,
- truth,
- dispute

must not automatically be assigned a specific Pāli lexeme such as diṭṭhi, taṇhā, upādāna, sacca or papañca without philological control.

Structural agreement is still valuable:
if two transmission lines independently preserve the same **problem architecture**, that is stronger evidence than repeated copies inside one Pāli textual family.

## 6. Cross-tradition architecture that survives the stress test

The following pattern is now comparatively robust:

1. Doctrinal positions can become objects of attachment.
2. Attachment is linked with self-positioning and conflict.
3. Purity/liberation is not achieved merely by possessing a formulation.
4. Desire, attachment and view-related fixation are repeatedly tied to contention.
5. The liberated sage is represented as non-contentious without being cognitively blank.
6. Early Buddhist discourse can distinguish functional teaching from attachment to teaching.

This pattern survives:
- Pāli Aṭṭhakavagga,
- T198 Arthapada at corpus/selected-unit level,
- SĀ 551’s independent explanatory tradition.

## 7. What T198 does NOT yet prove for this project

Do not claim:
- that T198 preserves every Pāli verse;
- that its chapter order is identical;
- that every Chinese view-word equals diṭṭhi;
- that every Chinese conflict-word equals vivāda;
- that it has a direct lexical equivalent of papañca in every relevant location;
- that “truth is one” has already been independently confirmed in exact Chinese wording;
- that later prose frames are necessarily as old as the verse cores.

## 8. Implication for historical confidence

The existence of:
- Pāli Aṭṭhakavagga,
- Chinese T198 Arthapada,
- quotations/exegesis such as SĀ 551,
- and fragmentary Indic evidence,

makes it increasingly difficult to dismiss the anti-grasping / anti-contention architecture as a late Theravāda invention.

Safe claim:
**the broad architecture is early and cross-traditionally attested.**

Unsafe claim:
**every philosophical nuance in the extant Pāli wording is therefore original to the historical Buddha.**

## 9. Synthesis update

The previous A2 distinction now becomes stronger:

**right/useful cognition**
can guide practice,

while

**appropriated cognition**
can become:
- “my doctrine,”
- “my status,”
- “my superiority,”
- “their error,”
- conflict.

Cross-tradition evidence supports the **relation-to-view** distinction more strongly than a blanket “view/no-view” dichotomy.

## 10. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Pāli alone proves the whole model.” — independent Chinese/Āgama evidence exists and must be used.
2. “Chinese and Pāli are word-for-word identical.” — false.
3. “Different wording means different doctrine.” — not necessarily; structural continuity may survive lexical divergence.
4. “Parallel count equals independent doctrinal votes.” — independence is partial and must be evaluated by transmission history.
5. “T198 automatically proves historical-Buddha wording.” — it strengthens antiquity, not verbatim attribution.
6. “Anti-dispute rhetoric means no rational disagreement.” — SĀ/Māgandiya structure targets attachment/status contention, not all analysis.

## 11. CONFIDENCE

**CROSS-TRADITION STRONG**
- Aṭṭhakavagga/Arthapada is preserved in more than one transmission.
- the collection architecture of sixteen units is independently attested.
- Māgandiya-style non-grasping/non-contention material is independently preserved.
- SĀ 551 confirms an early tradition interpreting an Arthapada Māgandiya verse through attachment, craving, views and contention.

**PROBABLE**
- the broader view/truth/dispute critique in Sn 4.8–4.14 reflects an early shared thematic complex rather than a late sectarian invention.

**OPEN**
- exact T198 verse correspondences for Sn 4.8, 4.12, 4.13, 4.14.
- exact Chinese lexical counterparts of papañca, pacceka-sacca and several key Pāli terms.
- relative age of prose framing versus verse cores in each unit.

## 12. PRACTICAL MEANING

Cross-tradition comparison changes how confidence is assigned:
- one vivid Pāli sentence is not enough;
- repeated Pāli copies are not automatically many independent witnesses;
- structural preservation across independent recensions carries more weight.

This directly supports the project’s core learning method:
**text → parallel → counterevidence → synthesis, with lexical humility.**
