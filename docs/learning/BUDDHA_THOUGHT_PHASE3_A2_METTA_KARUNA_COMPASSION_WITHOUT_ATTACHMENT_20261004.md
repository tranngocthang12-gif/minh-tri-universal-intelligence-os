# BUDDHA THOUGHT STUDY — PHASE 3 A2: METTĀ / KARUṆĀ / COMPASSION WITHOUT ATTACHMENT

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Status:** WORKING RESEARCH LOG / NOT VERIFIED

## Core finding

Early Buddhist mettā is not possessive affection. Sn 1.8 universalizes goodwill beyond near/far, seen/unseen and friend/non-friend. MN 21 tests goodwill under provocation: one may still distinguish harmful from harmless while refusing inner hate.

Karuṇā appears as a distinct brahmavihāra, likewise cultivated as immeasurable release of mind. The texts do not require compassion to collapse into emotional suffering with the sufferer.

## Guardrails

- mettā ≠ liking everyone;
- mettā ≠ approving harmful conduct;
- karuṇā ≠ hatred toward the person causing harm;
- boundaries/restraint do not by themselves contradict goodwill;
- brahmavihāra attainment ≠ automatically Nibbāna.

AN 4.125 shows that an ordinary person can cultivate brahmavihāras and attain exalted rebirth while still remaining within saṃsāra. AN 4.126 shows that when the same cultivated field is seen in terms of impermanence, suffering, emptiness and not-self, its liberative role changes.

## Working synthesis

Mettā weakens hostility without requiring possession.
Karuṇā responds to suffering without requiring hatred.
Both can be cultivated before final insight and can support concentration and the broader path.
They are best understood inside the whole path, not as a self-sufficient substitute for right view and wisdom.
