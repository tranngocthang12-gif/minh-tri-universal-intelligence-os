## Research question

What is the minimum defensible architecture of early Buddhist ethics and social thought, and how does it connect to liberation without turning Buddhism into a modern political ideology?

## 1. Ethical roots are the same roots that bind saṃsāra

Across MN 19, MN 61, AN 3.71 and the path texts:
- greed,
- hatred,
- delusion
produce harmful thought, speech and action;
- they also obscure discernment and obstruct liberation.

Therefore Buddhist ethics is not an external social code added onto meditation.

The same roots that create social harm also sustain bondage.

## 2. Intention is central, but consequence also matters

AN 6.63 places cetanā at the center of kamma.

MN 61 then requires reflection:
- before action,
- during action,
- after action,
asking whether the action harms self, others or both.

Therefore:
- intention alone is not enough;
- outcome alone is not enough.

The ethical field includes:
**intention + action + consequence + correction.**

## 3. Non-harm is both moral and epistemic

MN 19 and AN 3.71 link harmfulness with:
- affliction,
- confusion,
- obstruction of wisdom.

Thus non-harm is not merely “being nice.”
It is part of the purification of cognition.

A mind shaped by hostility sees and acts badly.
A mind trained in non-ill-will becomes more capable of clear discernment.

## 4. Brahmavihāras transform relation to others without possession

Phase 3 A2–A3 established a balanced field:

- mettā: non-hostile goodwill beyond preference;
- karuṇā: compassion toward suffering;
- muditā: non-jealous joy in others' good;
- upekkhā: steadiness without indifference or compulsive control.

These qualities undermine:
- hostility,
- envy,
- possessiveness,
- status comparison,
- reactive attachment to outcomes.

Thus the brahmavihāras are ethically positive forms of the same de-selfing movement examined earlier through anattā, māna and mamatta.

## 5. Right speech converts inner training into social conduct

Right speech requires:
- truth,
- benefit,
- timing,
- gentleness,
- goodwill.

The central lesson from MN 58 / AN 5.198:
**truth is necessary but not sufficient.**

Speech can be factually correct and still be unskillful if used:
- to humiliate,
- divide,
- retaliate,
- display superiority.

Thus Buddhist communication ethics targets not only content but motive and effect.

## 6. Caste critique shifts value from birth to conduct

Sn 3.9 / MN 98, MN 93 and DN 27 reject hereditary spiritual superiority.

The decisive shift is:
from:
- lineage,
- birth,
- inherited purity,

to:
- action,
- virtue,
- restraint,
- wisdom,
- liberation.

This is not a full modern political equality doctrine.

But it is a strong spiritual and moral equalization:
**no birth-group has a monopoly on purity or release.**

## 7. Social identity and māna

Caste/status claims such as:
- “we are superior,”
- “others are inferior,”
parallel the earlier analysis of māna as:
- superior,
- equal,
- inferior self-positioning.

Project synthesis:
**social hierarchy can become collective selfing.**

This does not mean all functional social distinction is forbidden.
It means inherited ranking cannot establish intrinsic spiritual worth.

## 8. Wealth is evaluated by acquisition, use and attachment

AN 8.54 and AN 4.61 show that household prosperity is not condemned in itself.

The ethical questions are:
- how was wealth acquired?
- is livelihood harmful?
- is expenditure balanced?
- are family, workers, friends and guests supported?
- is there generosity?
- does wealth become greed, heedlessness or identity?

Therefore:
**ownership is conventional; clinging and harmful use are the deeper problem.**

## 9. Right livelihood extends non-harm into economic life

AN 5.177 restricts trades tied to:
- weapons,
- living beings,
- meat,
- intoxicants,
- poison.

This establishes:
**economic success does not override ethical consequence.**

Livelihood belongs to the path because earning itself can become a field of harm or restraint.

## 10. Social relationships are reciprocal, not one-way

DN 31 treats:
- parents/children,
- teachers/students,
- spouses,
- friends,
- employers/workers,
- laypeople/renunciants
as reciprocal relations.

The structural principle is:
**role and power create duties.**

This is especially important where one side is stronger:
- parent,
- teacher,
- employer,
- ruler.

The stronger side is not morally exempt.

## 11. Social disorder has conditions

DN 5 and DN 26 show:
- poverty,
- lack of livelihood,
- bad incentives,
- violent punishment
can contribute to theft, violence and disorder.

This does not erase individual responsibility.

It adds a second layer:
**social arrangements also condition conduct.**

The Buddha's causal method therefore extends from mind to society:
do not only punish the result; examine the conditions feeding it.

## 12. Power is subordinate to Dhamma

DN 26's ruler:
- takes Dhamma as authority,
- protects broadly,
- provides for the poor,
- seeks ethical counsel,
- avoids unskillful policies.

Thus:
**power is not self-legitimating.**

A ruler may be strong yet ethically wrong.
Dhamma remains the higher standard.

## 13. Political order is not the final goal

SN 4.20 and the wider renunciant framework keep a distinction:

- righteous social order is valuable;
- liberation goes beyond possession, power and worldly status.

Therefore Buddhism does not collapse into political theory.

A just ruler is not the same as an arahant.

## 14. The minimum architecture of early Buddhist ethics

A cautious integrated model:

greed / hatred / delusion
→ distorted intention
→ harmful speech/action
→ harm to self / others / both
→ agitation / conflict / obscured discernment
→ continued bondage.

Training:
- non-greed,
- non-hatred,
- non-delusion,
- non-harm,
- truthfulness,
- generosity,
- right livelihood,
- mettā/karuṇā/muditā/upekkhā,
- reciprocal responsibility,
- socially skillful conditions
→ less harm,
→ less conflict/remorse,
→ greater clarity,
→ stronger concentration and wisdom,
→ movement toward release.

This is a SYNTHESIS, not a single canonical chain.

## 15. What the Buddha's social thought does NOT require

The early texts do not require labeling the Buddha as:
- socialist,
- capitalist,
- liberal,
- conservative,
- democrat,
- anarchist.

These categories are historically foreign to the corpus.

What can be defended is narrower and stronger:
- power is ethically constrained;
- poverty matters causally;
- livelihood matters morally;
- inherited status does not determine spiritual worth;
- social relations involve reciprocal duties;
- non-harm and truth apply beyond private meditation.

## 16. Ethics and liberation are one architecture

The deepest Phase-3 result is:

The Buddha does not have:
- one doctrine for “morality,”
- another unrelated doctrine for “meditation,”
- another for “liberation.”

The same defilements:
- generate harmful social behavior,
- distort cognition,
- disturb concentration,
- sustain craving and rebirth.

Therefore ethical training is already liberation-work.

## 17. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Buddhist ethics is only rule-following.”
2. “Only intention matters.”
3. “Only consequences matter.”
4. “Compassion means accepting harmful behavior.”
5. “Equanimity means indifference.”
6. “Birth determines spiritual worth.”
7. “Wealth itself is evil.”
8. “Crime is only individual failure.”
9. “Poverty removes personal responsibility.”
10. “The Buddha taught a modern political ideology.”
11. “Good government is equivalent to Nibbāna.”

## 18. CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- ethics is rooted in greed/hate/delusion and linked to wisdom/liberation.
- intention and harm to self/others/both are central.
- non-harm, right speech, right action and right livelihood are path factors.
- brahmavihāras train universal non-hostility/compassionate relation.
- spiritual worth is not fixed by birth.
- wealth/livelihood have ethical conditions.
- social and political conditions can contribute to disorder.
- rulers are morally constrained by Dhamma.

**CROSS-TRADITION STRONG AT SELECTED CORPUS LEVEL**
- DN 26 / Aggañña-related and kingship/social materials have non-Pāli witnesses.
- further exact line-by-line parallel work remains needed for several lay and ethical suttas.

**OPEN**
- exact political implications beyond ethical rulership.
- precise limits of coercion/punishment.
- historical implementation of caste equality and worker obligations.
- detailed cross-tradition history of several household and brahmavihāra passages.

## 19. Core synthesis

> Early Buddhist ethics is the social face of the same liberation process that operates inwardly. Greed, hatred and delusion create both suffering within and harm between people. The path therefore trains intention, speech, action, livelihood, generosity, compassion and responsibility while also addressing social conditions that feed disorder. Yet social reform remains subordinate to the larger aim: ending the defilements and the cycle of renewed becoming.
