## Research question

After C1–C3, what is the minimum doctrinal statement about Nibbāna that can be defended from early texts without:
- reducing it to annihilation,
- turning it into an eternal self,
- treating it as a place,
- or importing later ontology?

What does the Nibbāna material reveal about the Buddha’s general method of thought?

## 1. Start from cessation, not speculation

The most secure early-Buddhist entry is functional:

The cessation of suffering is tied to:
- fading and cessation of craving,
- relinquishing,
- releasing,
- not clinging.

SN 43 repeatedly defines the unconditioned through:
- ending greed,
- ending hatred,
- ending delusion.

Therefore the project’s first rule for Nibbāna is:

> Understand what ceases before asking what “substance” Nibbāna is.

This follows the Buddha’s broader method:
problem → cause → cessation → path.

## 2. Nibbāna has three main registers in the early texts

### A. Cessation register
Nibbāna is spoken of through:
- destruction of greed,
- destruction of hatred,
- destruction of delusion,
- ending of craving,
- ending of clinging,
- ending of renewed becoming and birth.

This is the most text-secure register.

### B. Realization register
Nibbāna is:
- sought in the noble search,
- realized/directly known,
- attained while alive,
- the culmination of the path.

This prevents reducing Nibbāna to a mere word for biological death.

### C. Unconditioned register
Nibbāna is called:
- asaṅkhata,
- amata,
- unborn,
- unbecome,
- unmade,
- unconditioned,
- “that āyatana.”

This prevents reducing it to merely another conditioned mental state.

These three registers should be held together.
They do not need to be forced into one grammatical category.

## 3. What “unconditioned” minimally means

The safest internal-Buddhist meaning is:

- conditioned phenomena arise dependent on causes and conditions;
- greed, hatred, delusion, craving and becoming belong to that conditioned domain;
- the path removes the conditions that sustain bondage;
- the goal is called unconditioned because it is not another produced link in the same arising-and-ceasing chain.

Thus:

> the conditioned path leads to realization by ending conditions; it does not need to manufacture Nibbāna as a new conditioned object.

This is strongly compatible with SN 43.

## 4. “Deathless” does not mean immortal self

Amata should first be understood through the cessation of renewed birth and death.

MN 26’s noble search sets:
- birth,
- aging,
- illness,
- death,
against:
- unborn,
- unaging,
- unailing,
- deathless Nibbāna.

The internal logic is:
**where renewed birth no longer arises, the round of aging and death no longer continues.**

This does not require an immortal personal consciousness.

## 5. “There is that āyatana” is a real assertion, but not a place-claim

Ud 8.1 says:
**atthi ... tadāyatanaṃ**.

But it simultaneously removes:
- ordinary material elements,
- this world/other world,
- sun/moon,
- coming/going/staying,
- passing away/arising.

Therefore the minimum conclusion is:

> the early text affirms a real liberating escape/end of suffering while refusing to classify it as another ordinary conditioned spatial-temporal realm.

Do not inflate āyatana into:
- a physical place,
- a cosmic substance,
- a metaphysical Self.

## 6. Negative/apophatic language has an internal purpose

Ud 8.4 removes:
- dependence,
- wavering,
- yearning,
- coming/going,
- arising/passing,
- here/there/between.

MN 140 removes:
- “I am,”
- “I am this,”
- “I will be,”
- “I will not be,”
and related forms of conceiving.

SN 44 removes:
- exists after death,
- does not exist,
- both,
- neither.

These are not random silences.

They progressively remove the very structures by which the mind:
- locates,
- identifies,
- possesses,
- projects,
- and constructs a self in a world.

Therefore a major internal function of Nibbāna language is:
**to prevent liberation from being turned back into a new object of selfing.**

This is a project synthesis grounded in the textual pattern.

## 7. Nibbāna while alive and final parinibbāna

Iti 44 distinguishes:

### With residue
An arahant is alive:
- faculties remain,
- pleasant/painful experience can continue,
- greed/hate/delusion are extinguished.

### Without residue
At the ending of the present life:
- no future remainder of conditioned becoming continues.

Thus:

**Nibbāna while alive**
= the causes of future renewed becoming are extinguished.

**Final parinibbāna**
= the remaining conditioned aggregate process reaches its end and no new birth follows.

This does not imply:
“a liberated self later gets destroyed.”

No permanent self is first established.

## 8. Why annihilationism is explicitly wrong

SN 22.85 treats the claim:
“the arahant is annihilated, perishes, and does not exist after death”
as wrong view.

The reason is not that the opposite “eternal existence” is true.

Rather:
the Tathāgata cannot be identified even here-and-now as:
- any one aggregate,
- inside or outside an aggregate,
- the sum of aggregates,
- or something wholly separate from them.

Therefore the later statement:
“that entity is annihilated”
rests on a self-like entity that the analysis never established.

## 9. Why eternal survival is also not affirmed

SN 44 and MN 72 refuse:
- exists,
- does not exist,
- both,
- neither.

The aggregate bases by which a being is reckoned are abandoned and do not arise again.

Thus early Buddhism does not replace annihilationism with:
- an eternal soul,
- an eternal personal consciousness,
- a definable being living in an eternal Nibbāna realm.

Both extremes go beyond the text.

## 10. The fire simile — fuel, cessation, and category failure

MN 72’s fire burns because of fuel.

When fuel is exhausted:
- burning ceases,
- asking “which direction did the fire go?” no longer applies.

Within the project’s dependent-arising reading:

- craving and clinging are fuel-like conditions;
- renewed becoming depends on them;
- when those conditions end, renewed becoming ends.

The simile is therefore best used to understand:
**dependent continuation and cessation**, not to prove either:
- absolute annihilation,
or
- hidden eternal survival.

## 11. “Unestablished consciousness” must remain carefully bounded

SN 22.54 and SN 22.87 use language of consciousness being:
- unestablished,
- not growing,
- liberated,
and in Vakkali’s case Māra cannot find where consciousness is established.

Safe conclusion:
the supports that establish consciousness in renewed becoming have been cut.

Unsafe conclusion:
this proves an eternal “pure consciousness” surviving parinibbāna.

That stronger ontology is not established.

## 12. The minimum defensible doctrinal statement

The project can now defend the following:

> Nibbāna is the real liberation in which greed, hatred, delusion, craving and clinging cease, so the conditions for renewed becoming and birth are ended. It is directly realized while alive and is called unconditioned/deathless because it is not another produced state within the cycle of conditioned birth and death. At final parinibbāna, the remaining conditioned aggregate process ends without renewed birth. The early texts do not describe this as either the annihilation of a permanent self or the eternal survival of a definable person.

This is a SYNTHESIS, not a single canonical quotation.

## 13. What must remain deliberately unasserted

Do **not** claim as historical-Buddha doctrine without stronger evidence:

- Nibbāna is a substance.
- Nibbāna is a place.
- Nibbāna is a cosmic Self.
- Nibbāna is eternal individual consciousness.
- Nibbāna is mere nonexistence.
- the Buddha after parinibbāna exists in a hidden fifth way.
- “unestablished consciousness” is a metaphysical Absolute.
- every use of nibbāna/parinibbāna follows one later standardized technical distinction.

The Buddha’s refusal of certain categories is itself part of the teaching and must not be “completed” by project speculation.

## 14. Milindapañha — what it clarifies well

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE
- Nibbāna has no fixed spatial location.
- A path can lead to realization without producing Nibbāna.
- after final parinibbāna the Buddha cannot be pointed to “here” or “there.”
- the extinguished-fire analogy clarifies lack of traceable location after fuel is gone.

These strongly fit SN 43, MN 72 and SN 44.

### MILINDA_EXTENSION
Milinda gives more explicit philosophical arguments about:
- Nibbāna’s reality,
- cognition of Nibbāna,
- unproduced status.

These are useful for reasoning, but should remain later clarification unless independently anchored in early texts.

## 15. What Nibbāna reveals about the Buddha’s method of thought

### A. Begin with the problem, not ontology
The Buddha repeatedly starts from:
- dukkha,
- its cause,
- its cessation,
- the path.

### B. Prefer causal explanation over hidden substances
Saṃsāra continues through conditions.
Liberation occurs when the conditions are ended.

### C. Reject false alternatives
The Buddha need not choose:
- eternal existence,
- annihilation,
when both assume a frame the analysis has dismantled.

### D. Use language pragmatically
Terms such as:
- unconditioned,
- deathless,
- peace,
- release,
- cessation,
show different aspects of one liberating goal.

### E. Refuse re-appropriation
Near liberation, the texts strip away:
- “I am,”
- “I will be,”
- here/there,
- exists/does not exist.

The teaching prevents the mind from turning release into another identity or possession.

## 16. Phase 2C conclusion

The most important result is not a speculative answer to:
“What metaphysical substance is Nibbāna?”

It is a clearer view of the Buddha’s method:

> Identify the conditions of suffering, remove them, directly realize cessation, and do not rebuild the ended structure through new concepts of self, possession, location or eternal identity.

This is the internal-Buddhist center of the Nibbāna material as far as the current evidence supports.

## 17. Confidence

**EBT_STRONG**
- cessation of greed/hate/delusion/craving is central.
- Nibbāna is a realized goal while alive.
- the unconditioned/deathless language is early-textually important.
- annihilationism is rejected.
- eternal postmortem personal survival is not affirmed.
- postmortem fourfold classification is rejected for reasons tied to aggregate-based reckoning and cessation.

**PĀLI-STRONG / PARALLEL AUDIT PENDING**
- exact antiquity of Iti 44’s two-element taxonomy.
- precise textual history of Ud 8.1–8.4.

**MILINDA_COMPATIBLE**
- non-spatiality,
- path-to-realization without production,
- extinguished-fire unpointability.

**OPEN**
- maximal ontology of asaṅkhata.
- exact semantic history of nibbāna/parinibbāna.
- interpretation of appatiṭṭhita viññāṇa beyond the minimal “support cut off” reading.
