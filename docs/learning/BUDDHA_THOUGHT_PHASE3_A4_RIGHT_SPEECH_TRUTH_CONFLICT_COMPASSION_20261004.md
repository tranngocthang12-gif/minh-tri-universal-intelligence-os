## Research question

When should truth be spoken? Is truthfulness sufficient for right speech? How do benefit, timing, gentleness and goodwill constrain speech? Can silence or delay be skillful? How does right speech interrupt papañca and social conflict?

This pass uses early Buddhist texts as the governing framework.

## 1. SN 45.8 — the minimum path definition

Right speech is defined as abstaining from:
- false speech,
- divisive speech,
- harsh speech,
- idle/frivolous chatter.

This establishes a clear negative boundary.

But other texts show that right speech is more than merely avoiding these four.

## 2. AN 5.198 — five positive criteria for well-spoken speech

AN 5.198 says well-spoken speech has five factors:
- timely,
- true,
- gentle,
- beneficial,
- spoken with a mind of mettā/goodwill.

This is a major correction to the idea:
“if it is true, I am entitled to say it however and whenever I want.”

Truth is necessary, but within this text it is not sufficient for speech to count as well-spoken.

## 3. MN 58 — truth and benefit are decisive; pleasantness is not

MN 58 analyzes speech by:
- factual/true or not,
- beneficial or not,
- pleasing or displeasing to others.

The Tathāgata:
- does not say what is untrue/unfactual;
- does not say what is true but unbeneficial;
- when something is true and beneficial, he knows the proper time to say it whether it is pleasing or displeasing.

The reason given is his sympathy/compassion for living beings.

Therefore:
**truth does not require pleasantness, but truth should be governed by benefit, timing and compassion.**

## 4. AN 5.198 and MN 58 are complementary, not contradictory

AN 5.198 says speech should be gentle.
MN 58 says a beneficial truth may still be displeasing to the listener.

These are not the same category.

A statement can be:
- gentle in manner,
yet
- unwelcome in content.

Thus:
**right speech does not require saying only what people enjoy hearing.**

It requires avoiding unnecessary harshness while still being able to speak difficult truth at the proper time.

## 5. MN 21 — how to receive speech mirrors how to give it

MN 21 analyzes speech directed at the practitioner along five dimensions:
- timely/untimely,
- true/false,
- gentle/harsh,
- beneficial/unbeneficial,
- spoken with goodwill/hate.

Even when others speak badly, the practitioner trains:
- not to let the mind be affected by hate,
- not to utter evil words,
- to remain concerned for the other person's welfare,
- to sustain goodwill.

This is important because right speech is not only control of output.
It is also training the reaction that would otherwise produce retaliatory speech.

## 6. MN 61 — speech must be examined before, during and after

Rāhula is told to examine verbal action:
- before speaking,
- while speaking,
- after speaking.

The question is whether it causes affliction/harm to:
- oneself,
- others,
- both.

If harm is foreseen, do not speak.
If harm becomes evident while speaking, stop.
If harm is discovered afterward, acknowledge it, learn, and restrain oneself in future.

Thus right speech includes:
**forecasting, monitoring, correction and truthfulness about one's own mistakes.**

## 7. Truthfulness is foundational but not exhaustive

MN 61 treats deliberate lying as especially corrosive.

Yet MN 58 and AN 5.198 prevent the reduction:
“right speech = technically accurate speech.”

A statement can be true and still fail because it is:
- unbeneficial,
- badly timed,
- harshly delivered,
- motivated by ill will.

Therefore the project distinction is:

**truth is a necessary floor; skillful communication requires more than truth alone.**

## 8. Can silence be skillful?

MN 58 strongly implies yes.

If speech is:
- true,
- but unbeneficial,
the Buddha does not say it.

If it is:
- true,
- beneficial,
but the time is wrong,
he waits for the proper time.

Therefore:
**not speaking, or delaying speech, can be skillful when speaking would not serve benefit or timing.**

Guardrail:
silence is not automatically right speech.
A beneficial truth may need to be spoken when the proper time arrives.

## 9. Compassion does not mean avoiding difficult truth

MN 58 explicitly allows beneficial truth that is displeasing.

The reason for speaking is not:
- victory,
- humiliation,
- venting,
but compassion for beings.

Thus compassion does not erase correction.

It changes:
- motive,
- timing,
- manner,
- purpose.

A difficult statement can be compassionate if it is true, beneficial, properly timed and not rooted in hate.

## 10. Right speech and the Aṭṭhakavagga conflict analysis

Phase 2A showed:
- view-clinging,
- māna,
- self-positioning,
- papañca
can culminate in quarrel, accusation, divisive speech and false speech.

Right speech now provides the behavioral interruption:
- do not lie,
- do not divide,
- do not wound through harsh speech,
- do not feed purposeless verbal proliferation.

Project synthesis:
**right speech is one gate where inner selfing either becomes social conflict or is prevented from doing so.**

## 11. Debate is not prohibited; status-war is the problem

The Aṭṭhakavagga criticizes:
- winning,
- praise,
- superiority,
- “only this is true” possession.

Milindapañha later distinguishes:
- discussion like scholars, where refutation and correction can occur without anger,
from
- discussion like kings, where disagreement becomes coercion and punishment.

This is MILINDA_COMPATIBLE with the early-text right-speech framework.

Rigorous disagreement can remain skillful when it preserves:
- truth,
- benefit,
- timing,
- non-hatred.

## 12. MN 117 — right speech is coordinated with view, effort and mindfulness

MN 117 says:
- right view recognizes wrong/right speech,
- right effort abandons wrong speech and enters right speech,
- right mindfulness keeps that task present.

Thus speech is not an isolated etiquette rule.
It is embedded in the training of perception, intention and attention.

## 13. A six-question internal speech test — PROJECT SYNTHESIS

Before speaking, a useful Buddhist-derived check is:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it beneficial?
3. Is this the right time?
4. Can it be said without unnecessary harshness?
5. Is the motive goodwill rather than retaliation/status?
6. Am I willing to remain silent or revise if these conditions are not met?

This list synthesizes MN 58, AN 5.198, MN 21 and MN 61.
It is not a canonical numbered formula.

## 14. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “If it is true, say it.” — MN 58 rejects true but unbeneficial speech.
2. “Compassion means saying only pleasant things.” — beneficial truth may be displeasing.
3. “Gentleness means never correcting.” — correction can be beneficial and timely.
4. “Silence is always superior.” — useful truth may need to be spoken at the proper time.
5. “Harsh truth is justified because it is true.” — AN 5.198 adds gentleness and goodwill.
6. “Right speech is just politeness.” — it is a path factor tied to truth, harm, intention and liberation.
7. “Debate itself is wrong.” — the deeper target is anger, status-defense, divisiveness and attachment to victory.

## 15. CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG / PĀLI TEXTUALLY STRONG**
- right speech abstains from false, divisive, harsh and idle speech.
- well-spoken speech is timely, true, gentle, beneficial and rooted in goodwill.
- true but unbeneficial speech should not be spoken.
- beneficial truth may be spoken even if unwelcome, at the proper time.
- verbal action should be monitored for harm before/during/after.
- goodwill should be preserved even when receiving hostile speech.

**PARALLEL AUDIT PENDING**
- exact Chinese/Āgama parallels for MN 58 / AN 5.198 / MN 61 were not line-audited in this pass.

**MILINDA_COMPATIBLE**
- rigorous disagreement without anger/coercion is compatible with Buddhist inquiry.

## 16. Core synthesis

> In the Buddha's thought, truthfulness is indispensable but not sufficient. Speech becomes right speech when truth is governed by benefit, timing, gentleness and goodwill. Compassion does not require hiding difficult truth; it requires that truth not become a weapon of hatred, pride or victory. Silence, delay and correction can all be skillful depending on whether they reduce harm and support the path.

This is a SYNTHESIS.
