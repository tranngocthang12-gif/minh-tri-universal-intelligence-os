# PHASE 4 — A7 — FEELING, CRAVING, AND THE TWO ARROWS

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: Does feeling necessarily become craving?

SN 36.6: ordinary person and learned noble disciple both experience pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling. The ordinary person adds mental suffering to bodily pain, resists pain, seeks sensual pleasure as escape, and remains tied to greed, aversion, and ignorance. The learned noble disciple experiences the first bodily arrow without the second mental arrow and feels without the same attachment.

SN 12.62 bounded audit slice: feeling arises dependent on contact and ceases when that contact ceases. Seeing this supports disenchantment, dispassion, and release. This does not close the full SN 12 backlog.

SN 12.52: focusing on the allure of clingable phenomena develops craving, clinging, becoming, and birth; focusing on drawbacks supports cessation.

SN 12.64: where passion, delight, and craving for the four nutriments are present, consciousness lands and grows and renewed becoming develops; where they are absent, that sequence does not develop.

CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the formula 'with feeling as condition, craving' should not be read as every feeling mechanically and inevitably producing craving. Feeling is the condition/field in the suffering-producing process; ignorance, resistance, delight, and appropriation determine whether the downstream pattern is fed.

MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Nāgasena says one who will not be reborn can still have bodily pain without mental pain because the conditions for bodily pain remain while the conditions for mental agony have ceased. This strongly fits the two-arrow structure but remains a Milindapañha explanatory layer.

Main conclusion: liberation does not abolish sensation. It removes the ignorance-driven appropriation and reactive pattern that turns feeling into the second arrow and feeds renewed becoming.
