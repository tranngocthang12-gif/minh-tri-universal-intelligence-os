# PHASE 4 — A18 — PAPAÑCA: FROM CONTACT TO PROLIFERATION AND CONFLICT

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: How does `papañca` transform contact, feeling, perception, and thought into proliferating classifications, self-positioning, and conflict?

## MN 18 Madhupiṇḍika
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the Buddha's brief statement says that where the causes by which the perceptions/categories of `papañca` assail a person are not relished, welcomed, or held to, the obsessions of passion, resistance, views, uncertainty, conceit, passion for becoming, and ignorance end; with them end quarrels, disputes, accusation, divisive speech, and false speech.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Mahākaccāna's accepted analysis gives the sequence: sense base + object + consciousness → contact → feeling → perception → thinking → `papañca`; based on what one `papañcizes`, the perceptions/categories of `papañca` then assail the person with respect to past, present, and future objects.
- SOURCE DISCIPLINE: the detailed chain is Mahākaccāna's explanation later confirmed by the Buddha, not the exact wording of the Buddha's initial brief statement.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: `papañca` is not mere quantity of thought. It is a recursive elaboration in which experience becomes categorized and fed back upon the experiencer, supporting obsession and conflict.

## Snp 4.11 Kalahavivāda
- TEXT_ATTESTED: quarrels, disputes, grief, selfishness, conceit, pride, and divisiveness are traced backward through what is dear, desire, pleasant/unpleasant, contact, and name-and-form.
- TEXT_ATTESTED / lexical: `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā` states that the categories/classifications of `papañca` have perception as their source/condition.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: when longing is absent, "mine-ness" does not exist; the sage who understands dependencies and is released does not enter disputes.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Snp 4.11 shows two connected tracks—an affective track (pleasant/unpleasant → desire → dear/not-dear → conflict) and a classificatory track (perception → `papañcasaṅkhā`). They should not be collapsed into one simplistic linear chain.

## Snp 4.12 Cūḷaviyūha
- TEXT_ATTESTED: disputants dwell in their own views, make their own views into truth, and disparage others.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the poem preserves a truth/falsehood distinction while attacking entrenchment, conceit, and dependence on views.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: `papañca` helps explain how a perceptual/cognitive distinction can become a social identity-position—"my view / your view", "skilled / fool", "true / false"—and then feed dispute. This is a synthesis across MN 18 and Snp 4.11-12, not a verbatim single-text definition.

## SN 35 bridge
- TEXT_ATTESTED: bounded SN 35 passages already audited show that sense objects and faculties are not themselves the fetter; desire-passion arising dependent on them is the fetter, and non-conceiving/non-clinging ends agitation.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the point of practice is therefore not sensory erasure but interrupting the appropriative/proliferative relation built on sensory experience.

## Papañca lexical guardrail
- PENDING_INTERPRETATION: no single English or Vietnamese gloss exhausts `papañca`.
- Translational possibilities include proliferation, elaboration, objectification, complication, or classificatory expansion; each highlights only part of the field.
- Do not reduce `papañca` to "overthinking".
- Do not identify `papañca` with `saññā`, `maññati`, `asmimāna`, or `diṭṭhi`; these interact but remain distinct analytic terms.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: no direct, source-audited Milindapañha doctrine of `papañca` has been established in this lesson.
- What is safely usable is the later text's dialectical discipline: expose hidden premises, distinguish designation from substance, and warn against conceit even in learning/attainment (e.g. the full pitcher analogy).
- Guardrail: do not invent a direct Milindapañha `papañca` theory merely because its argumentative style is compatible with this analysis.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
In the early texts studied here, conflict does not begin only when two people meet. It can be prepared inside experience: contact conditions feeling; feeling is perceived; perception is thought about; thought can become `papañca`; proliferated classifications then feed back as frameworks through which past, present, and future experience is interpreted. Combined with desire, mine-making, conceit, and view-entrenchment, this process can turn ordinary distinctions into self-positioning and dispute.

## Practical diagnostic model
PROJECT_INTERPRETATION:
1. contact: something is encountered;
2. feeling: pleasant/unpleasant/neutral tone;
3. perception: it is recognized/labeled;
4. thought: the mind works with the label;
5. `papañca`: distinctions proliferate and become a larger interpretive framework;
6. appropriation: "this is about me / mine / my side";
7. conflict: the framework meets competing frameworks.

This is a study model, not a claim that all texts present one rigid seven-step canonical chain.
