## Research question

Does the early-text material support one simple linear chain such as:

vedanā → saññā → vitakka → papañca → taṇhā → upādāna → self

or are these different analytical cuts through a more interdependent process?

## TEXTUAL FINDING

### 1. MN 18 / MĀ 115: cognitive sequence
MN 18 presents:
sense base + object → consciousness → contact → feeling → perception (saññā) → thinking (vitakka) → papañca; from what is papañcized, papañca-related perceptions/categories then assail the person regarding past, present and future objects.

MĀ 115 preserves a closely corresponding sequence:
eye + form → eye-consciousness → contact → felt experience → 想 → 思 → 念 → 分別, and applies the analysis across the six sense doors. Comparative scholarship identifies the later Chinese terms in this sequence as corresponding approximately to the Pāli papañceti / papañca-saññā-saṅkhā segment.

This gives strong cross-tradition support for a cognitive expansion after feeling and perception.

### 2. Saññā is not intrinsically delusive
AN 6.110 explicitly teaches cultivating perceptions of renunciation, good will and harmlessness to abandon sensual, malicious and harmful perceptions.

Therefore:
- saññā cannot be equated with delusion as such;
- the relevant question is what kind of perception operates and how it is used.

### 3. Papañca: translation remains open
The Pāli term appears in several related forms: papañceti, papañcita, papañca-saññā, papañca-saṅkhā, papañca-saññā-saṅkhā, nippapañca, etc.

Modern translations vary: conceptual proliferation, complication, objectification, manifoldization and related glosses.

Working policy:
- retain **papañca** as the technical term in core analysis;
- use glosses only contextually;
- do not define it by one English/Vietnamese equivalent.

### 4. SN 35.248 / SĀ 1168: papañca and self-referential construing
SN 35.248 classifies thoughts such as:
- “I am”,
- “I am this”,
- “I shall be / shall not be”,
- future identity in terms of form/perception

under several overlapping descriptions including maññita, papañcita and māna-related activity.

Its SĀ 1168 parallel preserves the same basic self-referential cluster:
“this is I,” “this is mine,” future existence/non-existence, future form/no-form, future perception/no-perception, etc., describing these as agitation/bondage-producing modes.

This strengthens the link between papañca/self-construal and identity construction across traditions, even though exact term-by-term equivalence should not be forced.

### 5. MN 38 / MĀ 201: viññāṇa is condition-dependent, not a self-identical transmigrating entity
MN 38 rejects Sāti’s claim that “this same consciousness” transmigrates unchanged. It says consciousness arises dependently and is named according to its condition: eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, etc.

MĀ 201 preserves the same dispute and the core principle:
consciousness arises because of conditions; where conditions are present it arises, and without conditions it ceases.

This is strong cross-tradition evidence against reading viññāṇa as an invariant subject/soul.

### 6. SN 12.65 / SN 12.67: consciousness is not simply one-way downstream
SN 12.65 and SN 12.67 present mutual conditionality:
- consciousness conditions name-and-form;
- name-and-form conditions consciousness.

SN 12.67 uses the simile of two sheaves of reeds leaning on one another.

This is decisive counterevidence to any model that represents the relevant early Buddhist psychology as one universally linear conveyor belt.

## PARALLEL STATUS

- MN 18 ↔ MĀ 115, EĀ 40.10: recognized parallel family; MĀ 115 preserves a closely matching six-sense cognitive sequence.
- MN 38 ↔ MĀ 201, EĀ 21.3 (partial), Sanskrit fragments: recognized parallel family; the rejection of unchanged transmigrating consciousness is preserved in MĀ 201.
- SN 35.248 ↔ SĀ 1168: recognized direct parallel; the self/future-existence cluster is preserved.
- SN 12.67 ↔ SĀ 288, Sanskrit fragment parallels: recognized parallel family for mutual dependence of consciousness and name-and-form; exact comparative pass remains to be expanded if needed.

## INTERPRETATION

The evidence supports at least two different analytical views:

### A. Cognitive-processing view
sense/object → consciousness → contact → feeling → perception → thought → papañca

### B. Dependent-arising / suffering-continuity view
contact → feeling → craving → grasping → becoming → birth → suffering

They overlap at contact/feeling but should not be mechanically spliced into one canonical chain.

Therefore the previous selfing synthesis must be corrected:
- **do not write:** vedanā → saññā → papañca → taṇhā → upādāna as though it were one canonical formula;
- **do write:** early texts provide several partially overlapping analyses of how sensory experience can develop into craving, grasping, conceptual proliferation, self-construal and conflict.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “saññā is inherently delusion” — contradicted by cultivated wholesome perceptions.
2. “papañca simply means thinking” — too broad; MN 18 distinguishes vitakka from papañca.
3. “viññāṇa is the hidden permanent knower” — contradicted by MN 38/MĀ 201.
4. “viññāṇa is only a momentary passive sense event” — too narrow; SN 12.65/67 give it a broader conditional role with name-and-form.
5. “the whole system is one linear chain” — contradicted by mutual consciousness/name-and-form conditionality and by the coexistence of different analytical sequences.

## MILINDA STATUS

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE
Milindapañha repeatedly analyzes sense experience into distinguishable functions such as contact, feeling, perception/idea, intention and reflection and treats consciousness/knowing without introducing an invariant soul.

### MILINDA_EXTENSION / methodological caution
Milinda also presents a more systematized analysis of mental factors as distinguishable functions associated with one sense episode. This is useful clarification, but it is later and should not be used to turn the early sutta sequences into an Abhidhamma-style fixed event ontology.

A useful Milinda insight is precisely that these mental conditions are analytically distinguishable while difficult to separate in lived experience.

## CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- viññāṇa is condition-dependent and not a self-identical transmigrating substance;
- MN 18/MĀ 115 preserve a cognitive sequence from contact/feeling through perception/thought toward a proliferative/conflict-prone process;
- saññā has both unwholesome and wholesome forms;
- the system cannot safely be reduced to one universal linear chain.

**EBT_PROBABLE**
- papañca is tightly associated with self-referential construction and conflict.
- exact semantic core of papañca remains translation-sensitive.

**OPEN**
- whether craving normally precedes, follows or recursively reinforces papañca in every relevant context.
- whether one unified process model can be reconstructed without importing later systematics.

## PRACTICAL MEANING

In direct observation, avoid forcing experience into a preconceived single sequence. Instead distinguish:
- what is being sensed;
- what feeling-tone arises;
- how it is recognized/labeled;
- what thought says about it;
- whether the mind begins expanding into past/future/self stories;
- whether desire, resistance or grasping is operating.

The practical hypothesis is that craving, grasping, self-construal and papañca can mutually reinforce one another even if the suttas analyze them through different schemas.

This is a SYNTHESIS to be tested, not a canonical formula.
