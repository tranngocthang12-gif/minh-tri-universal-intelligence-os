## A151 — disagreement, correction, and genuine concord

Central question:
When should one remain silent, when should one speak, and how can one correct another without turning disagreement into division? How does genuine concord differ from merely keeping the surface peaceful?

### TEXT_ATTESTED

1. **MN 58 (Abhaya)** gives a disciplined speech matrix. The Tathāgata does not speak what is untrue. For true speech, whether pleasant or unpleasant, usefulness and proper timing matter. Thus “it may upset someone” is not sufficient reason to suppress a beneficial truth, and “it is true” is not sufficient reason to say it immediately or harshly.
2. **AN 5.198** gives five conditions for admonishing another: speak at the right time, speak truthfully, speak gently, speak beneficially, and speak with a mind of loving-kindness rather than inner hate.
3. **MN 103** distinguishes disputes about the teaching from disputes driven by personal friction and describes roots of dispute such as anger, resentment, contempt, jealousy, deceit, and attachment to views. The state of the disputants matters, not only the proposition disputed.
4. **MN 104** treats concord as a serious communal good and provides ways of settling disputes. Harmony is therefore not mere avoidance of disagreement; it includes procedures for dealing with actual conflict.
5. Right speech includes abstention from divisive speech and cultivation of speech that reconciles and strengthens concord. This does not require concealing harmful conduct or falsehood.

### SILENCE != CONCORD

A151's central distinction:

- genuine concord: disagreement is handled without hatred, deception, faction-building, or abandonment of truth;
- superficial peace: important problems are suppressed so nobody appears upset;
- divisive speech: speech used to split, recruit factions, humiliate, or intensify hostility;
- corrective speech: truthful, beneficial, timely communication directed toward understanding, restraint, repair, or protection.

These are CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS categories, not a canonical fourfold taxonomy.

### MN 58 — TRUE IS NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT

A151 preserves three filters:
- Is it true?
- Is it beneficial?
- Is this the right time/manner?

Therefore two common errors are rejected:

Error A:
“I am only telling the truth,” used to justify cruelty.

Error B:
“We must preserve harmony,” used to justify hiding a necessary truth.

The first drops compassion/timing.
The second drops truth/benefit.

### AN 5.198 — CORRECTION TESTS THE CORRECTOR

Before correcting another, examine:
- timing;
- factual accuracy;
- gentleness;
- benefit;
- one's own motive.

A person can be factually right yet psychologically driven by revenge, superiority, or desire to embarrass.

Thus correction is not only a test of the recipient.
It is a test of the speaker's mind.

This final sentence is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### DISAGREEMENT != DIVISION

Two practitioners can disagree on:
- interpretation;
- procedure;
- factual assessment;
without becoming enemies.

Division begins when disagreement is appropriated:
“my view,” “our side,” “their side,” followed by anger, contempt, coalition-building, or refusal to hear.

A151 links this to earlier work on diṭṭhi-upādāna, māna, and group identity.

### WHEN SILENCE BECOMES UNWHOLESOME

Silence may be skillful when:
- facts are unclear;
- emotion is too heated;
- timing is poor;
- speaking would add no benefit.

Silence may become unskillful when it functions to:
- conceal serious harm;
- protect status or reputation;
- avoid necessary accountability;
- leave preventable harm unaddressed.

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION from the speech and ethical guardrails; it is not a verbatim canonical list.

### CORRECT WITHOUT MAKING AN ENEMY

A151's working method:
1. establish the facts;
2. check one's motive;
3. separate conduct/claim from permanent identity;
4. choose a useful time;
5. speak truthfully and gently;
6. state the harm or doctrinal issue precisely;
7. allow response and correction of one's own errors;
8. aim at repair/protection/understanding rather than victory;
9. avoid recruiting factions unnecessarily;
10. if harm continues, stronger boundaries or formal communal procedures may be required.

This ten-step method is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### CONCORD DOES NOT REQUIRE EQUAL TREATMENT OF TRUE AND FALSE

Non-divisiveness is not relativism.

If one claim is false or one conduct harmful, concord does not require saying:
“both sides are equally right.”

It requires addressing the difference without adding hatred, slander, humiliation, or factional craving.

### CONNECTION TO A150

A150 showed that group belonging can become collective identity.
A151 adds that conflict is a high-pressure moment where this appropriation becomes visible.

Ask:
“Am I trying to resolve the issue, or trying to make my side win?”

That question often exposes whether Dhamma or identity is directing the dispute.

### PRACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC

Before speaking in a conflict:
1. Do I know the facts?
2. Is what I plan to say true?
3. Is it useful?
4. Is now the right time?
5. Can I say it without unnecessary harshness?
6. Is there hidden anger, humiliation, or superiority in me?
7. Am I correcting the issue or attacking a person?
8. Am I creating factions?
9. Would silence protect peace—or merely protect reputation?
10. What outcome would count as repair rather than victory?

### MILINDAPANHA — MANDATORY

Consulted: YES.

Milindapanha provides later/paracanonical support through its dialogical pattern:
- difficult objections are permitted;
- apparent contradictions are stated openly;
- Nāgasena answers by distinction and reasoning;
- the dialogue aims at clarification rather than suppression of challenge.

Role:
later support for rigorous disagreement without equating challenge with hostility.

Guardrail:
Milindapanha does not override MN 58, AN 5.198, MN 103, MN 104, or early right-speech material.

### CORRECTIONS PRESERVED

- harmony != silence;
- truth != license for cruelty;
- unpleasant speech != wrong speech by definition;
- pleasant speech != right speech by definition;
- correction != humiliation;
- disagreement != schism;
- non-divisiveness != relativism;
- loving-kindness != refusal to set boundaries;
- communal reputation != truth;
- Milindapanha remains later/paracanonical support.
