## Research question

If there is no invariant self, what makes karmic responsibility coherent? What exactly is kamma in the early texts, what does it condition, how can present intention alter future continuity, and how does the early teaching avoid both fatalism and moral randomness?

## 1. AN 6.63 — kamma is centered on intention

AN 6.63 gives the famous formulation:
**“Intention (cetanā), I tell you, is kamma; intending, one acts by body, speech and mind.”**

The same discourse says:
- contact is the source/condition of kamma;
- kamma has diverse results;
- results may ripen in the present, later, or subsequently;
- there is a cessation of kamma and a path leading to that cessation.

This is crucial:
kamma is not introduced as a cosmic substance, fate-stock, or invisible moral fluid.
It is centered on intentional action.

### Parallel status
SuttaCentral parallel metadata lists:
- MĀ 111,
- T 57,
as Chinese parallels/counterparts.

This pass has **not** line-by-line verified that the exact sentence “cetanā is kamma” appears in identical form in both Chinese witnesses.

Therefore:
- Pāli textual status: strong;
- exact cross-tradition wording: pending.

## 2. SN 36.21 — not every experience is caused by past kamma

SN 36.21 directly rejects the thesis:
“Whatever a person experiences—pleasant, painful, or neutral—is all caused by past deeds.”

The discourse lists multiple sources of feeling/experience, including:
- bodily disorders,
- imbalance,
- climate,
- careless behavior,
- assault,
- kamma-result.

Therefore:
**kamma is one causal domain among others, not total determinism.**

This is decisive against:
- “everything that happens to you is repayment for past karma,”
- victim-blaming through karmic speculation,
- treating disease, injury or ordinary bodily states as necessarily moral desert.

Exact Chinese-parallel status for SN 36.21 was not established in this pass.

## 3. SN 12.38 / SĀ 359 — present volition and latent tendency shape future continuity

SN 12.38 says:
what one wills, plans, and has a latent tendency toward becomes a basis for the establishment of consciousness.

When consciousness becomes established and grows:
- future renewed existence arises,
- then future birth, aging and death.

When willing/planning/latent tendency no longer supplies a basis:
- consciousness is not established in that way,
- future renewed existence does not arise.

SĀ 359 is a direct parallel.

This means present intentional and dispositional activity is not merely the passive result of past kamma.
It helps shape future continuity.

## 4. SN 12.52 / SĀ 286 — what is “fed” continues

SN 12.52 says:
when one attends to the gratification in things that fuel grasping:
- craving grows,
- grasping arises,
- becoming,
- birth,
- aging/death and suffering.

The fire simile makes the mechanism clear:
a fire continues because fuel is repeatedly supplied.

When one contemplates the drawback rather than feeding gratification:
- craving ceases,
- grasping ceases,
- the downstream process ceases.

SĀ 286 is listed as the parallel.

Important implication:
the future is conditioned by what is **fed now**.

## 5. SN 12.51 / SĀ 292 / SF 159 — ignorance conditions karmically productive formation

SN 12.51 presents a thorough investigation of suffering back through dependent arising to ignorance.

It then says:
- one immersed in ignorance can generate meritorious, demeritorious or imperturbable formations;
- consciousness “fares on” accordingly;
- when ignorance has been abandoned and knowledge arisen, the practitioner no longer generates these karmically productive formations;
- not fabricating in this way, one does not cling; without clinging, there is no agitation; Nibbāna is realized.

SuttaCentral parallel metadata lists:
- SĀ 292,
- Sanskrit fragment SF 159.

This is a major correction to simplistic moralism:
**even wholesome/meritorious karmic production belongs to the conditioned saṃsāric framework; liberation ultimately goes beyond producing further rebirth-oriented karmic formations.**

Guardrail:
this does **not** mean ethical action is useless before liberation.
The early path repeatedly requires wholesome action, right effort, virtue and cultivation.

## 6. Responsibility without an invariant self

The early-text problem can be formulated:

If the future being is not numerically identical to a permanent self,
why should the present action have morally relevant consequences for that future stream?

The early material answers structurally rather than with a carrier-substance:
- intention produces action;
- action alters dispositions/conditions;
- craving and tendencies condition consciousness-establishment;
- consciousness/nāmarūpa support renewed existence;
- effects arise because of causal dependence.

Thus responsibility is grounded in **causal inheritance**, not in an immutable owner.

This is a SYNTHESIS, not one canonical sentence.

## 7. Milindapañha stress-test — “neither same nor another”

Milindapañha says the reborn one is:
**neither exactly the same nor entirely another.**

Its child/adult, lamp and milk-transformations analogies preserve:
- causal continuity,
- change,
- non-identity of substance.

### Mil 3.5.7 — no being transmigrates, yet kamma is not escaped

King Milinda asks:
if no being transmigrates from this body to another, would one not escape evil deeds?

Nāgasena says no, if rebirth occurs.

He uses the mango analogy:
- a thief steals fruit,
- not the literal same fruit or seed that was originally planted,
- yet the stolen fruit exists because of that planted tree,
- therefore causal responsibility remains.

Nāgasena then says this name-and-form performs pure or impure deeds, and because of that kamma another name-and-form is reborn.

This is later explanatory literature, but it directly stress-tests the responsibility problem.

**Status:** MILINDA_COMPATIBLE / later clarification.

## 8. The early model is not “moral accounting by a self”

A useful distinction:

### Not supported
A permanent self:
- owns a karmic balance,
- carries it unchanged,
- receives rewards/punishments later.

### Better supported
A conditioned stream:
- forms intentions,
- acts,
- thereby changes causal conditions,
- later states inherit effects from earlier states,
- continuity is real without strict identity.

This preserves both:
- anattā,
- moral causality.

## 9. Kamma is not the whole causal universe

SN 36.21 is the critical anti-fatalism text.

The model must therefore distinguish:
- **kammic causation**: consequences linked to intentional deeds;
- **non-kammic causation**: bodily, environmental, accidental/assault-related and other conditions.

This prevents the false equation:
**cause = kamma**.

Dependent arising is broader than karmic causation.

## 10. Present agency is conditioned but not irrelevant

The current early-text model does not require an unconditioned free-will self.

Instead:
- present intention is itself conditioned,
- but present intention can condition later outcomes,
- training changes the conditions under which future intentions arise.

Thus practical agency can exist without metaphysical independence from causes.

Working formulation:
**freedom grows by changing the causal constraints on future reaction, not by inserting an uncaused self into the process.**

This is a PROJECT SYNTHESIS.

## 11. Merit, good kamma and liberation are not identical

SN 12.51 creates an important three-level distinction:

1. unwholesome intentional activity can condition painful outcomes;
2. wholesome/meritorious activity can condition better outcomes and supports the path;
3. final liberation is not just accumulating the best possible rebirth-producing kamma; it ends the ignorance/craving process that keeps producing renewed existence.

Therefore Buddhist ethics is not reducible to:
“collect merit forever.”

Merit can be skillful within saṃsāra and path-supporting, while Nibbāna is the cessation of the rebirth-generating process.

## 12. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Kamma means fate.” — SN 36.21 directly contradicts this.
2. “Everything that happens is due to past kamma.” — directly rejected in SN 36.21.
3. “No self means no responsibility.” — causal continuity preserves consequence without invariant identity.
4. “The exact same consciousness receives the later result.” — MN 38 rejects unchanged transmigrating consciousness.
5. “Kamma is an invisible substance carried between lives.” — not how AN 6.63 defines it.
6. “Only past kamma matters.” — SN 12.38 and SN 12.52 show present volition/feeding changes future continuity.
7. “Good kamma is identical with liberation.” — SN 12.51 distinguishes meritorious formation from the ending of karmically productive formation under knowledge.
8. “Because intention is conditioned, ethical effort is meaningless.” — the path itself works by conditioning future intention, attention and response.

## 13. CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- cetanā/intention is central to the definition of kamma in AN 6.63;
- not all feeling/experience is caused by past kamma;
- present intention/tendency can condition future renewed existence;
- craving/grasping must be repeatedly “fed” to continue;
- liberation stops the production of the relevant rebirth-sustaining formations.

**CROSS-TRADITION STRONG**
- SN 12.38 ↔ SĀ 359;
- SN 12.51 ↔ SĀ 292 + SF 159;
- SN 12.52 ↔ SĀ 286.

**CROSS-TRADITION PARTIAL / PENDING**
- AN 6.63 has MĀ 111 and T 57 listed as parallels, but the exact cetanā=kamma wording has not yet been line-audited here.
- SN 36.21 parallel status not established in this pass.

**MILINDA_COMPATIBLE**
- responsibility through causal production without strict identity.

**OPEN**
- exact mechanics by which karmic causal efficacy is transmitted across the death/rebirth boundary in the earliest strata;
- how much technical “karmic seed/potential” language is early versus later metaphor/systematization;
- precise relation among cetanā, saṅkhāra and kamma in every early context;
- whether all wholesome action of an arahant is best described as “non-kamma” in the strict later technical sense — later traditions systematize this more explicitly than the suttas.

## 14. PRACTICAL MEANING

The ethically relevant question is not:
> “What did I do in a previous life to deserve this?”

but:
> “What conditions produced this situation, which of them are karmic or non-karmic, and what intention am I adding now?”

This makes ethics forward-looking as well as retrospective.

The present moment matters because:
- intention acts,
- action conditions habit/disposition,
- disposition conditions future perception/reaction,
- repeated craving/grasping feeds continued becoming.
