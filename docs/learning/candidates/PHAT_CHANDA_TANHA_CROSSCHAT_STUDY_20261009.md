## Central question

If the path ends craving, how can intention, aspiration, persistence and even the wish for liberation function during practice? Do not assume that all Vietnamese uses of "muốn", English "desire", Pāli `chanda`, and Pāli `taṇhā` are interchangeable.

## TEXT_ATTESTED — bounded reading

**AN 4.159, Bhikkhunī Sutta (Venerable Ānanda speaking):**
The discourse explicitly says a practitioner eventually abandons craving by relying on craving. A monk hears of another's liberation, wishes that he too might realize liberation, and eventually abandons craving. Pāli spot-check: `taṇhaṁ nissāya taṇhaṁ pajahati`. This is **not** a blanket endorsement of any craving. Source and Pāli: https://www.wordsofthebuddha.org/an4.159 . Compare translation by Thanissaro: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN4_159.html and Bhikkhu Sujato: https://thebuddhaswords.net/an/an4.159.html . The discourse directly attributes this explanation to Ānanda, not as verbatim direct Buddha speech.

**SN 51.13, Chanda Sutta:**
A base of power can be developed using concentration founded on `chanda` alongside the exertions involved in preventing/abandoning unskillful states and arousing/maintaining skillful states. The same term translated "desire" here has a path-supporting role. https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN51_13.html . This single text does not prove every occurrence of chanda is wholesome.

**SN 51.15, Uṇṇābhabrāhmaṇa Sutta (Ānanda speaking):**
A brahmin objects that abandoning desire via desire would be an infinite regress. Ānanda notes that the specific desire to go to the park subsides once the person arrives, illustrating how the desire to reach arahantship is stilled upon achievement. Pāli spot-check: `chandappahānatthaṁ` and `chandasamādhi` in the discourse. Translation by Thanissaro: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN51_15.html . Alternate translation/Pāli and related editorial cautions: https://thebuddhaswords.net/sn/sn51.15.html . Thus an instrumental motivation can have a task and endpoint.

**MN 19, Dvedhāvitakka Sutta (Buddha recounts pre-awakening practice):**
Thinking is distinguished by its quality and consequences, not rejected indiscriminately. Sensuality, ill will and harmfulness are abandoned; renunciation, non-ill will and harmlessness support discernment and the way to liberation. The discourse also notes that prolonging even skillful thought can tire the body and disturb concentration, leading to settling the mind. https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN19.html ; compare Bhikkhu Sujato: https://suttacentral.net/MN19/en/sujato . This is about `vitakka`; it is not a lexical definition of `chanda` or `taṇhā`.

## MILINDAPAÑHA — mandatory LATER/PARACANONICAL consultation

**Mil 6.3.11, "The Time of Nirvana"** examines the difference between practicing in order to realize Nibbāna and claiming that a practitioner produces Nibbāna as a conditioned artifact. Nāgasena uses examples of effort leading out of danger and into safety. This helps explain **means versus attainment**, but does **not** directly define chanda or taṇhā, and cannot override early-discourse evidence. Historical level: later/paracanonical. Source: https://www.dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/en/mil6.3.11.html .

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS — not a canonical numbered taxonomy

1. `chanda` describes desire/interest/intention in some contexts and can support effective, disciplined practice (SN 51.13–15); this does not make it universally wholesome by lexical identity.
2. `taṇhā` is a key target of abandoning, yet AN 4.159 uses craving-language for a provisional motivation that is eventually transcended. Do not overwrite AN 4.159 by saying every apparently worthy initial aspiration **must** already be free of taṇhā.
3. `vitakka` as intentional thinking in MN 19 is to be evaluated by harm, wisdom, and liberation, not equated with taṇhā or chanda.
4. In the path, a motive can be *used* without being made into an unending identity or permanent possession. This is a cross-text explanatory synthesis, not a dictionary definition or a license for craving.
5. SN 51.15 and AN 4.159 show **temporal instrumentality**: what is employed along the way need not be retained once the goal is reached.
6. Mil 6.3.11 gives a later explanatory caution: the practice and its effects must not be flattened into "effort manufactures the unconditioned." This last connection to the previous points is our synthesis only.
