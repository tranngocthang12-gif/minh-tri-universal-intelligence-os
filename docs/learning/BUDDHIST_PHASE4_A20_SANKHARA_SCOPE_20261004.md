# PHASE 4 — A20 — SAṄKHĀRA: CHOICES, FABRICATIONS, FORMATIONS, CONDITIONED THINGS

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: What does `saṅkhāra` mean in dependent origination, and why does the same word-family appear elsewhere with broader meanings such as volitional formations or conditioned things?

## SN 12.2 / MN 9
- TEXT_ATTESTED: in the dependent-origination analysis, `saṅkhārā` are classified as bodily, verbal, and mental formations/choices.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 9 preserves the same threefold classification and analyzes their origin in ignorance, cessation with ignorance, and the Noble Eightfold Path as the path to their cessation.
- SOURCE CAUTION: the compounds "bodily/verbal/mental formation" also occur in other contexts such as MN 44 with more specific context-bound definitions. Do not automatically import those sub-definitions into every occurrence of SN 12.2/MN 9.

## SN 12.51
- TEXT_ATTESTED: an ignorant person generates meritorious, demeritorious, or imperturbable `saṅkhāra`; consciousness then goes in the corresponding direction.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: with ignorance abandoned and knowledge arisen, the liberated practitioner does not generate/fashion those formations and does not cling; the discourse continues to Nibbāna.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: in this dependent-origination context, `saṅkhāra` has a strongly karmic-volitional function. It is not adequately rendered as a passive "thing that happens".

## AN 6.63 supporting bridge
- TEXT_ATTESTED: intention (`cetanā`) is identified as kamma; intending, one acts by body, speech, and mind.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this supports reading at least an important part of dependent-origination `saṅkhāra` as morally/causally potent volitional activity, while not asserting that `saṅkhāra` and `cetanā` are interchangeable in every textual context.

## SN 22.79 — aggregate usage
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the aggregate of volitional formations is explained through the activity of "constructing/fabricating the conditioned": it constructs conditioned form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, and consciousness.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: here `saṅkhārā` are an aggregate category with an active constructing/fabricating function; the term cannot simply be equated with the totality of all conditioned phenomena.

## Dhammapada 277–279 — broad usage contrast
- TEXT_ATTESTED: verses 277–278 say all `saṅkhārā` are impermanent and unsatisfactory; verse 279 shifts to "all dhammas are not-self".
- LEXICAL INTERPRETATION: in this broad contemplative usage, `saṅkhārā` is conventionally translated as formations/conditioned things. The shift from `saṅkhārā` to `dhammā` in verse 279 is important and should not be erased.
- GUARDRAIL: do not assume one English/Vietnamese gloss covers every occurrence. Context determines whether `saṅkhāra` refers to volitional/karmic formation, an aggregate, a conditioning activity, or conditioned formations more broadly.

## Saṅkhāra vs saṅkhata
- PROJECT_INTERPRETATION / LEXICAL GUARDRAIL: the terms share a root but should not be mechanically collapsed.
- Earlier A11 used `saṅkhata` versus `asaṅkhata` for conditioned versus unconditioned.
- SN 22.79 itself distinguishes formations that "construct the conditioned" (`saṅkhata`), showing that agent/process and product/status should not be treated as identical merely because the words are related.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Nāgasena explicitly treats intention/purpose as something conceived/prepared and illustrates how intentionally prepared good or bad action bears consequences beyond death.
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: continuity analogies ("neither the same nor another", lamp, milk-curd-butter-ghee) help explain how karmically potent activity can condition later continuity without requiring an invariant soul.
- Guardrail: Mi Tiên sharpens the causal argument but remains a later dialectical layer; it does not settle every lexical sense of early `saṅkhāra`.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
`Saṅkhāra` is a context-sensitive term. In dependent origination, the strongest current evidence points to formative, volitional, karmically consequential activity rooted in ignorance; SN 12.51 makes this especially explicit through meritorious/demeritorious/imperturbable formations. In the five aggregates, the term names the volitional-formations aggregate and is explained as constructing the conditioned. In broad contemplative formulas such as Dhammapada 277–278, the plural can cover conditioned formations generally. Therefore translating every occurrence simply as "hành", "formation", "fabrication", or "choice" without context loses important distinctions.

## Guardrails
- Do not read `saṅkhāra` as one fixed metaphysical substance.
- Do not reduce all `saṅkhāra` to conscious choice; underlying formations and context-specific uses are broader than ordinary deliberate decision.
- Do not turn "ignorance conditions saṅkhāra" into fatalism: the same texts make cessation possible through knowledge and the path.
- Do not identify the five-aggregate `saṅkhārakkhandha`, dependent-origination `saṅkhārā`, and every occurrence of "all saṅkhārā" as perfectly coextensive without passage-specific audit.
