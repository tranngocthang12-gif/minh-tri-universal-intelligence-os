- Snp 4.9 (Māgaṇḍiya), especially Sn 837, 839–847: not grasping among views; purity not based on view and not secured by the simple opposite; relying on a view; `sacca/musā`; conceit/`tammaya`; grasped view and clash.
- Snp 4.12 (Cūḷaviyūha), especially Sn 878–894: dwelling in one's own view; making one's view true; “one truth, not a second”; proliferating many claimed truths; reasoning around views; conceit and dispute.
- Snp 4.13 (Mahāviyūha), especially Sn 895, 903–913: “this alone is true”; one's convention called truth; purity through view; constructed view foregrounded and relied upon; not being view-led; release from `diṭṭhigata`.
- Snp 4.15 (Attadaṇḍa), especially Sn 941 and 946: positive `sacco` / `saccā` usage (truthful / not departing from truth), showing that the `sacca` word-family is not always polemical.
- Morphological guardrail: forms from `diṭṭha` (“seen”, past participle of √dis) occur in the same collection and must not be silently counted as noun `diṭṭhi` (“view”).

LEXICAL_FINDINGS:
1. `diṭṭhi` has a contextual range centered on view/opinion/doctrine/position. In Aṭṭhakavagga its negative force often comes from what is done with a view: dwelling in it, fixing it, taking it up, relying on it, putting it in front, claiming purity/supremacy through it, or using it in a dispute.
2. Therefore `diṭṭhi` is not lexically identical to `diṭṭhupādāna` or to “clinging”. Constructions such as taking up a `diṭṭhi` or relying on a `diṭṭhi` show the view and the grasping/reliance operation as analytically distinguishable.
3. The poems nevertheless contain genuinely strong “beyond/not constructing/not adopting views” language. It would also be an overcorrection to reduce the Aṭṭhakavagga to “views are fine so long as one is not emotionally attached.”
4. Snp 4.9 blocks a simplistic binary: purity is not said to come merely from a view, and the opposite pole (“no view” in common translations of the contrast) is not presented as a sufficient purity-method either. This weakens readings that turn “no-view” into a new doctrine or identity.
5. The dispute pattern is strongly attested: taking/fixing one's view, asserting exclusive truth, self-elevation or opponent-devaluation, victory/defeat, and quarrel repeatedly cluster together. But the text does not justify “every disagreement or every debate = view-clinging.”
6. Identification language needs precision. Snp 4.9 directly gives conceit/comparison and `tammaya` language in the relevant neighborhood, and Snp 4.12–4.13 directly give self-authorizing and superiority structures. The broader modern formulation “identity defense” is a cross-text analytical synthesis, not a lexical definition of `diṭṭhi`.
7. `sacca` is polyfunctional. In the assigned passages it can mark (a) truth/what is true, (b) what a disputant calls true, (c) individual/private truth-claims (`paccekasacca`), (d) the true/false pair `sacca/musā`, and (e) positive truth/truthfulness language in Snp 4.15.
8. The juxtaposition of “this alone is true” polemics with Snp 4.12's “one truth, not a second” means Aṭṭhakavagga is not safely reducible either to relativism (“there is no truth”) or to a rival exclusivist dogma. The key distinction is between truth and the act of making one's own view/convention into a self-certifying truth-claim.
9. The referent of “one truth” in Snp 4.12 is not explicitly identified there as the Four Noble Truths, Nibbāna, or an ineffable Absolute. Any such identification requires additional argument and should not be marked TEXT_ATTESTED from that verse alone.
10. `paccekasacca` should not be imported into later “two truths” schemes without evidence; in this local polemical context it is best treated cautiously as individual/private truth-claim or individually held truth.

TEXT_ATTESTED:
- Snp 4.8 directly links taking up a view plus “this alone is true” with debate.
- Snp 4.9 directly rejects purification merely by view and also rejects the simple opposite pole as a sufficient basis; it also uses view/reliance/grasping, true/false, conceit, and non-`tammaya` language in the same discourse.
- Snp 4.12 directly portrays disputants making their own views true, contrasts “one truth” with the multiplication of many claimed truths, and links view-positioning with dispute and conceit.
- Snp 4.13 directly portrays “this alone is true,” one's own convention being called true, purity sought through view, and release from view-positions.
- Snp 4.15 directly uses the `sacca` word-family positively; therefore “`sacca` always means dogmatic truth-claim in Aṭṭhakavagga” is false.
- Direct text supports a recurrent relation among fixed/adopted views, exclusivist truth claims, self-ranking, and dispute. A universal equation “view = clinging = identity” is not directly attested.

OVERCLAIMS_FOUND:
1. OVERCLAIM if stated universally: “Aṭṭhakavagga rejects every view as false.” The poems contain strong non-construction/non-adoption language, but they do not establish the proposition “all propositions/views are false,” and Snp 4.9 resists making the opposite/no-view pole into the purity criterion.
2. OVERCLAIM: “`diṭṭhi` means clinging.” More precise: `diṭṭhi` is view/position; clinging, taking-up, fixation, reliance and appropriation are related operations/states that can qualify what happens to a view.
3. OVERCLAIM: “`sacca` means subjective truth/dogma.” Snp 4.12 and 4.15 preserve positive/non-polemical truth senses as well.
4. OVERCLAIM: “Snp 4.12's one truth = Four Noble Truths / Nibbāna / ineffable Absolute” without an additional source argument. The verse does not itself name that referent.
5. OVERCLAIM if presented as direct Aṭṭhakavagga wording: “the text defines identity defense through views.” The text directly attests self-ranking, conceit, `tammaya`-related non-identification, self-certification and dispute; “identity defense” is the wider synthesis.
6. Earlier project classification around A127 is defensible only because it already labels the identity-defense component as CROSS_TEXT synthesis and keeps Aṭṭhakavagga lexical audit open. It should not be silently upgraded to passage-level direct attestation.

COUNTER_READINGS:
- Strongest “no-view” counter-reading: Snp 4.5, 4.9 and 4.13 can be read more radically than a mere anti-clinging ethic: the sage does not construct/adopt a view and is described as freed from view-positions. This evidence must not be minimized.
- Counter to that radical reading: Snp 4.9 denies that the simple opposite pole is itself the purity-basis, and the wider early-discourse corpus retains functional Right View. Therefore “Aṭṭhakavagga teaches propositional skepticism” is not compelled by these passages.
- Strongest anti-relativist counter-reading: Snp 4.12 states “one truth, not a second,” and Snp 4.15 uses truth/truthfulness positively. Therefore the critique of “this alone is true” is better read as a critique of appropriated/self-certifying truth-claims than as a denial of truth itself.
- Strongest counter to a dogmatic “one final proposition” reading: Snp 4.12–4.13 repeatedly show people making their own view/convention into truth and then fighting over it. The local context makes it unsafe to turn “one truth” into another sectarian slogan without further evidence.

UNCERTAINTIES:
- `diṭṭha` (“seen”) and `diṭṭhi` (“view”) are morphologically related-looking in Roman script but are not interchangeable lexical items. Ambiguous translated occurrences must be checked against Pāli morphology before counting them as `diṭṭhi`.
- Snp 4.9 `adiṭṭhiyā`: common translations render the contrast as “nor from no views”, but exact morphology and best English/Vietnamese rendering should remain open pending apparatus/parallel-level checking. The secure contextual claim is narrower: the simple opposite is not made the purity criterion.
- Snp 4.3 `saccamanā`: compound semantics should not be flattened into bare noun `sacca`; exact nuance remains open.
- Snp 4.9 `tammaya`: “made of that / identified with that / absorbed in that” has translation nuance. It supports a non-identification reading locally, but the modern noun “identity” is broader.
- Snp 4.12 “one truth”: exact intended referent remains underdetermined by that verse alone.
- Snp 4.15 `saccā avokkamma`: positive truth-reference is clear enough for the lexical guardrail, but the full doctrinal referent of `saccā` should not be over-specified from the line alone.
- This pass is not a full machine-normalized concordance and does not claim exhaustiveness for all inflected/compound forms across the whole Aṭṭhakavagga.
