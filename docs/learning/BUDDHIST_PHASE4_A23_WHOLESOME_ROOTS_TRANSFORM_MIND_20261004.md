# PHASE 4 — A23 — WHOLESOME ROOTS TRANSFORM THE MIND

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: Why are non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion ethically central, and how do generosity, goodwill, and wisdom transform the mind rather than merely accumulate "merit points"?

## AN 3.34
- TEXT_ATTESTED: greed, hate, and delusion are sources from which deeds arise and ripen.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the opposite roots—non-greed/contentment, non-hate/love, and non-delusion/understanding—are presented as sources of deeds whose karmic seed is brought to an end as the corresponding defilement is fully abandoned.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: wholesome action is not merely externally correct behavior; its quality depends on the root from which it grows.

## Iti 26 / AN 6.25-26 generosity bridge
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Iti 26 links giving/sharing with freedom from the stain of stinginess.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: AN 6.25-26 says recollection of one's generosity—living open-handed and free of stinginess—leaves the mind not overwhelmed by greed, hate, and delusion and makes it steady/upright.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: generosity trains non-greed by loosening possessiveness and the "mine" reflex; its significance is therefore transformational, not merely bookkeeping of future reward.

## MN 21 / SN 45.8 / MN 19 goodwill bridge
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right Intention includes renunciation, goodwill/non-ill-will, and harmlessness.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 21 instructs maintaining a mind of goodwill even under harsh provocation rather than letting hate take over.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 19 says repeatedly thinking in terms of renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness inclines the mind in those directions, while sensuality, ill will, and cruelty incline it oppositely.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: goodwill is not merely a feeling one possesses; repeated non-hate responses reshape the habitual direction of the mind.

## Wisdom / non-delusion
- TEXT_ATTESTED: AN 3.34 makes understanding/non-delusion the opposite root to delusion.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the wider track has already audited MN 9 / SN 12.23 / SN 22.59, where understanding origin/cessation and truly knowing-and-seeing leads toward dispassion and liberation.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: wisdom transforms ethics because it changes what is seen as worth craving, defending, or identifying with. Non-delusion is not mere information possession.

## MN 140 deepening
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 140 describes the highest noble generosity as the letting go of all attachments and the highest noble peace as the pacification of greed, hate, and delusion.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this is a strong textual guardrail against reducing dāna or puñña to accumulation alone; the trajectory of the path is toward relinquishment.

## Merit (puñña) guardrail
- TEXT_ATTESTED: early texts do speak of merit, fortunate rebirth, and fruits of giving.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: these results are part of the teaching, but they are not the whole purpose of wholesome cultivation. The same texts place stinginess, greed, hate, and delusion at the center of the mind-training problem and ultimately aim at their ending.
- GUARDRAIL: do not replace canonical merit language with the modern slogan "merit points"; the metaphor can mislead by making merit sound like a transferable numerical currency stored in a self.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: in an old translation of a dilemma on virtue and vice, Nāgasena argues that wholesome action can generate non-remorse, gladness, joy, tranquility, pleasure, collectedness, and knowing things as they are.
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: the Nāgasena/chariot debate preserves merit, demerit, practice, and responsibility while refusing a permanent ego-substance.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Mi Tiên therefore helps stress-test two points at once: wholesome action can change the quality and stability of mind, and moral continuity does not require an immutable self.
- Guardrail: these are later dialectical formulations and should not overwrite early-text differences or be treated as the exact wording of the Buddha in the Nikāyas.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
Non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion are ethically central because they are roots that reshape the causal direction of the mind. Generosity weakens stinginess and mine-making; goodwill weakens hostility and retrains response under provocation; wisdom weakens delusion and the mis-seeing that feeds craving and clinging. Merit language remains legitimate, including beneficial results, but the deeper trajectory is transformation and relinquishment, culminating in the ending of greed, hate, and delusion rather than the accumulation of a permanent stock owned by a self.

## Guardrails
- Do not say generosity is valuable only because it produces a future reward.
- Do not say merit is meaningless; early texts explicitly affirm fruits of giving and skillful action.
- Do not equate temporary non-greed/non-hate/non-delusion with full arahant-level eradication.
- Do not turn goodwill into passivity; non-hate can coexist with firm corrective action.
- Do not treat wisdom as mere intellectual knowledge; in this track its mature function is de-appropriating and liberative.
