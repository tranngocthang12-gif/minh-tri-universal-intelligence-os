# PHASE 4 — A15 — FROM RIGHT VIEW TO YATHĀBHŪTA-ÑĀṆADASSANA

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: What distinguishes conceptually correct Right View from the kind of knowing-and-seeing that actually changes craving, clinging, and liberation?

## MN 9
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right View repeatedly takes the form of understanding a phenomenon, its origin, its cessation, and the path leading to cessation.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: in the suffering formula, Right View is connected with giving up greed/aversion tendencies, uprooting the view-and-conceit "I am", abandoning ignorance, and giving rise to knowledge.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Right View is not exhausted by verbal assent to doctrines; in its mature form it reorganizes how the process of suffering is understood and responded to.

## SN 12.23
- TEXT_ATTESTED: concentration conditions truly knowing-and-seeing; truly knowing-and-seeing conditions disenchantment; disenchantment conditions dispassion; dispassion conditions liberation.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this gives a transformation sequence, not merely a proposition sequence. The relevant knowing changes valuation and attachment.

## SN 22.59
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the aggregates are to be seen "as they really are" with right wisdom as "not mine, not I, not my self".
- TEXT_ATTESTED: seeing in this way leads to disenchantment, dispassion, liberation, and knowledge of liberation.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: yathābhūta seeing is operationally evidenced by the downstream weakening of appropriation.

## SN 22.89
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Khemaka no longer takes any aggregate as self or belonging to self, yet a residual "I am" conceit remains until further contemplation of arising and vanishing removes it.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: doctrinally correct non-self understanding can coexist with subtler residual selfing. This strongly distinguishes conceptual correctness from full transformation.

## MN 117
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right View guides the path by recognizing wrong as wrong and right as right; effort and mindfulness work around that discernment.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: conceptual orientation is indispensable, but the path is designed to mature that orientation into direct, de-appropriating knowledge.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Nāgasena characterizes wisdom as illuminating and cutting; the lamp simile depicts wisdom dispelling ignorance and making the truths evident.
- PROJECT_INTERPRETATION: this helps distinguish "having the right sentence" from "seeing in a way that changes the causal structure of greed, hatred, delusion, and clinging".
- Guardrail: Milindapañha sharpens the argument but remains a later dialectical layer.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
Right View begins as correct orientation and discrimination, but the path does not end at conceptual correctness. Through ethical discipline, effort, mindfulness, and concentration, understanding matures into yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana: seeing conditioned phenomena in a way that undermines "mine/I/self", produces disenchantment and dispassion, and culminates in liberation. A useful test is therefore not merely "can I state the doctrine correctly?" but "does this seeing reduce appropriation, craving, and conceit?"

## Guardrails
- Do not equate yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana with any vague mystical intuition detached from the Four Noble Truths/dependent process.
- Do not assume conceptual study is useless; MN 9 and MN 117 make correct orientation indispensable.
- Do not call every strong meditative experience liberating insight; SN 12.23 places knowing-and-seeing in a causal sequence that must lead onward to disenchantment and dispassion.
- SN 22.89 shows residual "I am" conceit can remain after coarse self-view has been abandoned.
