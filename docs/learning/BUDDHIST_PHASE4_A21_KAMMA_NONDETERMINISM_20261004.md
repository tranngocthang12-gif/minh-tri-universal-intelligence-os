# PHASE 4 — A21 — KAMMA WITHOUT FATALISM

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: How does intention/kamma condition consciousness and future experience without becoming deterministic fate?

## AN 6.63
- TEXT_ATTESTED: "Intention, I tell you, is kamma"; intending, one acts through body, speech, and mind.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: kamma has results that can be experienced in this life, later in this life, or after that; the Noble Eightfold Path is given as the path to the cessation of kamma.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: kamma is centrally volitional and causally potent, but the discourse itself includes cessation; this is not a closed deterministic machine.

## SN 12.38 / SN 12.40
- TEXT_ATTESTED: what one intends, plans, and has underlying tendencies toward can provide a support for consciousness to become established and grow, leading to future renewed existence/rebirth.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: when intention, planning, and underlying tendencies do not provide that support, consciousness is not established in that way and future renewed existence does not arise.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: present volitional structure influences future continuity, but present transformation can alter the causal trajectory.

## SN 12.51
- TEXT_ATTESTED: ignorance conditions the generation of meritorious, demeritorious, or imperturbable formations; with ignorance abandoned and knowledge arisen, this mode of fabrication ceases.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the same system that explains karmic continuity also contains an exit condition; therefore it is not strict fatalism.

## SN 36.21 — important anti-fatalism correction/addition
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the Buddha explicitly rejects the view that every pleasant, painful, or neutral feeling is caused solely by past deeds.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the discourse names multiple sources of feeling, including bodily disorders, weather/change, lack of care, exertion, and past kamma.
- LOCKED CORRECTION: use SN 36.21 as the primary early-text guardrail against the claim "everything that happens to me is because of past kamma".
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: kamma is one causal stream among multiple conditions in lived experience; it is not an all-purpose explanation for every event.

## MN 57
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the discourse distinguishes dark kamma with dark result, bright kamma with bright result, mixed kamma with mixed result, and a kind of action that leads to the exhaustion of kamma.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: actions of body, speech, and mind can condition corresponding rebirth and feeling.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Buddhist ethics is not "good kamma forever"; the path ultimately aims beyond continued karmic production.

## SN 36.6 bridge
- TEXT_ATTESTED: an ordinary person and a learned noble disciple can experience the same painful bodily feeling but respond differently; the latter does not add the second mental arrow and does not feed the same greed/aversion/ignorance tendencies.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: even when a present feeling has arisen, present response is not mechanically fixed. New intention matters.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
Early Buddhist kamma is neither random nor fatalistic. Intentional action has causal consequences and can shape future consciousness, becoming, and rebirth. But not every present experience is the result of past kamma, and the causal field includes current conditions and current responses. Most importantly, ignorance and intention are themselves transformable through the path. The future is therefore conditioned, not predetermined.

## Practical reasoning model
PROJECT_INTERPRETATION:
- past kamma: inherited causal input;
- present conditions: body, environment, contact, social conditions, etc.;
- present feeling: current experienced result;
- present intention/response: new causal input;
- path/wisdom: changes the quality of present intention and can end karmic fabrication.

This is a study model, not a single canonical five-part formula.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: continuity analogies help explain how karmic consequence can continue without a permanent soul.
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: responsibility is preserved by causal continuity, while numerical identity of a permanent substance is not required.
- Guardrail: Mi Tiên clarifies continuity and responsibility; it should not be used to reintroduce a deterministic karmic ledger.

## Guardrails
- Do not say every illness, accident, poverty, or pain is the result of past kamma.
- Do not say kamma is irrelevant; AN 6.63 and the dependent-origination passages explicitly assign intention causal force.
- Do not equate "conditioned" with "predetermined".
- Do not turn liberation into accumulation of only good kamma; MN 57 includes a path to the exhaustion of kamma.
