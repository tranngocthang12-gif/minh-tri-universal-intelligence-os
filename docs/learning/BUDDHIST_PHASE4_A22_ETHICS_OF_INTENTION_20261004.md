# PHASE 4 — A22 — WHAT MAKES INTENTION ETHICALLY SKILLFUL OR UNSKILLFUL?

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: If kamma is intention, what makes an intention ethically skillful or unskillful—motive, act, anticipated/actual consequence, or a combination?

## AN 6.63
- TEXT_ATTESTED: intention (cetanā) is identified as kamma; intending, one acts by body, speech, and mind.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: intention is central to karmic agency, but AN 6.63 alone does not provide the full practical decision procedure for ethical evaluation.

## MN 61 — before, during, after
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Rāhula is instructed to examine bodily, verbal, and mental actions before acting, while acting, and after acting.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: a deed leading to harm/affliction for oneself, others, or both is classed as unskillful and connected with painful results; if anticipated, it should not be done; if discovered during action, it should be stopped; if recognized afterward, it should be disclosed/corrected and restraint cultivated.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: if the act does not lead to such harm and is skillful with pleasant results, it may be undertaken/continued and remembered with joy while training further.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: a good motive by itself does not exhaust ethical reflection; the practitioner must also test anticipated and actual consequences and revise behavior when harm becomes evident.

## MN 19
- TEXT_ATTESTED: thoughts of sensuality, ill will, and cruelty are grouped together; renunciation, non-ill-will, and harmlessness are grouped together.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the Buddha evaluates the first group because it leads to one's own affliction, others' affliction, or both, obstructs discernment, causes distress, and does not lead to Nibbāna.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: repeatedly thinking along a pattern inclines the mind in that direction.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: ethical quality involves both immediate motive and the trajectory of mind that repeated intention cultivates.

## AN 3.34
- TEXT_ATTESTED: greed, hate, and delusion are sources for the arising of kamma; non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion are corresponding wholesome roots.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the root/motive matters because it shapes the causal quality of the act, not merely the external behavior.

## SN 45.8
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right Intention/Right Purpose is renunciation, non-ill-will/goodwill, and harmlessness.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this gives a positive ethical orientation, not only a prohibition list.

## MN 21
- TEXT_ATTESTED: even under harsh or false speech from others, the disciple is instructed not to let the mind be overcome, not to speak evil words, and to maintain goodwill without inner hate.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: external provocation does not by itself determine the moral quality of one's response; the internal root and chosen response remain ethically relevant.

## Integrated ethical model
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
No single early passage among these gives a one-line formula "ethical value = motive + act + result." Taken together, however, they support a multi-dimensional discipline:
1. ROOT/MOTIVE: greed-hate-delusion versus non-greed-non-hate-non-delusion;
2. DIRECTION: renunciation, goodwill, harmlessness versus sensuality, ill will, cruelty;
3. FORESEEABLE HARM: examine likely harm to self, others, both before acting;
4. REAL-TIME FEEDBACK: stop when harm becomes evident during action;
5. ACTUAL RESULT AND REVIEW: examine consequences afterward, acknowledge error, restrain in future;
6. MENTAL TRAINING EFFECT: repeated intentions incline and train the mind.

This is a cross-text synthesis, not a verbatim canonical checklist.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Mil 3.5.8 says deeds cannot be pointed to as a substance stored somewhere, yet they remain causally connected with the continuity of name-and-form; the fruit/tree analogy is used to explain this.
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: rebirth/continuity examples preserve responsibility without an immutable self-substance.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: moral responsibility in this framework depends on causal continuity and intentional action, not on a permanent metaphysical agent.
- Guardrail: Mi Tiên clarifies continuity and responsibility; it does not replace MN 61's practical before/during/after ethical examination.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
Early Buddhist ethics is neither pure intentionism nor pure outcome-consequentialism. Intention is central to kamma, but ethical training also evaluates the roots of intention, foreseeable and actual harm, the effect on self/others, the direction of the mind, and whether the act supports or obstructs wisdom and liberation. A "good intention" therefore does not end the inquiry: one must keep examining the action while it unfolds and learn from its actual results.

## Guardrails
- Do not claim accidental harm and intentional harm have identical karmic status.
- Do not claim good motives automatically make a harmful act skillful.
- Do not reduce ethics to outcomes alone; AN 6.63 and AN 3.34 preserve intention/root as central.
- Do not reduce ethics to private motive alone; MN 61 requires examination of harm and result.
- Do not identify "pleasant result" with mere short-term pleasure; MN 19 evaluates whether a thought obstructs wisdom and leads away from Nibbāna.
