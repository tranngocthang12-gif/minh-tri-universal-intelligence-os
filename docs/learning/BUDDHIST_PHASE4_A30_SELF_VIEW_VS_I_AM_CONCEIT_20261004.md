# PHASE 4 — A30 — SELF-VIEW VS RESIDUAL "I AM" CONCEIT

**Status:** CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Question

Why can a practitioner stop identifying any aggregate as self or belonging to self and still retain a subtle residual sense of "I am"?

## Primary-text findings

### SN 22.89 — Khemaka
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: Khemaka says that regarding the five aggregates subject to clinging, he takes nothing as self or belonging to self, yet he is not yet an arahant.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: he says the residual "I am" has not yet been overcome even though he does not take any aggregate as "I am this."
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the residual is described as an "I am" conceit, an "I am" desire, and an underlying tendency.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: contemplating the arising and passing away of the five aggregates leads to the uprooting of this residual "I am."
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the discourse uses the analogies of the scent of a flower and the lingering smell on a cleaned cloth to show that the residual sense cannot be localized as one aggregate yet can remain as a subtle trace.
- `AUDIT_PROGRESS`: this is a bounded SN 22.89 audit slice and does not close the wider SN 22 mapping backlog.

### SN 22.59
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are not fit to be regarded as "mine / I am this / my self."
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: this explicitly targets appropriation and identification with the aggregates, while SN 22.89 shows that removing explicit identification need not immediately uproot every residual "I am" tendency.

### SN 22.91 / SN 22.102 support
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: related passages connect seeing the aggregates as "not mine, not I, not my self" with the ending of I-making, mine-making, and the underlying tendency to conceit.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: perception of impermanence is said to uproot the conceit "I am."
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: the residual conceit is treated not merely as a proposition to refute but as a deeply conditioned tendency weakened by repeated seeing of arising and passing away.

### MN 22 — raft and view-grasping
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: even correct teaching is a raft for crossing and is not to be held onto as an object of grasping.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: a practitioner can abandon crude self-view yet build a subtler identity around doctrine, attainment, or being "one who understands." This is a diagnostic application of the raft principle, not a verbatim statement from MN 22.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer

### Paññattipañha — chariot/person
- `MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC`: Nāgasena explains that "Nāgasena" is a conventional designation dependent on bodily factors and the aggregates, comparable to "chariot" as a designation dependent on its parts.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: conventional person-language can remain without requiring an immutable ego-substance. The problem addressed by SN 22.89 is subtler: even after explicit self-theory has loosened, the felt/reference tendency "I am" can linger.

## Main conclusion

`CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`:
Self-view and the conceit "I am" are not identical. Self-view is an explicit or implicit taking of phenomena as self or belonging to self. The residual "I am" conceit is a subtler self-referential tendency that can persist even when the practitioner no longer locates a self in any aggregate. It is uprooted not merely by adopting the proposition "there is no self," but by repeatedly seeing the aggregates as conditioned, arising, and passing away until the residual I-making loses its basis.

## Important distinction

- explicit self-view: "this aggregate is me / mine / my self";
- residual I-am conceit: a pre-reflective or habitual sense of "I am" that is not pinned to one aggregate;
- conventional designation: ordinary use of person-names and first-person language without committing to an immutable essence.

This three-way distinction is a cross-text analytic model, not a single canonical formula.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate intellectual acceptance of anattā with full uprooting of conceit.
- Do not infer that ordinary first-person grammar itself proves self-view.
- Do not interpret the chariot simile as denying practical persons, responsibility, or conventional speech.
- Do not claim SN 22.89 teaches a hidden true self behind the aggregates; the discourse explicitly refuses to locate "I am" in or apart from any aggregate.
- Do not mark the Aṭṭhakavagga audit complete; its lexical pass remains pending.
