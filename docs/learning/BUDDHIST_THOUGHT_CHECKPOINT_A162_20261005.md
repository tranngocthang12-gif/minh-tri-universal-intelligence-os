## Central question

How does early Buddhist material distinguish simple perception/recognition from conceiving, self-positioning, comparison, and proliferative conflict?

The key result of A162 is negative as well as positive:

- `saññā` is not itself equivalent to ego-making, falsehood, memory, or `papañca`;
- `maññati` is context-sensitive and can mean think/deem/conceive, but in MN 1 and Aṭṭhakavagga it can explicitly participate in appropriation and rank-based self-positioning;
- `papañca` is not all thought;
- the combined model is useful only as CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS, not as one verbatim canonical chain.

## LEXICAL / MORPHOLOGY CONTROL

### 1. `saññā`

- `saññā`: noun, commonly translated perception/recognition.
- `saññaṃ`: accusative singular form.
- `saññāya`: oblique singular form in contexts such as Snp 4.12.
- `sañjānāti`: finite verb, "perceives / recognizes".
- `saññatvā`: absolutive/gerundial form, "having perceived/recognized", as used in MN 1.

Guardrail: derivational relation does not license treating every occurrence of noun and verb as carrying one identical philosophical sense.

### 2. `maññati`

- `maññati`: 3rd singular present, contextually think/deem/conceive.
- `maññeyya`: optative, "should think/conceive".
- forms such as `maññāmahaṃ` show that the verb can also carry ordinary "I think/suppose" force.

Guardrail: `maññati` must not be translated as "ego-conceiving" in every occurrence. Context determines whether ordinary judgment, appropriation, comparison, or self-positioning is present.

### 3. `papañca`

- MN 18 uses the verb `papañceti` after perception and thought.
- MN 18 also uses `papañcasaññāsaṅkhā`; exact internal translation is difficult and must remain guarded.
- Snp 4.11 and Snp 4.14 use `papañcasaṅkhā`; exact compound semantics should not be silently fixed by a later commentary or a modern single-word gloss.

Guardrail: `papañca` is not safely reducible to "overthinking", "conceptual thought", or "all thinking".

## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. SN 22.79 — ordinary function of saññā

SN 22.79 asks why it is called perception and answers through the function of perceiving/recognizing, using colors such as blue, yellow, red, and white.

This gives a strong guardrail:

**ordinary recognition is already a text-attested function of saññā.**

Therefore A162 rejects the equation:

`saññā = selfing`.

The aggregate of perception can participate in later grasping or proliferation, but the early text does not define perception itself as ego-making.

### 2. MN 1 — perceiving and conceiving are textually distinguishable

For the uninstructed worldling, MN 1 repeatedly gives a pattern such as:

- perceives X as X (`sañjānāti`);
- having perceived X (`saññatvā`);
- conceives/thinks X in several ways (`maññati`);
- includes an explicit "X is mine" construction;
- delights in X.

For the trainee/arahant/Tathāgata, the discourse shifts to direct knowledge (`abhijānāti` / `abhiññāya`) and prohibits or negates the corresponding `maññati` constructions.

Text-attested result:

**MN 1 does not collapse perception and conceiving into one mental act.**

It also gives direct early evidence that conceiving can take a possessive form: "mine".

### 3. MN 18 — perception, thought, and papañca are distinct stages in the stated sequence

MN 18 gives:

contact -> feeling -> perception -> thinking -> papañca,

followed by being assailed by the categories/perceptions associated with papañca across past, future, and present sense experience.

Therefore:

- perception is not identical to thought;
- thought is not identical to papañca;
- papañca is not all cognition.

This preserves the correction already made in A144.

### 4. Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya — comparative self-positioning and dispute

Snp 4.9 states that one who `maññati` in terms of being equal, superior, or inferior would dispute on that basis, while the one not shaken by those three comparisons does not take up those positions.

This is unusually direct evidence for a link between:

- comparative self-positioning;
- `maññati`;
- dispute.

The same discourse also says that those who grasp `saññā` and views go about clashing in the world.

Guardrail:

The text criticizes grasping/attachment and comparative positioning; it does not state that every occurrence of perception causes dispute.

### 5. Snp 4.11 Kalahavivāda — saññā and papañcasaṅkhā are linked, but the verse is difficult

Snp 4.11 contains the phrase:

`saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`.

This textually links `saññā` as a condition/source with `papañcasaṅkhā`.

However the surrounding stanza also uses difficult expressions such as `na saññasaññī`, `na visaññasaññī`, `nopi asaññī`, and `na vibhūtasaññī`.

A162 therefore records only the bounded claim:

**the discourse links saññā with papañcasaṅkhā; the exact phenomenological and lexical interpretation remains OPEN.**

It is not used to redefine every perception as papañca.

### 6. Snp 4.14 Tuvaṭaka — papañcasaṅkhā, "asmīti", and rank-comparison

Snp 4.14 places `papañcasaṅkhā` next to a difficult `asmīti` phrase and immediately continues with training against internal craving.

It then says one should not, because of what is known, `maññeyya` oneself superior, inferior, or equal.

Text-attested result:

- the discourse directly opposes rank-based self-positioning;
- `maññeyya` is explicitly used in that comparison context;
- `papañcasaṅkhā` is connected in the stanza with an `asmīti` expression.

Guardrail:

The exact syntax/translation of `mantā asmīti` is disputed. A162 does not force one English rendering such as "I am the thinker" or "the idea I am" into canonical status.

### 7. Snp 4.12 Cūḷabyūha — saññā appears in a truth-claim/dispute context

Snp 4.12 says that disputants make their own views into truth and includes the phrase `aññatra saññāya ...` in discussing the plurality of supposedly eternal truths.

This is important for A163, but A162 keeps it lexical-only:

`saññā` here occurs in a truth-claim/dispute context and cannot simply be assumed to mean the same thing as color-recognition in SN 22.79 without contextual analysis.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Perception is not yet self-positioning

The most defensible combined reading is:

1. experience is perceived/recognized;
2. additional conceiving can occur around it;
3. conceiving can become possessive ("mine") or comparative ("equal / superior / inferior");
4. thought may further proliferate into `papañca`;
5. grasped perceptions/views and rank-positioning can contribute to conflict.

This is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS.

No single cited discourse gives this entire five-part chain.

### B. Three different self-positioning modes should be separated

A162 distinguishes at least three forms:

1. **possessive appropriation** — "X is mine" in MN 1;
2. **comparative positioning** — equal/superior/inferior in Snp 4.9 and 4.14;
3. **"I am" residual/existential positioning** — `asmīti` material, with SN 22.89 as an earlier checkpoint guardrail.

These are related for study but are not declared synonyms.

### C. The problem is not perception but appropriation and fixation

SN 22.79 prevents the project from demonizing perception.

The practical danger becomes clearer when:

- perception is taken as final truth rather than a conditioned recognition;
- `maññati` adds ownership, identity, rank, or speculative positioning;
- thought expands into papañca;
- views/perceptions are grasped and defended.

This formulation is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS.

### D. Papañca and self-positioning are related but not lexically identical

MN 18 gives papañca after thinking.
MN 1 gives `maññati` constructions without using papañca in that passage.
Snp 4.9 gives comparison/dispute through `maññati`.
Snp 4.11 and 4.14 connect `saññā` / `asmīti` with `papañcasaṅkhā`.

Therefore:

**self-positioning can feed the same conflict field as papañca, but `maññati`, `māna`, `asmīti`, `saññā`, and `papañca` must remain lexically distinct.**

## Practical diagnostic

When a conflict-story starts forming, ask:

1. **What was actually perceived?**  
   Separate the observed/recognized feature from later interpretation.

2. **What did the mind add through `maññati`?**  
   Did it become "mine", "about me", "because of me", or a claim about what the thing really is?

3. **Did comparison enter?**  
   Better, worse, equal; respected, humiliated, superior, inferior.

4. **Did thought become `papañca`?**  
   Is it now multiplying categories, imagined motives, past/future scenarios, and conflict positions?

5. **Am I grasping a perception or view as identity/truth-possession?**

6. **Can the event be known more directly without adding ownership, rank, or a defended standpoint?**

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION, not a canonical six-step list.
