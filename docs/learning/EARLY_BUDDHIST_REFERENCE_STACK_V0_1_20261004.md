# EARLY BUDDHIST REFERENCE STACK v0.1 — 2026-10-04

**Purpose:** Give MINH TRI a durable source hierarchy for understanding early Buddhist thought, translation, dialogue method, and later explanatory literature without collapsing different historical layers.

## 1. Primary early-discourse layer

### Pali Nikāyas / SuttaCentral
Use for:
- Pali text;
- English comparison;
- Vietnamese where available;
- parallel indexing.

Core collections:
- DN, MN, SN, AN;
- early Khuddaka materials, especially Sutta Nipāta / Aṭṭhakavagga, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Dhammapada, Theragāthā, Therīgāthā.

Primary web:
- https://suttacentral.net/

### Vietnamese Nikāya base
Primary Vietnamese witness for the four Nikāyas where available:
- HT Thích Minh Châu.

Rule:
Vietnamese translation is a serious witness, not the original. High-risk terms always return to Pali and at least one English comparison.

## 2. Chinese Āgama / cross-traditional layer

Use to test whether:
- a reasoning core survives outside the Pali transmission;
- a technical formula may be school/redaction specific;
- narrative or doctrinal details vary.

Priority comparative resources:
- SuttaCentral parallel metadata;
- Bhikkhu Anālayo's Āgama comparative studies;
- Journal of the Oxford Centre for Buddhist Studies;
- BDK English Tripiṭaka Chinese translations where relevant.

Method:
Pali claim -> Chinese parallel -> compare function before wording -> preserve divergence.

## 3. English translation comparison layer

Use:
- Bhikkhu Sujato for broad SuttaCentral coverage;
- Bhikkhu Bodhi for major Nikāya translations;
- Thanissaro Bhikkhu for independent renderings and interpretive notes;
- PTS/Rhys Davids historical translations where useful.

Rule:
A translation disagreement triggers:
1. Pali grammar/context;
2. parallel passage;
3. discourse function;
4. multiple-candidate meaning if still unresolved.

## 4. Milindapañha / Mi Tiên Vấn Đáp — explanatory dialogue layer

Historical role:
- a later Buddhist dialogue text, not a primary record of the historical Buddha's spoken discourses;
- normally treated as extracanonical in much of Theravāda, while Myanmar accepts it within the Khuddakanikāya;
- extant Pali text has compositional strata;
- Chinese Nāgasena material provides an important comparative witness.

Therefore MINH TRI classifies it:
**LATER_EXPLANATORY_REASONING_LAB**
not
**EARLY_BUDDHA_DIRECT_WITNESS**.

### Pali / English / Vietnamese digital witness
SuttaCentral:
- Pali Milindapañha;
- T. W. Rhys Davids English;
- Bhikkhu Indacanda Vietnamese, titled "Milinda Vấn Đạo".

Start:
https://suttacentral.net/mil1/vi/indacanda

### Vietnamese Mi Tiên Vấn Đáp
Useful Vietnamese reading witness:
- HT Giới Nghiêm translation;
- Tỳ kheo Giới Đức revision, 2003.

Public reading copies exist through Vietnamese Buddhist libraries such as:
- dieungu.org;
- thuvienphatgiao.com;
- thuvienhoasen.org.

Use only as translation/explanatory witness; historical claims in introductions require independent checking.

### Chinese counterpart
BDK English Tripiṭaka:
**The Scripture on the Monk Nāgasena**, Chinese counterpart to the Milindapañha, translated by Bhikkhu Anālayo (2021).

Use to distinguish:
- earlier/shared Nāgasena dialogue core;
- Pali expansions/local formulations.

BDK:
https://www.bdkamerica.org/product/analysis-of-the-middle-and-extremes-the-scripture-on-the-monk-nagasena/

## 5. Why Milindapañha is useful for the Owner's goal

Owner's goal is not only "what doctrines exist?" but:
**how Buddhist reasoning answers hard questions.**

Milindapañha is unusually useful for:
- apparent contradiction resolution;
- analogy/simile construction;
- conventional vs analytic language;
- continuity without permanent identity;
- question classification;
- adversarial dialogue;
- testing whether a doctrine has been over-literalized.

But every inference about "how the Buddha himself thought" must be back-checked against:
- Nikāya;
- Āgama;
- early verse material.

## 6. Secondary scholarly layer

Use for historical-critical boundaries, not as canonical authority:
- Peter Harvey, *An Introduction to Buddhism*;
- Bhikkhu Anālayo, comparative Āgama/Vinaya publications;
- scholarly work on Chinese Nāgasena parallels and Milindapañha textual strata;
- BDK critical translation apparatus.

## 7. Source weighting rule

For reconstructing historical Buddha-thought:

1. EARLY_MULTI_PARALLEL_HIGH_CONFIDENCE
2. EARLY_PALI_STRONG
3. EARLY_BUT_VARIANT
4. LATER_EXPLANATORY_EARLY-COMPATIBLE
5. LATER_CANONICAL / SCHOOL_SYSTEMATIZATION
6. COMMENTARIAL
7. MODERN_INTERPRETATION

Milindapañha usually begins at level 4 unless a particular teaching is independently supported by earlier parallel material.

## 8. Prohibition

Do not cite Nāgasena as:
"the Buddha said..."

Correct form:
"Milindapañha/Nāgasena explains..."
then separately show whether early discourses support the same reasoning.
