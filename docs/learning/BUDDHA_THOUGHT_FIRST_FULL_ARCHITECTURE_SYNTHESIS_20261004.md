## Purpose

Integrate the first-pass architecture of early Buddhist thought across:

DUKKHA → PERCEPTION → SELFING → CRAVING → DEPENDENT ARISING → KNOWLEDGE → PRACTICE → LIBERATION

This document is a synthesis of prior source-level passes. It is not a claim that any one sutta states the whole architecture in this form.

## 1. DUKKHA — the problem is conditioned and diagnosable

The Buddha’s method repeatedly treats dukkha not as an abstract metaphysical evil but as a conditionally arisen problem to be understood in terms of:
- what it is,
- what gives rise to it,
- what its cessation is,
- what path leads to cessation.

The practical consequence is that suffering is approached causally rather than fatalistically.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG.

## 2. PERCEPTION — experience is processed, not simply “received”

Early texts distinguish multiple functions:
- sense base/object,
- consciousness,
- contact,
- feeling,
- perception,
- thought,
- papañca-related expansion.

MN 18 and its parallels support a cognitive expansion from contact/feeling through perception and thought toward papañca. MN 38 and its parallel reject the idea of one self-identical consciousness transmigrating unchanged.

Saññā itself is not inherently delusive; wholesome perceptions can be cultivated.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG for conditioned consciousness and non-equation of saññā with delusion; EBT_PROBABLE for the exact semantic core of papañca.

## 3. SELFING — “self” is better studied as a family of processes

The strongest current model is not “there is one self-making event,” but overlapping processes:
- maññanā / construing,
- ahaṅkāra / I-making,
- mamaṅkāra or mamatta / mine-making,
- māna / comparison and self-positioning,
- diṭṭhi / views,
- upādāna / grasping,
- asmimāna / residual “I am” conceit,
- papañca / proliferative self-world construction.

SN 22.89 shows that explicit self-view can weaken while residual “I am” remains.

SN 35.248 shows self-referential content can be simultaneously described in terms of conceiving, proliferation and conceit.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG that self-view and residual “I am” are not identical; EBT_PROBABLE that selfing is best modeled as a recursive network.

## 4. CRAVING — feeling does not mechanically force craving

The standard formula vedanā → taṇhā is a conditional relation, not a one-to-one mechanical necessity.

SN 36.6 shows:
- painful feeling can occur without the second mental arrow in a trained disciple;
- untrained reaction can add sorrow, resistance and compensatory sensual seeking.

MN 44 shows not every pleasant feeling is accompanied by passion, nor every painful feeling by aversion, nor every neutral feeling by ignorance.

Therefore liberation does not require eliminating feeling; it transforms reactivity.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG.

## 5. DEPENDENT ARISING — process logic without a permanent carrier

Dependent arising explains continuity by conditions rather than by a permanent self-substance.

Important guardrails:
- do not collapse the twelvefold formula into only a momentary psychology;
- do not reduce it to only a three-life scholastic scheme;
- do not splice every cognitive sequence into one universal chain.

SN 12 also preserves reciprocal relations such as consciousness ↔ name-and-form, showing that not all early-Buddhist causal analysis is linear.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG that conditioned continuity does not require an invariant self; OPEN on one unified model that explains every application without later systematization.

## 6. KNOWLEDGE — liberating knowledge is causal, practical and transformative

Right view is not merely “holding the correct doctrine.”

MN 9 repeatedly uses a diagnostic structure:
phenomenon/problem → origin → cessation → path.

MN 117 presents right view as a path-leading factor working with right effort and right mindfulness.

SN 22.89 shows propositional correction is insufficient; the residual “I am” tendency is removed through sustained seeing of arising and disappearance.

SN 12.23 embeds knowing-and-seeing in cultivated conditions leading through disenchantment and dispassion to release.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG that liberating knowledge is process-oriented and tied to cultivation; OPEN on exact historical status of some Pāli-specific technical taxonomies.

## 7. PRACTICE — the path is coordinated and adaptive

The path is neither:
- a rigid eight-step ladder,
nor
- an unordered list.

The best current synthesis is a feedback cultivation system:
- teaching/good friendship supplies orientation;
- yoniso manasikāra frames the right causal question;
- right view diagnoses;
- right effort adjusts;
- right mindfulness retains and monitors the task;
- ethics and sense restraint shape the field;
- concentration stabilizes;
- investigation and awakening factors balance the current state;
- knowing-and-seeing matures into disenchantment, dispassion and release.

SN 46 balancing material suggests practice is adaptive: sluggish and agitated minds require different emphases.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG for path-factor interdependence and conditioned cultivation; EBT_PROBABLE for the modern “feedback system” model.

## 8. LIBERATION — Nibbāna is safest understood first by function, then by contrast

The strongest early-text level is functional:
- destruction of greed,
- hatred,
- delusion,
- ending of craving,
- ending of clinging and renewed becoming.

SN 43 groups Nibbāna with terms such as:
- asaṅkhata / unconditioned,
- amata / deathless,
- taṇhakkhaya / ending of craving,
- virāga / dispassion,
- vimutti / release.

Udāna 8.3 uses unborn/unbecome/unmade/unconditioned language.

SN 44 refuses ordinary postmortem predicates:
exists / does not exist / both / neither.

Therefore:
- Nibbāna is not adequately described as a place;
- not adequately described as annihilation;
- not adequately described as an eternal personal soul;
- not merely a temporary calm state.

**Confidence:** EBT_STRONG for functional cessation language and limits on ordinary postmortem predicates; OPEN for a complete ontology of the unconditioned.

## 9. EPISTEMOLOGY OF NIBBĀNA

The current three-layer model:

1. transmitted/conceptual understanding,
2. practice-based verification of causal mechanisms,
3. direct realization.

MN 26 and its parallel support direct realization as the goal of the noble search.

SN 43 presents conditioned paths leading to the unconditioned.

Milindapañha later clarifies:
- conditions may lead to realization without producing Nibbāna itself;
- indirect knowledge through testimony/analogy is possible;
- direct realization remains stronger.

**Status:** EBT_STRONG for direct-realization orientation; MILINDA_COMPATIBLE for the explicit three-level clarification.

## 10. MI TIÊN VẤN ĐÁP — ROLE IN THE ARCHITECTURE

Use Milindapañha as:
- clarification,
- logical stress-test,
- later systematic explanation.

Do not use it as:
- automatic proof of historical Buddha wording,
- substitute for Nikāya/Āgama parallels,
- permission to import a later fixed ontology into flexible early-text analyses.

Current strong-compatible contributions:
- chariot / conventional designation,
- causal continuity without strict identity,
- body pain without mental agony,
- sense experience without lust,
- no fixed spatial location for Nibbāna,
- path conditions realization rather than producing the unconditioned,
- distinction between attention and wisdom.

## 11. FIRST-PASS MASTER SYNTHESIS

A working, non-canonical summary:

Experience arises through conditions.
Feeling does not itself imprison; craving, resistance and ignorance can develop around it.
Perception and thought can expand into papañca.
Craving, grasping, mine-making, conceit, views and self-construal can reinforce one another.
This produces defensive identity, conflict, becoming and continued suffering.

The path intervenes not by destroying cognition, feeling or conventional personhood, but by:
- framing experience causally,
- discriminating skillful/unskillful,
- restraining and training behavior,
- stabilizing attention,
- seeing arising and passing,
- weakening appropriation,
- ending craving and clinging.

Liberation is not the construction of a perfected self.
It is the ending of the conditions that require a self to be continually built, defended and carried forward.

This paragraph is a SYNTHESIS, not a quotation or single-sutta doctrine.

## 13. WHAT IS NOW STRONG ENOUGH TO CARRY FORWARD

1. Buddha’s method is dominantly causal/process-oriented.
2. No permanent invariant self is needed to explain continuity.
3. “No-self” is not adequately reduced to one metaphysical slogan.
4. Selfing is multi-layered and can persist after explicit self-view weakens.
5. Feeling is not identical with craving; reactivity can be trained.
6. Karma is not total fatalism.
7. Right view is necessary but view-clinging is still grasping.
8. Liberating knowledge is cultivated, not merely asserted.
9. The path is coordinated, ethical, cognitive, affective and contemplative.
10. Nibbāna is strongly defined by cessation of defilements and craving, while complete metaphysical characterization remains limited.
