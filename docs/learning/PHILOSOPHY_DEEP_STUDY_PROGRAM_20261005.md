## Objective

Build a deep, connected, historically aware understanding of philosophy rather than a collection of quotations or isolated schools.

The track must learn:
- major philosophical problems and why they arise;
- competing positions and their strongest arguments;
- conceptual distinctions and hidden assumptions;
- historical development and cross-tradition contrasts;
- objections, counterexamples, revisions, and unresolved questions;
- how philosophical reasoning transfers into later learning and practical judgment.

## Method

For each material unit:

SOURCE / TEXT / HISTORICAL CONTEXT
-> CLAIM / ARGUMENT
-> CONCEPTUAL MAP
-> STRONGEST COUNTERARGUMENT
-> SELF-CRITIQUE
-> CROSS-LINK TO PRIOR KNOWLEDGE
-> DURABLE MEMORY CUES
-> APPLICATION / TRANSFER
-> OPEN QUESTIONS / NEXT UNIT

Do not collapse disagreement into artificial harmony.
