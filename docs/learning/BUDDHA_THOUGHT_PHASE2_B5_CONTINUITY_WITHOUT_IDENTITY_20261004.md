## Scope discipline

This pass studies rebirth strictly from the internal logic of early Buddhist thought.

Modern science is **not** used as the framework for deciding whether rebirth is true or false. Historical/philological research is used only to clarify texts, parallels, layers and terminology.

Primary question:
> What is the smallest rebirth model we can defend from early Buddhist texts without importing either an eternal self or later scholastic machinery?

## 1. First boundary: the Buddha rejects the “same thing travels” model

MN 38 explicitly rejects Sāti’s claim that the very same consciousness runs and wanders on from life to life.

The discourse insists:
- consciousness arises dependent on conditions;
- consciousness is named according to its condition;
- it is not an invariant speaker/knower that carries karmic results unchanged.

Therefore the minimal EBT rebirth model cannot contain:
- an eternal ātman,
- a permanent witness,
- numerically identical consciousness moving from body to body.

**Confidence: EBT_STRONG.**

## 2. Second boundary: early Buddhism does not thereby reject rebirth

The same early corpus repeatedly speaks of:
- future renewed becoming,
- future birth,
- consciousness descending in conception,
- kamma ripening in different domains,
- beings passing away and reappearing.

SN 12.38 says intention, planning and latent tendency can provide a support for the establishment of consciousness; when consciousness becomes established and grows, there is renewed becoming in the future, then future birth, aging and death.

AN 3.77 presents:
- kamma as field,
- consciousness as seed,
- craving as moisture,
and says this leads to production of renewed becoming in the future.

DN 15 connects consciousness and nāmarūpa with conception and maturation in the womb.

Therefore:
**REBIRTH / RENEWED EXISTENCE IS EBT_STRONG.**

The question is not whether rebirth appears in early Buddhist thought, but how continuity is explained without an invariant carrier.

## 3. Third boundary: viññāṇa and nāmarūpa are mutually dependent

SN 12.67’s reed-sheaf simile says:
- consciousness conditions name-and-form,
- name-and-form conditions consciousness.

DN 15 uses the same reciprocity in both:
- conception/development,
- ongoing experiential contact.

This blocks both:
- “consciousness exists independently first and enters matter,”
and
- “matter alone is first and produces consciousness as a self-sufficient origin.”

The early structure is relational and conditional.

**Confidence: EBT_STRONG / cross-tradition strong at structural level.**

## 4. Fourth boundary: ethical continuity is carried by causation, not ownership

AN 6.63 centers kamma on cetanā/intention.

SN 12.38 says present intention/tendency affects whether consciousness obtains a basis for future renewed existence.

SN 12.52 uses the image of feeding a fire:
when gratification/craving is fed, grasping and becoming continue; when not fed, they cease.

Thus responsibility does not require a permanent owner who carries a karmic account.

Minimal early model:
- intention conditions action,
- action participates in the causal stream,
- craving and tendencies condition future establishment,
- future states arise dependent on previous conditions.

This is **causal inheritance without immutable ownership**.

This phrase is PROJECT SYNTHESIS, not a canonical quotation.

## 5. The Buddha explicitly rejects “same experiencer” vs “different experiencer”

SN 12.17 and SN 12.46 are decisive for the philosophical framing.

The alternatives:
- “the one who acts is exactly the same one who experiences,”
- “the one who acts is one person and the experiencer another,”

are treated as two extremes.

The Buddha responds through dependent arising rather than choosing either identity proposition.

This is extremely important for the project:
the early teaching does not solve karmic continuity by asserting strict sameness, and does not solve anattā by asserting total disconnected difference.

The middle explanation is conditional arising.

## 6. Bhava and jāti retain real rebirth meaning

SN 12.2 and AN 3.77 prevent the project from reducing:
- bhava to only “identity formation,”
- jāti to only “ego-thought appearing.”

Bhava includes sensual/form/formless modes of becoming/existence and future renewed becoming.

Jāti includes actual birth/rebirth and manifestation of aggregates/sense bases among beings.

Present-life psychological applications may illuminate the teaching, but they must not replace these early meanings.

## 7. Gandhabba and the transition remain underdetermined

MN 38 and MN 93 present conception as requiring:
- parental union,
- fertility,
- presence of a gandhabba.

Comparative witnesses vary in terminology.

The strongest safe conclusion:
- conception is not portrayed as merely physical reproduction;
- a rebirth-transition condition is included;
- this condition is not an eternal soul, because MN 38 has already rejected unchanged transmigrating consciousness.

The exact ontology of gandhabba remains OPEN.

## 8. Intermediate state: do not force a sectarian answer into EBT

SN 44.9 says that when a being has laid down this body and is not yet reborn in another, craving is its sustenance.

Chinese parallel material supports a transition formulation.

This is substantial evidence that early textual material can accommodate an interval.

However, it does not by itself prove the fully developed antarābhava doctrines of later schools.

Likewise, the later Theravāda cuti→paṭisandhi model of immediate relinking is more technically specified than the early suttas examined here.

Therefore:
- **intermediate-transition reading:** EBT_PROBABLE / TENSION;
- **fully developed intermediate body:** LATER SYSTEMATIZATION;
- **fully specified instantaneous paṭisandhi mechanism:** LATER SYSTEMATIZATION.

The minimal EBT rebirth model does not need to decide this question.

## 9. MINIMAL EBT REBIRTH MODEL

The following is the narrowest synthesis that currently survives counterevidence:

### Conditions sustaining saṃsāric continuity
- avijjā / ignorance,
- taṇhā / craving,
- upādāna / grasping,
- cetanā/kamma / intentional activity,
- latent tendencies,
- establishment/growth of viññāṇa.

### Conditional continuity
- viññāṇa and nāmarūpa mutually support lived/reborn existence;
- conditioned becoming (bhava) develops;
- birth (jāti) occurs;
- aging/death and suffering follow.

### What does NOT continue as an invariant
- no eternal self,
- no unchanged consciousness,
- no numerically identical substance is required by the texts.

### What DOES continue
Not a thing, but **causal efficacy and conditioned dependence**:
earlier intentions, craving, grasping and formations condition later becoming.

This last sentence is SYNTHESIS, not a direct quotation.

## 10. What “person” means inside this model

Early Buddhist analysis need not deny conventional person-language.

One can still say:
- a person acts,
- a person receives consequences,
- a being is reborn,

without treating “person” as an invariant metaphysical substance.

The analytical level asks:
what are the conditions by which this continuity occurs?

This is consistent with the broader early-Buddhist pattern already seen in:
- aggregates,
- selfing,
- dependent arising,
- chariot/person reasoning later clarified by Milinda.

## 11. MILINDA: clarification versus extension

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE — “neither same nor another”
Nāgasena explains continuity through:
- child and adult,
- lamp flame across time,
- milk becoming curd/butter/ghee.

These clarify non-identity with causal continuity.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE — rebirth without transmigration
One lamp lights another without a substance moving from one flame to the next.
A learned verse is transmitted without a “verse substance” traveling from teacher to student.

This fits MN 38’s rejection of unchanged transmigrating consciousness.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE — responsibility without identical substance
The mango analogy explains why later consequences still belong causally to earlier deeds even though the later fruit is not numerically identical to the planted seed.

### MILINDA_EXTENSION
Nāgasena’s polished formulas make the logic more explicit than the early suttas.
They clarify the EBT architecture but should not be treated as verbatim historical-Buddha formulations.

### MILINDA TENSION
Some Milinda passages/traditional translations imply immediate rebirth succession.
This should not override the early textual ambiguity around SN 44.9/intermediate transition.

## 12. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “A soul must travel or rebirth is impossible.” — MN 38 rejects the invariant consciousness model.
2. “No soul means death ends all continuity.” — SN 12.38, AN 3.77 and DN 15 preserve renewed becoming/birth.
3. “The same person receives the result in an absolute metaphysical sense.” — SN 12.17/46 reject the strict-sameness extreme.
4. “A completely unrelated person receives the result.” — the different-person extreme is also rejected.
5. “Kamma requires an owner who stores it.” — intention and conditional causality are enough for the textual model.
6. “Bhava/jāti are only present-moment psychology.” — standard definitions preserve rebirth meanings.
7. “Gandhabba is the hidden ātman.” — incompatible with the same discourse family’s rejection of unchanged consciousness.
8. “EBT already contains the full Theravāda paṭisandhi mechanism.” — not established.
9. “EBT certainly teaches a fully developed intermediate body.” — also not established.

## 13. CONFIDENCE MAP

**EBT_STRONG**
- rebirth/renewed becoming is part of early Buddhist teaching;
- same unchanged consciousness does not transmigrate;
- intention/kamma and craving matter for future continuity;
- viññāṇa and nāmarūpa mutually condition each other;
- strict sameness and strict otherness are both rejected as explanatory extremes;
- bhava/jāti have genuine rebirth meanings.

**CROSS-TRADITION STRONG**
- rejection of unchanged consciousness has Āgama support;
- viññāṇa–nāmarūpa reciprocity has parallel support;
- several dependent-arising continuity passages have Chinese/Sanskrit witnesses.

**EBT_PROBABLE / OPEN**
- gandhabba as the rebirth-transition being/process;
- some form of intermediate transition.

**LATER_SYSTEMATIZATION**
- exact cuti/paṭisandhi/bhavaṅga mechanism;
- detailed school-specific intermediate-body ontology.

## 14. Central philosophical result of Phase 2B

The question:
> “What thing travels from one life to the next?”

is probably already misframed from the standpoint of dependent arising.

The early teaching asks instead:
> “With what conditions does renewed becoming arise, and with the cessation of what conditions does it cease?”

This is the same middle method seen elsewhere:
- not eternalism,
- not annihilationism,
- not a hidden carrier,
- not causal disconnection.

**Continuity is explained by dependent arising.**

## 15. Practical meaning inside Buddhist thought

The rebirth doctrine is not merely cosmological information.

Its practical force is:
- craving now matters,
- intention now matters,
- clinging now matters,
- ignorance now matters,
because these are conditions of renewed becoming.

The path therefore does not aim to discover “the traveler.”
It aims to end the conditions by which travel through saṃsāra continues.

This links Phase 2B directly to Nibbāna.
