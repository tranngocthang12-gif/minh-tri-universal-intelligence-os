## A147 — uncertainty, honest not-knowing, and vicikicchā

Central question:
How does uncertainty itself become a source of suffering: why does the mind demand certainty, how is vicikicchā different from honest not-knowing, and how does the Buddha proceed when evidence is incomplete?

### TEXT_ATTESTED

1. The five-hindrance formulas classify vicikicchā as a hindrance. It is a state that obstructs collectedness and clear progress, not merely the neutral fact that some proposition is currently unknown.
2. MN 95 gives a strong epistemic guardrail: faith, preference, oral tradition, reasoning, and reflective acceptance may all be present without licensing the claim “only this is true; anything else is false.” One may preserve truth by reporting the actual basis of one's confidence without overstating certainty.
3. MN 63 shows that the Buddha refuses to make liberation depend on settling speculative questions that do not conduce to the goal. The method is not “answer every uncertainty first,” but “identify what is relevant to suffering and its cessation.”
4. MN 2 likewise distinguishes wise attention from unproductive self-speculation. Some questions can generate a thicket of views rather than understanding.
5. Wise-attention and right-effort material show that when uncertainty is present, the task is to investigate skillfully, abandon what is unwholesome, cultivate what is wholesome, and act according to the best-supported view without pretending certainty that is not present.

### HONEST NOT-KNOWING != VICIKICCHĀ

A147’s central distinction:

- honest not-knowing: “the evidence is not sufficient yet; I do not know.”
- vicikicchā as hindrance: doubt/indecision that obstructs practice, leaves the mind unable to commit to appropriate investigation or action, and repeatedly circles without resolution.

This is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS, not a one-line canonical definition of vicikicchā.

The key guardrail:
**epistemic humility is not the same thing as paralyzing doubt.**

### WHY THE MIND DEMANDS CERTAINTY

A147 applies prior A132–A146 work.

Uncertainty can threaten:
- control;
- status;
- safety;
- identity;
- the wish to know what will happen.

The mind may therefore prefer a false certainty to an honest “I do not know.”

Examples:
- “They definitely hate me.”
- “This plan will certainly fail.”
- “I am definitely right.”
- “Nothing bad can happen.”

All four remove uncertainty psychologically, but none is justified merely because certainty feels safer.

These are CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION examples.

### MN 95 — STATE THE STRENGTH OF EVIDENCE AT ITS ACTUAL STRENGTH

A147 adopts the rule:
- if it is faith, call it faith;
- if it is inference, call it inference;
- if it is tradition, call it tradition;
- if it has been directly known, distinguish that from the earlier stages.

This prevents both dogmatism and nihilistic indecision.

The wording is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS grounded in MN 95.

### MN 63 — NOT EVERY UNANSWERED QUESTION MUST BE SOLVED FIRST

The poisoned-arrow simile provides a major methodological lesson.

Some uncertainty is irrelevant to the immediate task.

A person can spend enormous energy demanding:
“first tell me everything with certainty”
while the actual causes of suffering remain untouched.

A147 therefore distinguishes:
- relevant uncertainty: must be investigated because it affects right action or understanding;
- irrelevant/speculative uncertainty: does not need resolution before practice can proceed.

This twofold framing is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### DOUBT AND INVESTIGATION ARE NOT THE SAME

Investigation asks:
“What evidence would help? What can be tested? What distinction matters?”

Hindering doubt circles:
“But what if? But what if? But what if?”
without generating a better inquiry or next action.

A147 therefore treats skillful investigation as active, structured, and open to revision; vicikicchā is the obstructive mode in which uncertainty itself becomes a barrier.

This is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### ACTION UNDER UNCERTAINTY

The Buddha’s method does not require omniscience for every practical decision.

A guarded working process:
1. identify what is known;
2. identify what is unknown;
3. identify what matters to suffering/non-harm;
4. gather relevant evidence where possible;
5. choose the most skillful available action;
6. remain ready to revise;
7. do not convert provisional judgment into identity.

This seven-step procedure is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### CONNECTION TO FEAR AND CONTROL

A146 showed that fear-driven projection turns possibility into certainty.
A147 adds the inverse mistake:
fear of being wrong can stop action entirely.

Thus two extremes:
- false certainty;
- frozen indecision.

The middle path is:
**provisional clarity with revisability.**

This wording is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### PRACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC

When uncertainty is painful, ask:
1. What exactly do I not know?
2. What do I know with reasonable confidence?
3. What evidence would change my view?
4. Is this uncertainty relevant to the action at hand?
5. Am I seeking truth, or seeking relief from the feeling of not knowing?
6. Am I inventing certainty to feel safe?
7. Am I refusing to act because certainty is impossible?
8. What is the least harmful, most evidence-aligned action available now?
9. Can I act provisionally and revise later?
10. Is the doubt producing investigation or merely repetition?

### MILINDAPANHA — MANDATORY

Consulted: YES.

Relevant later support:
- Milindapanha’s question-and-answer method repeatedly decomposes apparent contradiction into distinguishable cases rather than resolving uncertainty by assertion.
- Nāgasena’s reasoning emphasizes clarification before conclusion, supporting the distinction between disciplined inquiry and mere indecision.

Role: later/paracanonical support only.

Guardrail:
Milindapanha does not replace MN 95, MN 63, MN 2, or five-hindrance material.

### CORRECTIONS PRESERVED

- not-knowing != failure;
- doubt != investigation;
- vicikicchā != every question;
- humility != relativism;
- provisional judgment != dogmatism;
- action under uncertainty != reckless guessing;
- unanswered speculation need not block practice;
- certainty-feeling != truth.
