## Test 1 — Separate the three roles

Scenario: A 45–60 second animation shows a person waiting for a message that never arrives.

Answer:
- Tổng biên tập decides the audience promise, central meaning, what must be included, what must be removed, and whether the ending pays off the premise.
- Đạo diễn decides blocking, framing, shot size, camera movement, performance/action, sound intention, and where the viewer should look.
- Dựng decides when information/emotion arrives, where to cut, how long to hold, and how to preserve emotion/story/rhythm across cuts.

Result: PASS — role boundaries are understood as different control questions rather than job titles.

## Test 2 — Beautiful shot with no story function

Scenario: After a strong opening hook, there is a beautiful 6-second establishing shot that adds no new information, emotion, setup, or payoff.

Answer:
Default action is cut or compress. Beauty alone is not sufficient justification. Keep it only if it serves a specific function such as emotional breathing room, geography, contrast, or anticipation.

Result: PASS — applies the editorial rule instead of protecting decorative material.

## Test 3 — Emotion vs spatial continuity

Scenario: The strongest emotional reaction take slightly bends screen continuity; the technically clean take is emotionally weaker.

Answer:
Test the stronger emotional take first. Emotion and story rank above perfect spatial continuity, provided the audience can still understand the scene and the discontinuity does not create harmful confusion. If confusion becomes dominant, repair with another angle, insert, sound bridge, or alternate cut.

Result: PASS — understands trade-off, not a rigid continuity rule.

## Test 4 — Diagnose YouTube retention

Scenario:
- Strong click-through promise.
- Noticeable retention dip from 12–18 seconds during explanation.
- Large spike around 35 seconds.

Answer:
1. Inspect 12–18 seconds for repetition, delayed payoff, unclear exposition, low visual change, or promise mismatch.
2. Inspect the 35-second spike to determine whether viewers rewatched because it was compelling or because it was confusing.
3. Test moving some of the 35-second value/setup earlier.
4. Do not assume a spike is automatically positive.
5. Change one major variable where practical so the next result is interpretable.

Result: PASS — retention is treated as diagnostic evidence, not decoration.

## Test 5 — Directing AI animation continuity

Scenario: Eight AI-generated shots must feel like one continuous scene.

Answer:
Continuity review must track at minimum:
- character identity
- costume
- body proportions
- lighting direction
- background geography
- object placement
- camera axis
- movement direction
- eyeline
- emotional state across the cut

Result: PASS — extends live-action continuity logic to generative production.

## Test 6 — Promise mismatch

Scenario: Title/thumbnail promise an emotional reveal, but the opening spends 20 seconds on background explanation.

Answer:
Rewrite the opening so it immediately signals or begins fulfilling the promised emotional reveal. Move only necessary context after the hook, or distribute context through action and visual storytelling.

Result: PASS — understands that editorial promise governs the opening.
