## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. MN 18 — papañca occurs after perception and thought in the stated process

MN 18 gives the process:

- contact;
- feeling;
- what one feels, one perceives/recognizes;
- what one perceives, one thinks about;
- what one thinks about, one `papañceti`;
- because of that, `papañcasaññāsaṅkhā` assail the person with respect to past, future, and present sense experience.

This directly establishes that, in MN 18:

- `sañjānāti` / perception is not identical to `vitakketi` / thought;
- `vitakketi` is not identical to `papañceti`;
- papañca is downstream of ordinary perceptual and thought operations in the passage.

**Guardrail:** MN 18 does not lexically define papañca as "all thought", "memory", "imagination", or "identity".

### 2. MN 18 — conflict and papañca are structurally connected

The discourse frames the teaching around the conditions under which categories related to papañca assail a person and around the ending of:

- taking up rods and weapons;
- quarrels;
- disputes;
- accusations;
- divisive speech;
- false speech;
- unwholesome states.

This supports the project use of MN 18 as a conflict-generation text.

It does not, by itself, prove that every conflict is caused by papañca or that every papañca episode produces overt social conflict.

### 3. Snp 4.11 — direct relation: `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`

Snp 4.11 directly states that `papañcasaṅkhā` have `saññā` as `nidāna`.

This is strong early evidence for a perception/papañca relation.

**Critical limit:** the line does not explicitly insert `taṇhā`, `diṭṭhi`, or `māna` as a threefold definition of papañca.

### 4. Snp 4.11 — papañca belongs inside a wider inquiry into dispute

The surrounding Kalahavivāda inquiry traces quarrels/disputes through what is dear, desire, pleasant/unpleasant, contact, name-and-form, grasping/appropriation, and perception-related structures.

Therefore papañca is not isolated from craving, attachment, and conflict in the wider discourse.

But the text should not be rewritten as a single linear psychological formula if it does not present one.

### 5. Snp 4.9 and related Aṭṭhakavagga verses — conceit/comparison and views are independently tied to dispute

A162 and PHAT-ATTHAKA-LEX-02 establish that:

- Snp 4.9 directly connects conceiving oneself equal/superior/inferior with dispute;
- Aṭṭhakavagga repeatedly connects grasped views, exclusive truth-claims, superiority, comparison, and dispute;
- Snp 4.9 also distinguishes grasping `saññā` and view from detachment toward them.

This supplies early-text evidence that `māna`/comparison and `diṭṭhi`/view-grasping can participate in the same conflict field as papañca-related material.

**Text-attested limit:** these verses do not say "papañca = taṇhā + diṭṭhi + māna".

### 6. AN 4.173 and related early uses — nippapañca is treated as a positive liberative orientation

Early texts also contrast papañca with `nippapañca` / non-proliferation or non-diffuseness in positive path language.

This shows that papañca is not merely a neutral synonym for "having concepts".

The exact best English rendering remains context-sensitive.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Papañca is best treated as a process/function, not a single modern noun

Across MN 18 and Snp 4.11, a guarded working model is:

**papañca is a proliferative elaborating process in which what has been perceived and thought about expands into a field that can dominate experience and feed conflict.**

This is a synthesis.

"Conceptual proliferation" is a useful modern rendering in many contexts, but it is not an exhaustive lexical definition.

### B. Taṇhā, diṭṭhi, and māna are strongly relevant without being the early definition

Early texts independently show:

- craving/appropriation feeding grasping and continuation;
- views becoming grasped positions and bases for dispute;
- conceit/comparison generating equal/superior/inferior self-positioning.

Therefore it is reasonable to use taṇhā, diṭṭhi, and māna as major explanatory contributors to proliferative self-conflict.

But the sentence:

`papañca = taṇhā-papañca + diṭṭhi-papañca + māna-papañca`

is not established here as an explicit Nikāya/Aṭṭhakavagga definition.

That classification belongs to the exegetical layer discussed below.

### C. "Identity story" remains synthesis

The project has used a practical model:

event/perception  
→ interpretation/thought  
→ proliferation  
→ mine/I/ranking/view-position  
→ repeated story  
→ conflict.

A163 keeps this as CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS only.

The pieces are early-attested in different texts, but no single early passage provides that complete chain.

### D. Papañca is not simply "too many thoughts"

A person can:

- remember accurately;
- plan;
- reason;
- examine conduct;
- analyze Dhamma;

without those functions necessarily being papañca.

The problem is not sheer thought quantity.

The project therefore looks for features such as:

- compulsive expansion;
- grasping;
- self-reference;
- comparison;
- fixed view-position;
- repetitive past/future construction;
- conflict-producing categorization.

This feature-list is analytic synthesis, not an early canonical checklist.

### E. Saññā is a condition/material, not automatically a defilement

Snp 4.11 gives a direct `saññā`–`papañcasaṅkhā` relation.

But PHAT-ATTHAKA-LEX-02 adds a necessary guardrail:

- Sn 802 speaks specifically of `pakappitā saññā`, a qualified/fashioned saññā;
- Sn 847 distinguishes `saññā` from grasping it;
- other early material intentionally cultivates certain perceptions.

Therefore A163 rejects:

"saññā itself is inherently papañca."

## LATER/PARACANONICAL / EXEGETICAL

### 1. Mahāniddesa — explicit taṇhā/diṭṭhi/māna papañca classification

Mahāniddesa, commenting on Aṭṭhakavagga material, explicitly analyzes `papañcasaṅkhā` in terms including:

- `taṇhā-papañcasaṅkhā`;
- `diṭṭhi-papañcasaṅkhā`;
- `māna-papañcasaṅkhā`.

This is important evidence for an early exegetical tradition connecting papañca with craving, views, and conceit.

**Classification:** LATER/PARACANONICAL / EXEGETICAL for the present project.

It is not silently promoted to TEXT_ATTESTED for the root Aṭṭhakavagga or Nikāya passages.

### 2. Why the later triad is plausible but still later

The later triad is not arbitrary:

- early texts already connect craving with grasping;
- views with fixation/dispute;
- conceit with comparison/self-positioning;
- papañca with perception/thought and conflict.

So the exegetical synthesis fits major early-text patterns.

But "fits the evidence well" is not the same claim as "the root discourse explicitly defined it this way."

### 3. Milindapañha

**Consulted:** YES.

Milindapañha does not supply a decisive independent lexical definition of `papañca` for A163.

Its role is methodological:

- separating neighboring mental functions before relating them;
- refusing to infer a permanent inner agent from coordinated mental operations;
- using distinction and objection to prevent category collapse.

This supports A163's method but does not override MN 18 or Snp 4.11.

## INTEGRATION OF PHAT-ATTHAKA-LEX-02 — PROVISIONAL

Findings accepted provisionally:

1. `saññā` is context-sensitive and cannot be reduced to memory or conceptual construction alone.
2. Sn 802 has `pakappitā saññā`; dropping `pakappitā` overstates the verse.
3. Sn 847 distinguishes detachment from/grasping of `saññā`.
4. Sn 874 directly gives `saññā` as `nidāna` of `papañcasaṅkhā`, but does not define papañca fully.
5. `maññati` has both ordinary "think/suppose" uses and marked possessive/self-ranking uses.
6. No audited Aṭṭhakavagga passage states a complete formula `saññā -> maññati -> papañca -> identity -> dispute`.

These points refine A162 and directly constrain A163.

## Practical understanding

A163's practical diagnostic is:

1. What was actually perceived?
2. What thought followed?
3. What has been added beyond the available evidence?
4. Has craving entered as "I need this / I must have this / I must get rid of this"?
5. Has view entered as "this interpretation alone must be true"?
6. Has conceit entered as "what does this make me relative to them?"
7. Is the mind now replaying, forecasting, comparing, and defending?
8. Is the expansion producing more greed, hatred, delusion, or conflict?
9. Can the process stop at clear knowing without further appropriation?

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION, not a canonical list.
