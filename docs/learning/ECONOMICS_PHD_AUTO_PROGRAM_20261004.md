## 3. Competency architecture

### Gate M0 — Mathematical foundations
Required competence:
- proof methods and mathematical maturity;
- single- and multivariable calculus;
- linear algebra and matrix calculus;
- constrained/unconstrained optimization;
- convexity and envelope theorems;
- probability theory and mathematical statistics;
- real analysis: limits, continuity, compactness, convergence;
- dynamic optimization and Bellman equations;
- basic measure/integration concepts needed for advanced probability/econometrics.

Evidence:
- closed-book diagnostic problems;
- derivations written from first principles;
- proof critique: identify invalid steps and repair them;
- optimization and dynamic-programming problem sets.

### Gate M1 — Graduate microeconomics
Core:
- preference relations, utility representation;
- consumer and producer theory;
- expenditure/cost duality;
- choice under uncertainty;
- general equilibrium;
- welfare theorems and social choice;
- game theory: static/dynamic, complete/incomplete information;
- information economics, adverse selection, moral hazard;
- auctions and mechanism design;
- matching and contract theory foundations.

Evidence:
- graduate problem sets;
- theorem assumptions and counterexamples;
- equilibrium derivations;
- oral-defense style explanation of comparative statics and welfare claims.

### Gate M2 — Graduate macroeconomics
Core:
- dynamic optimization;
- Solow and endogenous growth;
- Ramsey/Cass-Koopmans;
- overlapping generations;
- consumption/saving and asset pricing foundations;
- real business-cycle models;
- New Keynesian models;
- monetary and fiscal policy;
- labor/search and unemployment;
- heterogeneous-agent macro foundations;
- crises, frictions, and open-economy macro foundations.

Evidence:
- solve deterministic and stochastic dynamic models;
- derive equilibrium conditions;
- compute/approximate model solutions;
- compare model predictions with data;
- critique calibration/identification assumptions.

### Gate M3 — Econometrics and statistics
Core:
- probability, LLN, CLT, asymptotic arguments;
- linear regression and projection;
- MLE, GMM, M-estimation;
- hypothesis testing and confidence procedures;
- endogeneity and instrumental variables;
- panel data;
- difference-in-differences;
- regression discontinuity;
- randomized experiments;
- matching/selection;
- causal inference and potential outcomes;
- time series;
- high-dimensional methods;
- structural estimation foundations;
- robustness, specification, measurement error, missing data.

Evidence:
- derive estimators and asymptotic distributions;
- identify assumptions needed for causal claims;
- simulation studies;
- empirical replication;
- adversarial robustness checks.

### Gate M4 — Research methods and scientific practice
Core:
- question formation;
- literature search and synthesis;
- theory-to-test mapping;
- identification strategy;
- pre-analysis logic where applicable;
- data provenance and reproducibility;
- model comparison and falsification;
- scientific writing;
- seminar presentation;
- peer review and referee-response practice.

Evidence:
- one full replication package;
- one literature map;
- one research design memo;
- one second-year-paper-equivalent research paper;
- oral seminar and hostile-referee simulation.

### Gate M5 — Fields
Select only after core diagnostics. Two major fields plus breadth/minor work.

Candidate fields:
- Development Economics

- Public Economics
- Industrial Organization
- International Economics
- Financial Economics
- Monetary/Macroeconomics

No field is promoted to "major" merely by preference; selection should follow Owner goals, core performance, and research opportunity.

### Gate M6 — Dissertation-level portfolio
Requirements:
- research agenda with a coherent economic question;
- at least three paper-quality research projects or equivalent dissertation structure;
- explicit contribution relative to literature;
- theory/empirics appropriate to the question;
- reproducible code/data provenance where legally possible;
- robustness and counterevidence section;
- external-style critique;
- oral defense simulation.

Passing this gate means "doctoral-level learning evidence within MINH TRÍ", never an accredited degree.
