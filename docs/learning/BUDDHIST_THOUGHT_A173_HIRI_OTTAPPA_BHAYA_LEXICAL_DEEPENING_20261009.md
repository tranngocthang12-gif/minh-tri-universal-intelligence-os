## What was learned / revised

Prior A173 adequately contrasts prudence and recklessness, but risks collapsing `ottappa` into generic fear of danger. New cross-text evidence supports a tighter, non-exclusive functional differentiation.

## TEXT_ATTESTED: source-level anchors

- **AN 2.9 — Lokapāla Sutta**: two bright qualities `hirī` and `ottappa` are presented as guardians of the world/ethical restraint. These are paired ethical qualities, not a diagnostic taxonomy of ordinary fear. Primary translation: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN2_9.html .
- **AN 7.6 — Dhana Sutta (treasures)**: hiri is linked to concern/shame about wrongdoing by body, speech and mind; ottappa is a morally protective concern/dread regarding bodily, verbal and mental misconduct; source describes treasures of the noble disciple. Source: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN7_6.html . Lexical gloss varies among translators, and a full Pāli line-by-line comparison remains OPEN.
- **MN 4 — Bhayabherava Sutta**: `bhaya-bherava` characterizes fear-and-dread in isolated forest practice, with attention to one's own moral conduct and the response to fear. Source: https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.004.than.html . `bhaya` and `bherava` often occur together here; do NOT assert they are invariably two mutually exclusive mental categories.
- **MN 2 — Sabbāsava Sutta**: distinguish suitable endurance from wise avoidance; active avoidance of animals and hazardous places is explicitly described. Source: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN2.html . This is a teaching on abandoning āsavas, not a universal hazard-management policy.
- **Dhp 129–130**: fear/trembling at violence and the life-valuing comparison provide an early Buddhist verse context of fear/violence, with a compassionate non-harming imperative. Translations distinguish wording across verses; a direct Pāli morphology audit remains OPEN. Source: https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.10.than.html . Do not treat the verse as defining ottappa or proving every liberated person fears death.

## Mandatory explanatory layer: Milindapañha

**Mil 5.2.3 — Death**, later/paracanonical: Nāgasena's replies to the scope of 'all fear death' and the arahant exception, and his analogy of a sick patient fearing medical treatment though desiring cure, illustrate restricted context and avoiding hasty universalizations. Source: https://dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/en/mil5.2.3.html .
Classification: LATER_EXPLANATORY_EARLY_COMPATIBLE only for the *general scope-checking method*; no claim of original Buddha-authored terminology or definitive textual dependence between Dhp 129 and Mil 5.2.3.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS — functional model, NOT original canonical taxonomy

1. `ottappa`: in AN 2.9/7.6 it appears in the domain of ethics, especially recognizing and restraining wrongdoing of body, speech and mind; it should not be defined merely as panic.
2. `bhaya`/`bherava`: MN 4 discusses experienced fear/dread in a specific ascetic wilderness setting; such fear is not automatically identical with wholesome moral `ottappa`.
3. One response can involve discomfort or fear but still involve wisdom. MN 2 includes reasoned endurance *and* appropriate avoidance; the mere label 'fearful' or 'fearless' cannot by itself settle the right conduct.
4. A universal slogan such as 'Buddha always commands confronting fear' contradicts MN 2's avoidance treatment. A universal slogan 'avoid every discomfort' contradicts MN 2's endurance treatment.
5. The *kind of object, conduct, motive, foreseeable harm and context* must be checked before combining the categories. This last decision frame is a project synthesis, not a numbered list in the Nikāyas.

## Hostile counterexamples / uncertainty

- A person refuses a harmful action because it is wrong, without ordinary physical fear: compatible with `ottappa`, but not enough to infer absence of other motives.
- A person fears an aggressive dog and withdraws: MN 2 illustrates a reason to avoid danger, but this does NOT establish that the fear itself is `ottappa`.
- A person feels no anxiety about lying: absence of felt fear does NOT imply an ethical judgment is sound.
- A person remains in an unsafe place solely to look brave: MN 4's context does NOT prove this is wise.
- **Unresolved lexical issue:** `hiri`, `ottappa`, `bhaya`, `bherava` do not map one-to-one to the English/Vietnamese categories of shame, anxiety, dread, conscience, prudence or cowardice. Morphology, exact Pāli clauses, discourse parallels and translation diversity remain OPEN.
