## 1. Unified role model

### 1.1 Tổng biên tập — decides WHAT deserves to exist

Core responsibilities:
1. Define the audience and the promise of the piece.
2. Reduce the idea to one clear sentence: what the viewer will understand, feel, or want after watching.
3. Decide what belongs in the story and what must be cut.
4. Protect coherence across title, thumbnail/premise, opening, body, climax, and ending.
5. Enforce factual, copyright, safety, tone, and brand-consistency checks.
6. Require evidence for claims; distinguish fact, interpretation, and creative fiction.
7. Run a postmortem after publication using real audience behavior instead of taste alone.

Editorial question:
> If this section vanished, would the viewer lose meaning, emotion, clarity, or necessary setup?

If the answer is no, the default is to cut or compress it.

### 1.2 Đạo diễn — decides HOW the audience experiences it

Core responsibilities:
1. Define the intended emotion or attention target for every beat.
2. Translate script beats into blocking, composition, shot size, camera angle, movement, sound, and performance.
3. Build a shot list before production so coverage is deliberate rather than accidental.
4. Preserve spatial and visual continuity unless breaking continuity serves a specific story purpose.
5. Direct the viewer's eye: decide what must be noticed first, second, and last.
6. Capture enough coverage for the editor while avoiding redundant shots with no storytelling function.
7. Protect the final emotional intention during the cut.

Director question:
> What should the viewer be looking at, understanding, and feeling at this exact moment?

### 1.3 Biên tập/dựng — decides WHEN information and emotion arrive

Use Walter Murch's Rule of Six as a priority order:
1. Emotion
2. Story
3. Rhythm
4. Eye trace
5. 2D screen plane
6. 3D spatial continuity

Operational interpretation:
- A technically perfect cut is not automatically a good cut.
- If a cut improves emotion and story but slightly bends spatial continuity, test it rather than rejecting it automatically.
- If a flashy transition does not serve emotion, story, rhythm, or clarity, remove it.
- Organize footage first, watch all usable material, make an assembly/rough cut, then refine pacing, sound, color, and effects.

## 2. YouTube editorial feedback loop

YouTube's audience-retention tools distinguish:
- Intro retention: how many viewers remain after the first 30 seconds.
- Top moments: sections where viewers largely keep watching.
- Spikes: sections that are rewatched or shared, or sometimes need rewatching because they were unclear.
- Dips: sections viewers skip or abandon.

MINH TRÍ operating rule:
1. The opening must fulfill the promise implied by title/thumbnail instead of delaying it.
2. If the strongest moment appears late, test moving its value or setup earlier.
3. Treat spikes as a question, not automatically as praise: was the moment compelling or confusing?
4. Treat dips as diagnostic evidence: inspect repetition, slow setup, unclear exposition, weak visual change, or promise mismatch.
5. Compare similar formats with similar formats; Shorts, long-form video, and live content have different viewing behavior.

## 3. Pre-production workflow

### Gate A — Editorial brief
Before script or shot design, write:
- Audience
- One-sentence promise
- One-sentence story
- Intended emotion
- Required facts / sources
- Forbidden drift: material that would weaken the promise
- Ending payoff

### Gate B — Beat map
Break the piece into beats. For each beat record:
- New information
- Emotional change
- Visual action
- Audio purpose
- Why the beat must exist

No beat should survive only because it is "beautiful."

### Gate C — Shot list
For every required shot record:
- Scene / beat
- Shot number
- Shot size
- Camera angle
- Camera movement
- Subject/action
- Lens/equipment requirement when relevant
- Audio/dialogue
- Story purpose
- Priority: must-have / optional

Shot lists should communicate the director's vision and reduce missed coverage.

### Gate D — Blocking and eye path
Before camera or animation execution:
- Where is the subject?
- Where does the subject move?
- Where is the camera?
- Where should the viewer look?
- Does the next shot preserve or intentionally break orientation?
- Are eyelines and screen direction understandable?

## 4. Production principles

1. Capture the emotional take, not merely the technically clean take.
2. Get the must-have coverage before decorative shots.
3. Record room tone / clean audio assets when live production applies.
4. Maintain continuity in props, position, eyeline, direction, costume, lighting, and action unless discontinuity is intentional.
5. For animation or AI-generated shots, continuity checks expand to:
   - character identity
   - costume
   - proportions
   - lighting direction
   - background geography
   - object placement
   - camera axis
   - motion direction

## 5. Post-production workflow

### Pass 1 — Assembly
Put all necessary story beats in order. Do not polish.

### Pass 2 — Story cut
Remove material that does not advance meaning, character, emotion, or necessary setup.

### Pass 3 — Rhythm cut
Adjust shot duration, reaction time, silence, pauses, transitions, and scene entrances/exits.

### Pass 4 — Eye-trace and continuity
Check where the eye lands across each cut, screen direction, eyelines, action matching, and geography.

### Pass 5 — Sound-first review
Watch with special attention to dialogue clarity, music entry/exit, sound bridges, silence, and unwanted discontinuities.

### Pass 6 — Picture polish
Color, graphics, effects, reframing, captions, and final visual cleanup.

### Pass 7 — Cold-view review
Review from the start without stopping and record only viewer-facing problems. Do not fix while watching.

## 6. Short-form / animation operating pattern

For a short piece, test this sequence:
1. Immediate visual/question/payoff signal.
2. Establish who/what matters.
3. Escalate or introduce contrast.
4. Deliver the strongest visual or emotional turn.
5. End on a clean payoff, reveal, loop, or unresolved question that is intentional.

Do not force arbitrary cut frequency. Fast cutting is useful only when it serves clarity, energy, comedy, urgency, or platform behavior.

## 7. Practice curriculum

### Exercise 1 — Tổng biên tập
Take five candidate YouTube/animation ideas.
For each, write:
- audience promise
- why now
- one-sentence story
- strongest moment
- what can be cut

Deliverable: one selected concept plus four documented rejection reasons.
Status after completion: PRACTICED, not VERIFIED.

### Exercise 2 — Đạo diễn
Take one 45–60 second concept.
Build:
- beat map
- simple floor/space map
- 8–12 shot shot-list
- viewer eye-path notes
- continuity risks

Deliverable: production-ready directing package.
Status after completion: PRACTICED, not VERIFIED.

### Exercise 3 — Dựng
Create three cuts of the same source:
- emotion-first
- story-clarity-first
- rhythm/energy-first

Compare what was gained and lost in each version.
Deliverable: edit decision log.

### Exercise 4 — YouTube feedback
After a real upload has enough data:
- inspect intro retention
- mark top moments
- inspect spikes
- inspect dips
- record one hypothesis per anomaly
- change only one major variable per next experiment where practical

Deliverable: retention postmortem linked to the next editorial hypothesis.

## 8. Review checklist before final approval

### Tổng biên tập
- [ ] Promise is clear.
- [ ] Opening fulfills the promise.
- [ ] Every beat earns its place.
- [ ] Facts are sourced where required.
- [ ] Tone and identity are consistent.
- [ ] Ending pays off the setup.

### Đạo diễn
- [ ] Intended emotion exists for every important beat.
- [ ] Viewer eye path is controlled.
- [ ] Blocking and composition tell the story.
- [ ] Shot list contains must-have coverage.
- [ ] Continuity is deliberate.
- [ ] Sound has a storytelling purpose.

### Dựng
- [ ] Cuts preserve emotion and story before technical perfection.
- [ ] Rhythm changes intentionally.
- [ ] No unnecessary repetition.
- [ ] Audio is intelligible and purposeful.
- [ ] Graphics/effects do not compete with the story.
- [ ] Final cold-view pass completed.
