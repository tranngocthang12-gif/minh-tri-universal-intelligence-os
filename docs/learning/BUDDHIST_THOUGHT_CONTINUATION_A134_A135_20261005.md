## A134 — wise effort vs compulsive striving

TEXT_ATTESTED:
- Four right efforts require desire/effort/energy to prevent and abandon unwholesome states and arouse/maintain wholesome states.
- Chanda is context-sensitive and is not globally identical to taṇhā.
- SN 46 balances energizing factors with calming factors according to the state of mind.
- AN 6.55 Soṇa uses the lute-string simile: too tight leads to agitation, too loose to laxity; balanced energy is the target.

CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS:
Wise effort works on trainable conditions. Compulsive striving is driven by identity, fear, comparison, or demand for guaranteed outcomes.

MILINDAPANHA:
Consulted as later/paracanonical support for viriya as a sustaining/supporting wholesome quality. No override of early balancing texts.

## A135 — attachment to progress, attainment, purity, and the identity “I am progressing”

Central question:
Can meditation, insight, morality, stages, purity, or progress themselves become objects of clinging and conceit?

### TEXT_ATTESTED

1. MN 22’s raft simile: Dhamma is for crossing over, not for clinging to. Even a useful and true teaching must not be converted into possession.
2. MN 29 / MN 30 heartwood similes warn against stopping at gain/honor/fame, virtue, concentration, or knowledge and vision as if these were the final heartwood. Attachment to intermediate attainments can derail the purpose of practice.
3. MN 113 describes the “good person” as not exalting self or disparaging others on account of qualities such as faith, learning, seclusion, and meditative attainments. This directly guards against turning progress into rank.
4. SN 22.89 shows that explicit identity-view may weaken while residual “I am” conceit remains. Therefore even genuine progress can still be appropriated by asmimāna.
5. Earlier right-view and right-effort material remains relevant: path factors are functional means for release, not trophies that establish a superior self.

### PROGRESS != IDENTITY

A135 distinguishes:
- measuring whether practice is becoming more skillful;
- constructing an identity from that measurement.

Useful assessment asks:
“What has actually weakened? What has been cultivated? What still needs training?”

Identity-making asks:
“What does this prove I am?”

The second shift is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### THE SUBTLE TRAP

A practitioner may stop clinging to ordinary status and then build a new status:
- “I meditate more deeply.”
- “I understand non-self.”
- “I am more purified.”
- “I am at a higher stage.”
- “I am not attached like other people.”

This can preserve the same comparison machinery under spiritual language.

The exact examples are CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION, not canonical quotations.

### MN 29 / MN 30 — INTERMEDIATE GOODS ARE NOT THE HEARTWOOD

The heartwood similes are crucial because they do not deny the value of virtue, concentration, or knowledge.

They deny mistaking an intermediate benefit for the final aim.

Thus:
valuable != final;
attained != possessed self;
progress != completion.

### MN 113 — DO NOT USE QUALITY TO RANK SELF

A quality can be real without becoming conceit.

One may truthfully know:
“concentration is stronger than before”
without adding:
“therefore I am superior.”

A135 therefore preserves:
accurate assessment != māna by definition.

The problem is comparison/appropriation, not knowledge of fact.

### “I AM PROGRESSING” AS A TEMPORARY CONVENTION

Conventional language about progress is useful for training, planning, and correction.

But it becomes dangerous when:
progress -> possession -> status -> comparison -> fear of losing attainment.

That chain is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### FEAR OF REGRESSION

Attachment to attainment can generate a new fear:
“If this state disappears, what am I?”

This links A135 back to A132–A134:
what is appropriated becomes something one must defend and control.

The answer is not to stop cultivation, but to stop making the cultivated state into self.

### MILINDAPANHA — MANDATORY LATER/PARACANONICAL SUPPORT

Consulted: YES.

Relevant later support:
- anti-pride/full-vessel imagery cautions against inflation from learning or accomplishment;
- the distinction between reasoning/wisdom and conventional designation supports the idea that qualities can function without becoming a fixed self;
- Milindapanha is used only as later support, not as an early-text authority on path-stage mechanics.

### PRACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC

When progress becomes emotionally charged, ask:
1. What quality has actually changed?
2. Is the change stable, temporary, or uncertain?
3. Am I using the information to train, or to rank myself?
4. Do I need others to recognize the attainment?
5. Am I afraid of losing the state because it has become “mine”?
6. If the state weakens, can I return to conditions and practice without identity-collapse?
7. Does this attainment reduce greed, hatred, and delusion, or mainly increase self-importance?
8. Can I use the path fully while remembering the raft function: for crossing, not possession?

### CORRECTIONS PRESERVED
- non-clinging to progress != neglecting progress;
- measuring practice != conceit by definition;
- attainment != permanent identity;
- genuine meditative quality != proof of superiority;
- path factors are means, not trophies;
- fear of regression can itself reveal appropriation;
- Milindapanha remains later/paracanonical support.
