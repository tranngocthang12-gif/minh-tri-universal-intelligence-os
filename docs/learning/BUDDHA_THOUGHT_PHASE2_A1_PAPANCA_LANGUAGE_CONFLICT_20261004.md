## Research question

How does ordinary perception become self-referential conceptual expansion and then social conflict? Is papañca simply “thinking too much,” or is it a more specific mode of self/world construction?

## 1. MN 18 — cognitive expansion is not identical with thinking

MN 18 gives the core sequence:
sense base + object → consciousness → contact → feeling → perception (saññā) → thinking (vitakka) → papañca.

Then the person becomes “assailed” by papañca-related perceptions/categories concerning objects past, present and future.

Key correction:
- vitakka precedes papañca in this discourse;
- therefore papañca cannot simply mean “all thinking.”

The discourse opens with the Buddha’s claim that his teaching aims at a state in which one does not quarrel with anyone and the relevant perceptions no longer obsess the practitioner.

Thus papañca is tied not only to cognition but to conflict.

## 2. MN 18 gives a crucial intervention point

Mahākaccāna’s explanation says that where there is nothing to relish, welcome or remain fastened to in relation to the conditions that lead to papañca, the underlying tendencies to:
- passion,
- resistance,
- views,
- uncertainty,
- conceit,
- passion for becoming,
- ignorance

come to an end.

This is decisive:
papañca is not treated as an isolated language malfunction. It is sustained by affective appropriation — relishing, welcoming and fastening.

Therefore the current model must include both:
- cognitive categorization,
- affective investment.

## 3. SN 35.248 — self-reference is simultaneously maññanā, papañca and māna

SN 35.248 classifies self-referential constructions such as:
- “I am,”
- “I am this,”
- “I shall be / shall not be,”
- future identity in terms of form and perception

under overlapping descriptions including:
- conceiving (maññita/maññanā),
- proliferation/objectification (papañcita),
- conceit (māna).

This strongly supports a network model:
papañca is not merely adding more neutral concepts; it can participate in self-location and identity projection.

## 4. Sn 4.9 Māgandiya — comparison converts identity into dispute

Sn 4.9 states that one who supposes oneself:
- equal,
- superior,
- inferior

thereby enters dispute, whereas one not moved by these three comparisons has no basis for that kind of quarrel.

The same discourse rejects both:
- defining purity by possessing view/learning/knowledge/practice,
and
- claiming purity simply from lacking them.

This supports a functional use of knowledge without identity-appropriation.

## 5. Sn 4.11 Kalahavivāda — conflict has multiple conditional roots

Sn 4.11 traces quarrels/disputes backward through:
- what is dear/not dear,
- desire,
- pleasant/unpleasant,
- contact,
- name-and-form,
while also linking papañca-related categories to perception.

This matters because it does not present one universal linear chain.

A reasonable synthesis is:
- perception contributes to categorization/proliferation;
- desire and appropriation contribute to conflict;
- these processes can converge and reinforce one another.

## 6. Sn 4.14 Tuvaṭaka — self-referential cognition is placed at the root of papañca

Sn 4.14 opens by targeting the root of papañca-related categories with a phrase often translated along the lines of:
“I am the thinker.”

The exact philology of mantā remains disputed. Therefore:
- safe conclusion: the verse links a self-referential cognitive stance with the root of papañca;
- unsafe conclusion: one English/Vietnamese gloss is certainly the exact original semantic value.

The same discourse warns against using any truth one knows as a basis for hardening oneself or measuring oneself as better, worse or equal.

This is strong evidence that cognitive possession → identity → comparison is a central target.

## 7. Aṭṭhakavagga as a conflict laboratory

Across Sn 4.8–4.14, recurring targets include:
- “only this is true” positioning,
- debate victory,
- comparison,
- dependence on views,
- grasping perceptions/views,
- becoming/non-becoming,
- self-positioning.

Therefore the Aṭṭhakavagga does not primarily look like a ban on cognition.
It looks much more like a critique of:
**identity-laden cognition that becomes a basis for contest, possession and conflict.**

## 8. Arthapada / T198 — independent recension support

T198 佛說義足經 (Yizujing / Arthapada) is an early Chinese translation by Zhi Qian from another Indic recension and is recognized as a parallel to the Aṭṭhakavagga.

The extant Chinese text explicitly marks parallels to:
- Sn 4.9 Māgandiya (vv. 835–847),
- Sn 4.11 Kalahavivāda (vv. 862–877).

The T198 material preserves the broad architecture of:
- view/identity dispute,
- dear/not-dear,
- desire,
- contact,
- grasping/appropriation,
- conflict.

Methodological caution:
Old Chinese vocabulary does not map one-to-one onto Pāli terms. The strongest use of T198 here is structural confirmation, not lexical identity.

## 9. Papañca should not be collapsed into one translation

Current safe policy:
retain **papañca** in core analysis.

Possible contextual glosses:
- proliferation,
- complication,
- elaboration,
- objectification,
- self-reflexive conceptual expansion.

No single gloss has yet earned exclusive status.

The commentarial equation of papañca with craving, conceit and views is later and useful, but should not be assumed to be the full lexical meaning of the early term.

## 10. FIRST NETWORK MODEL OF CONFLICT

Non-canonical synthesis:

contact
→ feeling
→ perception
→ thought
→ self/world categorization
↔ “I / mine”
↔ comparison
↔ view-position
↔ relishing / resistance / becoming-desire
→ defensive speech/action
→ quarrel/conflict
→ new contact/feeling
→ renewed loop

This model is deliberately recursive.

Why recursive?
Because:
- MN 18 puts thought before papañca;
- other early material can put papañca-related categories before further thinking/desire/conflict;
- Aṭṭhakavagga links perception, desire, identity and quarrel through multiple routes.

Therefore “one conveyor belt” is less faithful than a conditioned feedback network.

## 11. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Papañca = thinking.” — MN 18 distinguishes vitakka from papañca.
2. “Papañca = language itself.” — wholesome/right speech and right view remain path factors.
3. “Perception itself is the enemy.” — wholesome perceptions are explicitly cultivated elsewhere.
4. “Views cause conflict simply because views exist.” — the stronger target is grasping, identity, comparison and exclusivist truth-possession.
5. “Aṭṭhakavagga rejects all knowledge.” — Sn 4.9 rejects both purity-by-possession and purity-by-absence.
6. “Selfing begins only after papañca.” — SN 35.248 and Sn 4.14 show self-reference can already be part of papañca’s content/root.
7. “T198 proves exact Pāli lexical meaning.” — it provides independent structural evidence, not one-to-one lexeme equivalence.

## 12. CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- MN 18 distinguishes perception, thought and papañca;
- papañca is associated with conflict;
- self-referential construing, conceit and papañca overlap;
- comparison as equal/superior/inferior feeds dispute;
- cognitive positions can become objects of grasping rather than being inherently wrong.

**CROSS-TRADITION STRONG AT STRUCTURAL LEVEL**
- T198 preserves independent Aṭṭhakavagga recensions including Māgandiya and Kalahavivāda structures.
- MN 18 has recognized Āgama parallels, supporting a cross-tradition cognitive/conflict architecture.

**EBT_PROBABLE**
- papañca is best understood as self-referential conceptual expansion/objectification rather than generic thought.
- conflict emerges from interaction between categorization and affective appropriation.

**OPEN**
- exact lexical core of papañca;
- exact relation among papañca, taṇhā, māna and diṭṭhi across every early stratum;
- complete line-by-line T198 lexical alignment for Sn 4.8–4.14.

## 13. PRACTICAL MEANING

When conflict begins, look for the transition from:
- “something happened”
to
- “this means something about me”
to
- “my position”
to
- “their position”
to
- “I am right / higher / lower / attacked”
to
- defending, attacking or rehearsing the conflict.

The possible intervention is not “stop thinking.”
It is:
- notice relishing/resistance,
- notice identity-loading,
- notice comparison,
- notice the moment a useful distinction becomes a possessed position.
