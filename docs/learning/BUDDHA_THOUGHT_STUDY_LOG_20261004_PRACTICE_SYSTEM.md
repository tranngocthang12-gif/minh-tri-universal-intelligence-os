## Research question

How is liberating knowledge cultivated as a path process rather than merely held as a view? How do sammādiṭṭhi, sammāvāyāma, sammāsati, sammāsamādhi, yoniso manasikāra, satipaṭṭhāna and the awakening factors interact?

## TEXTUAL FINDING

### 1. MN 117 + MĀ 189 + Tibetan parallel: right concentration is path-integrated

The common core of the Mahācattārīsaka family presents right concentration as supported by the other path factors and also presents a sequential relation in which right view leads through the path factors toward right concentration.

Comparative study of MN 117, MĀ 189 and the Tibetan parallel shows that the same family also portrays right view, right effort and right mindfulness as collaborating in the cultivation of other path factors.

Therefore the sequential list should not be interpreted as a rigid one-factor-at-a-time ladder. The more defensible reading is coordinated development with directional structure.

### 2. MN 117: view, effort and mindfulness form an operational loop

For wrong/right view, intention, speech, action and livelihood:
- right view diagnoses wrong and right;
- right effort works to abandon the wrong and enter/develop the right;
- right mindfulness keeps the relevant task present.

The discourse describes these factors as running/circling around the target factor.

Working synthesis:
- view = orientation/diagnosis;
- effort = active adjustment;
- mindfulness = task retention/monitoring;
- concentration = integration/stability of the whole trained configuration.

This is a modern functional model, not canonical terminology.

### 3. Yoniso manasikāra is not neutral attention

AN 2.126 and MN 43 state two conditions for the arising of right view:
- the voice/utterance of another;
- yoniso manasikāra (appropriate/rational/wise attention).

SN 45.55/69 treats accomplishment in yoniso manasikāra as a forerunner for developing the noble eightfold path.

MN 2 clarifies the practical contrast:
- inappropriate attention can feed sensuality, becoming and ignorance;
- appropriate attention attends to suffering, its origin, cessation and the path.

Therefore yoniso manasikāra is not merely “paying more attention.” It is attention framed so that it reveals causal structure relevant to suffering and its ending.

### 4. Early Buddhist epistemology is neither pure authority nor pure self-reliance

The “voice of another + wise attention” formula is philosophically important.

Right view arises through:
- external input/teaching/dialogue,
- internally appropriate examination.

This blocks two extremes:
- blind acceptance of authority;
- the idea that truth must be discovered without instruction, community or inherited teaching.

SN 45.49 adds good friendship as a forerunner of the path.

### 5. AN 10.61: liberation has a wider ecology of conditions

AN 10.61 gives a long support sequence:
good association → hearing true teaching → confidence → wise attention → mindfulness/full awareness → sense restraint → good conduct → four satipaṭṭhānas → seven awakening factors → knowledge and freedom.

This shows that knowledge/freedom is not produced by one isolated meditation technique. Social, cognitive, ethical and contemplative conditions form a support ecology.

Parallel status for the exact full sequence was not completed in this pass; do not treat every link as cross-tradition proven yet.

### 6. SN 46.53: practice is adaptive, not “maximize one factor”

When the mind is sluggish:
- investigation, energy and rapture are appropriate;
- tranquility, concentration and equanimity are not the timely emphasis.

When the mind is restless:
- tranquility, concentration and equanimity are appropriate;
- investigation, energy and rapture are not the timely emphasis.

Mindfulness is said to be useful throughout.

This is direct evidence against a mechanical method that applies the same mental quality at maximum strength regardless of state.

Cross-tradition verification of this exact balancing discourse remains pending.

### 7. SN 12.23 / AN 11.2: knowledge emerges from conditioned cultivation

SN 12.23 presents:
suffering → confidence → joy → rapture → tranquility → pleasure → concentration → true knowing-and-seeing → disenchantment → dispassion → freedom → knowledge of ending.

AN 11.2 gives a closely related positive sequence beginning from skillful ethics/no regret and proceeding through joy, tranquility and concentration toward knowing, disenchantment, dispassion and knowledge/vision of freedom.

Important implication:
- concentration does not replace discernment;
- discernment does not float free from trained affective/ethical conditions;
- positive affective states such as joy, rapture, tranquility and pleasure can be conditions of liberating development.

### 8. Satipaṭṭhāna and awakening factors are linked

AN 10.61 explicitly makes the four satipaṭṭhānas nutriment/support for the seven awakening factors, which in turn support knowledge and freedom.

MN 10/DN 22 include observation of the awakening factors within satipaṭṭhāna itself:
one knows whether each factor is present, absent, arising or being developed.

This reinforces a monitoring-and-adjustment interpretation of mindfulness practice.

## SYNTHESIS — PRACTICE AS A FEEDBACK CULTIVATION SYSTEM

A non-canonical working model:

external teaching / good friendship
→ wise attention: frame the right causal question
→ right view: diagnose what leads to suffering/cessation
↔ right effort: abandon/develop
↔ right mindfulness: remember/monitor the task
↔ ethics + sense restraint: shape the field of experience
↔ concentration: stabilize/integrate the mind
↔ investigation / awakening factors: dynamically balance the current state
→ knowing-and-seeing
→ disenchantment
→ dispassion
→ release

Feedback is crucial:
- mindfulness notices state;
- view interprets relevance;
- effort adjusts;
- awakening factors are rebalanced;
- concentration improves observation;
- observation revises the next response.

This model is an explanatory synthesis. No single sutta states it in this diagram.

## MILINDA STATUS

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE — virtue as basis
Mil 3.1.9 describes virtue as the basis on which faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom and awakening factors develop. This fits the early-text pattern that ethical conduct supports concentration and awakening.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE — mindfulness as retaining the task
Mil 3.1.13 describes mindfulness as recalling/discriminating helpful vs harmful, right vs wrong qualities and sustaining what should be developed. This matches the active, task-oriented role of mindfulness in MN 117 better than a purely passive “bare noticing” model.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE / LATER SYSTEMATIZATION — wisdom cuts
Mil 3.1.15 and related passages characterize wisdom as cutting defilements and illuminating the truths. This is compatible with the earlier distinction between attention and transformative discernment, but the sharply separated functional definitions are later systematization.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “The eightfold path is a rigid eight-step ladder.” — MN 117 and its parallels show both ordering and collaboration.
2. “Mindfulness alone is the Buddhist method.” — right mindfulness operates with view, effort, ethics, concentration and the awakening factors.
3. “Mindfulness is only nonjudgmental present-moment noticing.” — too narrow for the path texts, which give it a task-retaining and discriminative role.
4. “Concentration by itself is liberating.” — noble right concentration is defined in relation to the other path factors; SN 12.23 places concentration before, not instead of, knowing-and-seeing.
5. “Wise attention means thinking endlessly about metaphysical self questions.” — MN 2 treats many such identity questions as inappropriate attention and redirects attention to suffering/origin/cessation/path.
6. “The path is purely individual introspection.” — good friendship, hearing the teaching and the voice of another are explicit conditions.
7. “Ethics is just a preliminary that can be discarded once meditation begins.” — AN 10.61 and the awakening-factor material retain ethics/sense restraint as upstream supports.
8. “Practice means suppressing affect.” — joy, rapture, tranquility and pleasure can function as positive conditions in the path.

## CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- right concentration is integrated with the other path factors;
- right view, effort and mindfulness collaborate rather than functioning only as isolated sequential steps;
- wise attention is a major condition for right view/path development;
- right view depends on both external teaching and internal examination;
- concentration supports knowing-and-seeing, which supports disenchantment/dispassion/release.

**TEXTUALLY STRONG IN PĀLI; PARALLEL AUDIT PENDING**
- the exact adaptive balancing instructions of SN 46.53;
- the exact full support ecology of AN 10.61.

**EBT_PROBABLE / SYNTHESIS**
- the path is best understood as a feedback cultivation system rather than either a rigid ladder or an unordered bundle of practices.

## PRACTICAL MEANING

A practice question should not be only:
> “Am I mindful?”

It should become:
- What is the mind’s present condition?
- What frame am I using to understand it?
- Is this frame reducing or feeding suffering?
- Does the mind currently need more investigation/energy, or more tranquility/stability?
- Is ethical conduct making the mind easier or harder to steady?
- Am I remembering the task, or merely watching experience without direction?
- Is concentration making causal patterns clearer, or becoming an end to possess?

A key practical hypothesis:
**the path trains a capacity to diagnose, regulate, stabilize and understand experience without turning the training itself into a new identity.**
