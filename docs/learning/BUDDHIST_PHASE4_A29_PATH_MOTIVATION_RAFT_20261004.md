# PHASE 4 — A29 — PATH MOTIVATION IS A RAFT, NOT A NEW SELF

**Status:** CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Question

When wholesome motivation succeeds, what happens to the motivation itself? Does the path use desire/effort as a tool that is eventually relinquished, and how does this avoid constructing a new identity such as "I am a great practitioner"?

## Primary-text findings

### SN 51.15
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: Ānanda answers the objection that desire cannot be abandoned by means of desire. He uses the example of wanting to go to a park: once one arrives, the corresponding desire, energy, intention, and inquiry subside.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: for an arahant, the prior desire, energy, mental application, and inquiry directed toward arahantship have subsided once the task is completed.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: path motivation is instrumental and finite; it is not a possession to carry after its function has been completed.

### AN 4.159
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: Ānanda says that, in dependence on craving, craving can be abandoned. The example is hearing that another monk attained liberation, forming the aspiration to attain it oneself, and later abandoning craving after realization.
- `LEXICAL_GUARDRAIL`: this passage itself uses taṇhā-language instrumentally. Therefore the safer rule is not "all taṇhā-like motivation is identical" but "the path can provisionally make use of a motivating state whose function ends when the goal is reached."
- Do not generalize AN 4.159 into permission to cultivate ordinary sensual, becoming, or identity craving.

### MN 22 — raft simile
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the Dhamma is compared to a raft used for crossing, not for carrying around after crossing.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the discourse explicitly says even teachings are to be relinquished, much more what is not the teaching.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: correct method can become wrong grasp if converted from a means into an object of possession.

### SN 22.59
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are to be seen as "not mine, not I, not my self."
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: spiritual effort, attainments, knowledge, and meditative states occur within conditioned processes and should not be appropriated into a permanent practitioner-identity.

## Main conclusion

`CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`:
The path has a self-consuming structure. Motivation, effort, teachings, views, and practices are used because they perform a liberating function. When that function is fulfilled, clinging to the means would recreate the very appropriation the means were meant to undo. The path is therefore not "acquire a better self"; it is "use skillful conditions to end the conditions of grasping, then do not grasp the tools."

## Identity trap

A practice can start wholesomely:
- "I want to understand suffering."
and later turn into:
- "I am someone who understands more deeply than others."
- "My attainment is who I am."
- "My doctrine is mine and must win."

This is not a single canonical formula; it is a project diagnostic derived from the raft simile plus non-appropriation teachings.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer

### Mil 7.3.8
- `MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC`: Nāgasena praises continuous zeal, effort, wise attention, discrimination of wholesome/unwholesome states, and restraint of the mind.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: Mi Tiên preserves intense effort while the early-text material above supplies the crucial terminal condition: effort serves liberation and does not become a permanent identity or possession.

## Guardrails

- Do not use "let go of the raft" as an excuse to abandon ethics, concentration, or wisdom before their function is fulfilled.
- Do not say "all teachings are useless"; MN 22 says the raft is useful precisely because it gets one across.
- Do not turn non-attachment to practice into laziness or anti-intellectualism.
- Do not infer that every provisional motivation is wholesome merely because it is "for practice."
- Milindapañha remains a later dialectical layer.
