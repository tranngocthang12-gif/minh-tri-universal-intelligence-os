# PHASE 4 — A15 — FROM RIGHT VIEW TO KNOWING-AND-SEEING

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: What distinguishes conceptually correct understanding from yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana / seeing with right wisdom that actually weakens craving, clinging, and the residual 'I am' tendency?

MN 9:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right View is repeatedly framed as understanding a phenomenon, its origin, its cessation, and the practice leading to cessation.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: this understanding is tied not merely to description but to abandoning greed/aversion tendencies, uprooting the view-and-conceit 'I am', ending ignorance, giving rise to knowledge, and ending suffering in this life.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: correct doctrine becomes liberative Right View when it functions causally in the path, not merely when it is verbally memorized.

SN 12.23:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: immersion/concentration conditions truly knowing-and-seeing; truly knowing-and-seeing conditions disillusionment; disillusionment conditions dispassion; dispassion conditions freedom.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the sequence gives a functional test for mature understanding: it changes affective orientation toward conditioned phenomena and leads downstream toward dispassion and release.

SN 22.59:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the aggregates are to be seen yathābhūtaṁ sammappaññāya as 'not mine, not I, not my self'.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: seeing thus leads to disenchantment, dispassion, liberation, and knowledge of liberation.
- Guardrail: do not reduce yathābhūta seeing to a slogan such as 'there is no self' detached from the aggregate analysis and the downstream transformation.

SN 22.89:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Khemaka can refrain from taking any aggregate as self or belonging to self while a residual 'I am' conceit/desire/tendency still remains.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: sustained contemplation of the arising and passing away of the five clinging aggregates uproots that residual 'I am' tendency.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this passage directly shows that correct explicit belief can precede a deeper restructuring of habitual selfing; propositional assent and uprooting of latent conceit are not identical.

MN 117:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Right View works together with Right Effort and Right Mindfulness; it identifies what is wrong/right while effort abandons/develops and mindfulness monitors the process.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: mature Right View is embedded in an active training system rather than standing alone as an abstract opinion.

MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC:
- Nāgasena describes wisdom as cutting/severing and illuminating: when wisdom arises it dispels the darkness of ignorance and makes the Noble Truths plain, illustrated by a lamp in a dark house.
- This is a later dialectical sharpening of the functional difference between possessing information and wisdom that changes how things are seen.
- Guardrail: use the lamp analogy as Milindapañha reasoning, not as an early-Nikāya quotation.

Main conclusion:
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Early Buddhist thought distinguishes at least functionally between knowing the correct teaching and a knowing-and-seeing that reorganizes one's relation to experience. The latter is marked not simply by stronger conviction but by downstream effects: disenchantment, dispassion, weakening of appropriation, uprooting of residual 'I am', and liberation. The texts do not require us to define this as a wholly nonconceptual mystical event; the safe claim is that liberating insight is transformative and causally efficacious, not merely verbally correct.

Important guardrails:
- Do not equate memorized doctrine with liberating insight.
- Do not equate liberating insight with a contentless experience; the texts still describe what is known and seen.
- Do not infer that one flash of insight always completes the path; SN 22.89 explicitly shows residual 'I am' can remain after substantial doctrinal correction.
