# EARLY BUDDHIST HARD CLUSTER v0.8 — 2026-10-04

**Status:** CONSCIOUSNESS/REBIRTH + SAMATHA/VIPASSANA + VIEW-GRASPING STRESS TEST
**Boundary:** Stronger reconstruction of early-Buddhist reasoning. No claim of complete canon mastery and no claim that one passage supplies a complete metaphysics of rebirth.

## 1. Consciousness and rebirth — what the texts strongly reject

### MN 38 and MĀ 201
Both witnesses preserve the same central error:
- a monk claims that **this same consciousness** goes on to rebirth without changing;
- the claim is explicitly rejected;
- consciousness is said to arise dependent on conditions;
- without its conditions it does not arise / ceases.

MĀ 201 is particularly explicit:
- consciousness arises because of conditions;
- eye + form -> eye-consciousness, and similarly for the other sense bases;
- consciousness is compared functionally to fire named according to its fuel/condition.

### High-confidence conclusion
Early-Buddhist consciousness must **not** be modeled as:
- an immortal soul;
- an unchanged subject;
- a metaphysical witness that carries personal identity unchanged across lives.

Evidence status:
**EARLY_MULTI_PARALLEL_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.**

## 2. SN 12.12 / SĀ 372 — replace "who?" with "under what conditions?"

The Moḷiyaphagguna dialogue is methodologically decisive.

Repeated questions such as:
- who experiences consciousness-food?
- who contacts?
- who feels?
- who craves?

are rejected because they presuppose an enduring subject.

The discourse replaces:
**WHO DOES IT?**
with:
**WHAT CONDITIONS IT?**

### Reasoning update
When a question about rebirth or experience is grammatically built around a hidden carrier-self:
1. detect the carrier assumption;
2. refuse to reify an agent merely because grammar asks for one;
3. answer through conditional sequence instead.

This is not denial that events occur.
It is a shift from substance grammar to process grammar.

## 3. SN 12.38 / SĀ 359 — continuity through conditioned establishment

SN 12.38 and its SĀ parallel connect:
- intending;
- planning/mental preoccupation;
with:
- an object/support for consciousness;
- establishment/growth of consciousness;
- future renewed existence;
- future birth, aging and death.

### Strong inference
The texts do describe continuity toward future existence.

### Equally strong guard
They do not thereby say:
- an unchanging consciousness-self travels intact.

### Working model
**causal continuity without identity-substance.**

This is the current best text-grounded reconstruction.

## 4. DN 15 — the hardest tension: consciousness "descends into the womb"

DN 15 says, in its causal analysis:
- if consciousness did not descend into the mother's womb, name-and-form would not take shape;
- if consciousness departed after descent, name-and-form would not develop;
- consciousness and name-and-form are mutually conditioning within the relevant domain.

This creates a surface tension with MN 38 if one reads "consciousness descends" as:
> the same permanent consciousness travels from one body to another.

That reading is blocked by MN 38/MĀ 201.

### Current resolution
Distinguish:
- **rebirth-conditioning function of consciousness**;
from:
- **numerical identity of an unchanged consciousness-substance**.

DN 15 supports the former.
MN 38/MĀ 201 reject the latter.

### Remaining uncertainty
The exact mechanism connecting one life-process to the next is not fully specified by one stable cross-traditional formula.
Different early passages use:
- consciousness;
- gandhabba / related conception language;
- intention and establishment of consciousness;
- mutual consciousness/name-and-form conditioning.

Therefore:
**DO NOT fill the gap with a soul, ālayavijñāna, bhavaṅga, or any later theory unless explicitly marked as later interpretation.**

Contradiction status:
**PARTIALLY RESOLVED — IDENTITY ERROR CLOSED; COMPLETE REBIRTH MECHANISM STILL UNDERDETERMINED.**

## 5. Translation card refinement — viññāṇa

The term must now carry explicit flags.

### DO NOT TRANSLATE AS
- "the consciousness" if that wording suggests one enduring entity;
- "soul";
- "true awareness".

### Use context tags
- SENSE_CONSCIOUSNESS;
- AGGREGATE;
- DEPENDENT_ORIGINATION_LINK;
- REBIRTH_CONDITIONING_FUNCTION;
- MEDITATIVE_CONSCIOUSNESS_FIELD;
- CONTESTED_SPECIAL_USAGE.

### Rule
The lemma `viññāṇa` by itself proves neither:
- permanent identity;
nor
- a fully worked-out no-continuity theory.

Context decides.

## 6. Jhāna and insight — no single rigid order

### Gradual-training family
Many early discourses present:
ethics -> restraint -> mindfulness -> abandoning hindrances -> jhāna -> knowledge/insight -> release.

This is a major and repeated pedagogical sequence.

### MN 64
Jhāna (and some formless attainments) is explicitly used as a basis for insight:
- phenomena present there are regarded as impermanent/stressful/not-self;
- the mind inclines toward the deathless.

### MN 111
Sāriputta analyzes mental qualities associated with successive jhānas and higher attainments.
The text demonstrates **insight operating in intimate relation to deep collectedness**, though details differ by attainment and may include review after emerging.

### AN 4.170
Four routes are listed for arahantship:
1. serenity before insight;
2. insight before serenity;
3. serenity and insight yoked together;
4. a case where Dhamma-agitation settles and the mind becomes unified, after which the path develops.

### Strong conclusion
The early texts do **not** support one universal claim:
> "insight must always come only after jhāna."

Nor do they support the opposite universal claim:
> "jhāna/serenity is dispensable."

### Better model
**samatha and vipassanā are mutually required capacities whose developmental sequencing can vary.**

The repeated gradual-training sequence remains important.
AN 4.170 prevents turning that pedagogical sequence into a single mandatory chronology for every practitioner.

Contradiction status:
**RESOLVED AS MULTIPLE DEVELOPMENTAL ROUTES WITH SHARED PATH INTEGRATION.**

## 7. Translation card refinement — samatha / vipassanā

### samatha
Do not reduce to:
- relaxation;
- suppression;
- "concentration technique".

Working function:
**settling, unification, calming/stabilizing of mind.**

### vipassanā
Do not reduce to:
- one modern meditation brand;
- bare observation.

Working function:
**clear seeing / discernment of phenomena in ways that support dispassion and release.**

### Rule
The pair describes capacities/functions.
A modern technique-name must not be projected backward as if identical with all early usages.

## 8. Aṭṭhakavagga vs right view — the apparent contradiction

### Aṭṭhakavagga
Snp 4.8, 4.12, 4.13 repeatedly criticize:
- "only this is true" identity;
- debate for victory;
- defining others as fools;
- attachment to what is seen/heard/thought;
- taking a view as a basis for superiority and purification.

### MN 117 and related path material
Right view is:
- an indispensable path factor;
- active discrimination of wrong/right;
- tied to ethical causality;
- coordinated with effort and mindfulness.

### Bad resolutions to reject
1. "The Buddha had no views."
2. "All propositions are equally false."
3. "Right view is just another dogma."
4. "Aṭṭhakavagga must be dismissed because it conflicts with the Nikāyas."

## 9. Working resolution — diṭṭhi content vs diṭṭhi appropriation

A crucial distinction:

### RIGHT VIEW AS FUNCTION
- sees causal/ethical structure;
- detects wrong view;
- guides action and training;
- supports release.

### VIEW AS APPROPRIATION
- "this is mine";
- "this makes me superior";
- "only my formulation purifies";
- argument becomes identity-defense;
- conceptual victory replaces liberation.

Snp 4.12 even says there is **one truth, not a second**, while criticizing the many self-proclaimed truths created through view-fixation.

Thus Aṭṭhakavagga is not straightforward relativism.

### Remaining tension
The rhetorical intensity of "not taking a position" in the Aṭṭhakavagga may reflect:
- genre;
- audience;
- an early stratum emphasizing non-grasping.

Do not erase that difference by pretending all texts use `diṭṭhi` in exactly one technical sense.

Contradiction status:
**PARTIALLY RESOLVED — FUNCTION/GRASPING DISTINCTION STRONG; STRATUM/GENRE DIFFERENCE PRESERVED.**

## 10. Philosophical update

The stronger current reconstruction:

> Early Buddhism does not solve continuity by inventing a permanent bearer, does not solve meditation by enforcing one universal sequence, and does not solve dogmatism by abandoning truth.

Instead:
- continuity is causal;
- identity-substance is rejected;
- serenity and insight integrate flexibly;
- right view functions as a path instrument;
- grasping at view is itself diagnosed as a cause of conflict/bondage.

## 11. New safeguards

### NO-SOUL-BY-GRAMMAR
Never infer an enduring entity merely because a sentence uses a subject noun.

### NO-GAP-FILLING
Where rebirth mechanism is textually underdetermined, do not import a later theory silently.

### NO-SEQUENCE-DOGMATISM
A repeated training sequence is evidence of a standard pedagogical path, not proof of a single universal chronology.

### NO-RELATIVISM-FROM-ANTI-DOGMATISM
Criticism of view-grasping does not erase right/wrong discrimination.

### NO-MODERN-TECHNIQUE BACKPROJECTION
Modern samatha/vipassanā method brands are not identical to every early textual occurrence.
