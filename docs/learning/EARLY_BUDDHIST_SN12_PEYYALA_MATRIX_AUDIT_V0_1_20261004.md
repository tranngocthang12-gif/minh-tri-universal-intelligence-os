## 3. Major reasoning result

The collection ends not with a new metaphysical doctrine, but with a **training matrix**.

This matters:
Dependent origination is not presented only as:
"a theory of twelve links."

It is treated as:
- an object of diagnosis;
- something to know in terms of origin and cessation;
- a field requiring teacher/training/desire/energy/mindfulness/clear comprehension/heedfulness.

### New synthesis
**CAUSAL KNOWLEDGE REQUIRES TRAINING CONDITIONS.**

## 4. Sati and sampajañña are separate in the matrix

The sequence separately lists:
- sati;
- sampajañña.

Therefore:
do not translate both into one undifferentiated "awareness."

Working distinction:
- sati: keeping in mind / sustained mindful continuity;
- sampajañña: clear/situational comprehension of what is occurring and its significance.

The exact semantic boundary remains context-dependent.

## 5. Appamāda as capstone quality

The matrix culminates in appamāda.

Working function:
- non-negligence;
- heedfulness;
- sustained diligence in relation to the task.

This supports reading Buddhist practice as:
not merely momentary insight,
but disciplined continuity.

## 6. Methodological finding about repetition

A peyyāla is not low-value filler.

Its function can be:
- exhaustively cross-producting a principle;
- preventing a reader from restricting a rule to one favored factor;
- converting an insight into a systematic training matrix.

### MINH TRI rule
When a canonical passage is repetitive:
do not count repetitions as new doctrines;
but do not delete them as meaningless.
Ask what scope the matrix is enforcing.

## 7. Historical/comparative caution

Direct parallels are clearer for some entries (e.g. training/application) than for every expanded peyyāla unit.

Therefore:
- the Pali matrix is textually real;
- the exact 132-unit expansion is not automatically proof that the historical Buddha recited every repeated unit in its preserved redactional form.

Evidence label:

- exact full redactional matrix: SCHOOL/REDACTIONAL_FORM_UNCERTAIN.
