## Purpose

Integrate the first full study cycle:

DUKKHA → PERCEPTION → SELFING → CRAVING → DEPENDENT ARISING → KNOWLEDGE → PRACTICE → LIBERATION

without turning the synthesis into a closed doctrine.

The central methodological rule remains:

**TEXT ≠ INTERPRETATION ≠ SYNTHESIS.**

This document is the third category.

---

# 1. DUKKHA — the governing problem

The first architecture is not organized around “What substance is reality made of?” but around:

- what is dukkha/stress/suffering,
- what conditions it,
- whether it can cease,
- what practice leads to cessation.

SN 56.11 frames the middle way and four truths around:
- dukkha,
- origin,
- cessation,
- path.

This is the governing problem that gives the other analyses their practical direction.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** the Buddha's teaching is fundamentally diagnostic and therapeutic in structure, but should not be reduced to modern “therapy” or mere psychological comfort.

---

# 2. PERCEPTION — experience is analyzed as conditioned process

The early-text analyses repeatedly distinguish functions such as:

sense base + object
→ consciousness
→ contact
→ feeling
→ perception
→ thinking
→ papañca-related expansion

MN 18 and its MĀ/EĀ parallels support the broad cognitive sequence from sense contact through feeling/perception toward conceptual proliferation.

MN 38 and its parallels reject an invariant consciousness that transmigrates unchanged.

SN 12 material further complicates any simple linear model because consciousness and name-and-form can be mutually conditioning.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** cognition is conditioned and processual, not evidence for a fixed inner knower.

**OPEN:** a single universal cognitive micro-sequence should not be imposed across all suttas.

---

# 3. SELFING — “self” is not exhausted by one explicit belief

SN 22.89 / SĀ 103 shows:
- explicit self-view can be absent,
- residual “I am” conceit/desire/tendency can remain.

SN 35.248 / SĀ 1168 connects self-referential thought with:
- maññanā/conceiving,
- māna/comparison,
- papañca-related activity.

Aṭṭhakavagga material links:
- equal/superior/inferior self-positioning with dispute,
- desire with possessiveness/mine-making,
- perception with papañca-related constructions.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** selfing includes more than a proposition “a self exists.”

It includes overlapping processes:
- “I am,”
- “this is mine,”
- comparison,
- identity positioning,
- view appropriation,
- future self-projection.

**EBT_PROBABLE / SYNTHESIS:** selfing is better modeled as a recursive network than as a single downstream event.

---

# 4. CRAVING — feeling does not mechanically equal craving

MN 148 and SN 36 material show:
- pleasant feeling can be relished/held, feeding passion;
- painful feeling can be resisted, feeding aversion;
- neutral feeling can be misunderstood, feeding ignorance;
- trained non-reactivity is possible.

Thus:

**vedanā conditions taṇhā**
does not mean
**every feeling inevitably produces craving.**

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** feeling and reactive craving must be distinguished.

A practical intervention becomes intelligible:
feelings can occur without the same conditioned reaction being fed.

This makes dependent arising non-fatalistic.

---

# 5. DEPENDENT ARISING — the master method is conditionality, not substance

SN 12.15 rejects the polarity:
- “everything exists,”
- “everything does not exist,”

and instead teaches arising and cessation through conditions.

The standard formula links:
ignorance → formations → consciousness → name-and-form → six senses → contact → feeling → craving → clinging → becoming → birth → dukkha.

But other SN 12 texts show reciprocal structures such as:
consciousness ↔ name-and-form.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** dependent arising is a causal/conditional analysis of arising and cessation, not a deterministic doctrine and not a disguised eternal-substance theory.

**EBT_STRONG:** the formula includes a real rebirth/renewed-existence dimension.

**CAUTION:** do not collapse all links into a millisecond psychological chain, and do not limit them only to a rigid three-life scheme.

---

# 6. KNOWLEDGE — right view is functional and self-correcting

MN 9 repeatedly uses an analytical structure:
- know a phenomenon/problem,
- know its origin,
- know its cessation,
- know the path to cessation.

MN 117 makes right view a forerunner and coordinates it with:
- right effort,
- right mindfulness.

At the same time, view-clinging (diṭṭhupādāna) is itself something to abandon.

SN 22.89 shows that conceptual correction alone is insufficient; arising and disappearance must be seen deeply enough to remove residual “I am” tendencies.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** Buddhism does not reject all views.

It distinguishes:
- wrong view,
- right view used as path,
- clinging to view as possession/identity.

**EBT_PROBABLE:** right view is best understood as a self-transcending diagnostic tool whose success eventually undermines the clinging mechanism that could appropriate it.

---

# 7. PRACTICE — the path behaves like an interactive cultivation system

MN 117 shows right view, right effort and right mindfulness working together.

Wise attention (yoniso manasikāra), external teaching/good friendship, ethics, sense restraint, mindfulness, concentration and awakening factors form a wider ecology of training.

SN 12.23 gives:
concentration → knowing-and-seeing → disenchantment → dispassion → release.

The path therefore cannot be reduced to:
- mindfulness alone,
- concentration alone,
- intellectual study alone,
- morality alone.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** liberation is cultivated through interacting ethical, cognitive, attentional and concentrative conditions.

**EBT_PROBABLE / SYNTHESIS:** the path is better modeled as a feedback cultivation system than as either a rigid staircase or an unordered bag of techniques.

---

# 8. LIBERATION — what ceases is textually clearer than what “substance Nibbāna is”

SN 43 groups Nibbāna with:
- ending of greed,
- hatred,
- delusion,
- taṇhakkhaya,
- virāga,
- vimutti,
- asaṅkhata,
- amata.

Udāna 8.3 speaks of the unborn/unbecome/unmade/unconditioned.

SN 44 refuses the four postmortem predicates:
- exists,
- does not exist,
- both,
- neither.

MN 26 presents Nibbāna as a goal to be directly realized.

### Current conclusion
**EBT_STRONG:** the safest description of Nibbāna begins functionally:
the ending of greed, hate, delusion, craving, clinging and renewed becoming.

**EBT_STRONG:** it is described as unconditioned and can be realized while alive.

**OPEN:** a complete ontology of the unconditioned goes beyond what the current audit securely establishes.

---

# 9. THE FIRST FULL WORKING ARCHITECTURE

The synthesis now looks like this:

### A. Experience occurs dependently
There is no need to posit an invariant knower behind every event.

### B. Feeling gives the process affective direction
Pleasant / painful / neutral do not yet equal bondage.

### C. Ignorance + craving + appropriation make experience personally binding
The mind begins:
- wanting,
- resisting,
- making “mine,”
- construing “I,”
- comparing,
- defending positions.

### D. Papañca and identity can recursively amplify the process
Past, future, social comparison, views and self-narratives can feed renewed feeling, craving and conflict.

### E. Dukkha persists because its conditions persist
No permanent substance is needed to carry the process.

### F. Practice changes conditions
Ethics, wise attention, right view, effort, mindfulness, concentration and discernment progressively weaken the reinforcing loops.

### G. Liberating knowledge sees conditioned arising/cessation without appropriation
This is not merely adopting the proposition “no self.”

### H. Nibbāna is the ending of the binding causal drivers
Greed, hate, delusion, craving, clinging and renewed becoming no longer continue in the old way.

# 10. CENTRAL THESIS — CURRENT WORKING FORM

A cautious formulation of the Buddha's thought architecture:

> Suffering is not best understood as the property of a permanent self but as a conditioned process. Sensory and mental experience arises through conditions; feeling becomes a critical junction at which craving, resistance and ignorance may be reinforced. Through craving, appropriation, comparison, conceptual proliferation and clinging, experience is repeatedly organized as “I” and “mine,” producing conflict and continued becoming. Because these processes are conditioned rather than immutable, they can be trained and brought to cessation. The path cultivates ethical conduct, appropriate attention, right view, mindfulness, effort, concentration and discernment so that arising and cessation are known directly. Liberation is the fading of passion, ending of clinging and ignorance, and cessation of the conditions that perpetuate suffering and renewed becoming.

**Status:** SYNTHESIS / EBT-COMPATIBLE / NOT A CANONICAL QUOTATION.

# 11. WHAT THIS ARCHITECTURE REJECTS

The current evidence does not support reducing the Buddha's thought to any one of these:

1. **nihilism:** “there is no person, nothing matters”;
2. **eternalism:** “an unchanged consciousness/self survives behind experience”;
3. **fatalism:** “past kamma determines everything now”;
4. **anti-thinking:** “all thought/view is bad”;
5. **mindfulness-only practice:** “bare noticing alone is the entire path”;
6. **pure intellectualism:** “correct beliefs alone liberate”;
7. **pure absorptionism:** “concentration alone is liberation”;
8. **annihilationism about Nibbāna:** “the person is simply destroyed into nothing”;
9. **reification of Nibbāna:** “Nibbāna is a cosmic place or eternal personal soul.”

Each rejection has different levels of textual support; they should not be treated as one undifferentiated claim.

# 12. ROLE OF MILINDAPAÑHA AFTER ROUND 1

The Milindapañha has proven most useful as a **clarifying stress-test**, not a primary historical-Buddha source.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE examples
- chariot: conventional designation without invariant essence;
- continuity without strict identity;
- bodily pain without the same mental bondage;
- taste without lust;
- distinction between attention and wisdom;
- Nibbāna not spatially located;
- cause/path for realization ≠ cause producing Nibbāna.

### MILINDA_EXTENSION
- detailed taxonomies of mental functions;
- polished ontological arguments about Nibbāna;
- precise epistemic claims about how Nibbāna is cognized;
- more systematic explanatory schemas than the suttas.

Rule preserved:
**Milinda clarifies; early discourses and parallels constrain.**

# 13. OPEN / TENSION REGISTER

These questions remain open and must survive the synthesis:

### A. Papañca
- exact semantic core;
- relation to maññanā, saññā, taṇhā and conflict;
- whether any unified causal role can be reconstructed without over-systematizing.

### B. Consciousness and name-and-form
- exact scope of reciprocal conditionality;
- relation between sense consciousness, rebirth-linking interpretations and broader dependent-arising usages.

### C. Bhava
- exact semantic range: existence/becoming/rebirth process;
- how far present-moment identity “becoming” is textually grounded versus interpretive application.

### D. Nibbāna
- exact ontology of asaṅkhata;
- relation between nibbāna/parinibbāna across textual strata;
- meaning and textual history of positive epithets such as amata.

### E. Textual stratification
- which formulas are common across Pāli/Āgama/Sanskrit witnesses;
- which are Pāli-specific expansions;
- relative antiquity of Aṭṭhakavagga formulations versus later systematizations.

### F. Rebirth
- how early texts conceptualize causal continuity across lives without invariant self;
- exact role of viññāṇa, kamma, craving and becoming;
- where later Abhidhamma explanations add structure not explicit in the suttas.

# 14. HISTORICAL-BUDDHA CONFIDENCE MAP

### Highest current confidence
- dukkha/origin/cessation/path architecture;
- conditional arising and cessation;
- no-self/de-identification from aggregates;
- consciousness as conditioned rather than invariant;
- feeling/craving distinction;
- craving/clinging as central causal drivers;
- ethical and meditative path;
- right view as necessary but not an identity to cling to;
- liberation as ending greed/hate/delusion and clinging.

### Medium / requires continuing parallel-text audit
- exact psychology of papañca;
- precise recursive “selfing network” reconstruction;
- exact semantic range of bhava;
- fine-grained path-factor interaction;
- two-Nibbāna-elements taxonomy.

### Low / OPEN
- a complete metaphysical ontology of Nibbāna;
- a single universal moment-by-moment map of mind;
