## 2. Model survived: conditional anti-reification

MN 38 directly attacks the view that the very same consciousness transmigrates unchanged. The discourse frames consciousness as dependently arisen and named according to its condition.

This strongly supports:
- consciousness is not a hidden permanent carrier-self;
- causal continuity does not require identity of an unchanging substance;
- "what continues?" should be reframed through conditional process rather than metaphysical identity.

This strengthens, rather than weakens, the v0.2 anti-reification model.

## 3. Model correction A — phenomenology must not become idealism

SN 35.23 defines "the all" in terms of:
- eye/forms;
- ear/sounds;
- nose/odors;
- tongue/tastes;
- body/touches;
- mind/mental phenomena.

A naive synthesis could turn this into:
> Buddhism teaches that only subjective experience exists.

That claim is too strong.

Correct operational interpretation:
- the discourse establishes the complete domain relevant to sensory-cognitive experience and the path analysis;
- it blocks an allegedly knowable "other all" outside that domain;
- it does not license modern idealist metaphysics without further argument.

### Revision
Change "phenomenological" to:
**EXPERIENTIAL-DOMAIN DISCIPLINE WITHOUT ONTOLOGICAL OVERREACH.**

## 4. Model correction B — mindfulness is not passive non-judgment

MN 19 actively sorts thought into two ethical-causal classes:
- sensuality / ill will / cruelty;
- renunciation / good will / harmlessness.

The evaluation criterion is causal:
- harms self/others;
- obstructs wisdom;
- leads toward distress or release.

MN 20 then gives a graded toolbox for stubborn unwholesome thought:
1. switch to a skillful theme;
2. examine the drawbacks;
3. deliberately withdraw attention;
4. relax the fabrication/process producing the thought;
5. as last resort, forcibly restrain the mind.

### Revision
The early Buddhist mind-training model is not:
> observe everything neutrally and never intervene.

It is:
> know accurately, classify by causal/ethical effect, and intervene with the least forceful effective method.

This also converges with MN 2's multi-method treatment architecture.

## 5. Model correction C — Buddhist ethics is not reducible to stress management

MN 21 pushes non-hostility beyond ordinary therapeutic comfort.

The standard is not merely:
- "do what reduces your stress."

It demands:
- no hateful speech;
- no inner hate;
- maintaining goodwill even under extreme abuse.

The simile of the saw deliberately sets a boundary case.

### Revision
The system must model Buddhist ethics as:
**transformative virtue with non-hatred as a constitutive goal**, not merely instrumental emotion regulation.

## 6. MN 1 — conceiving and appropriation are deeper than simple perception

MN 1 distinguishes:
- perceiving an object as such;
- conceiving about it / in relation to it / as it / as mine;
- delighting in it.

The repeated Pali family around `maññati` is difficult and should not be flattened to one modern psychological term.

The structural point is more secure than any one translation:
- perception is followed by conceptual appropriation;
- appropriation and delight bind the ordinary person;
- deeper understanding changes the relation to the same domain of experience.

### Translation caution
Vietnamese and English renderings of the `maññati` formulas vary substantially.
This text is therefore a high-priority translation-card case.

## 7. MN 72 + SN 44.10 — category discipline, not evasiveness

MN 72 rejects the standard speculative positions and uses the extinguished-fire simile.

SN 44.10 explains why a simple yes/no answer about the self's survival would mislead:
- one answer aligns with eternalism;
- the opposite aligns with annihilationism;
- the questioner's current confusion must also be considered.

### Revision
The response model now distinguishes:
1. **FALSE-BINARY REFRAME**;
2. **CATEGORY-NONAPPLICABILITY**;
3. **PEDAGOGICAL NON-ANSWER**;
4. **ORDINARY UNKNOWN**.

These must never be conflated.

## 8. MN 26 — core quest stable, narrative frame variable

MN 26 presents the noble search:
- beings subject to birth, aging, sickness and death seek what is likewise subject to them;
- the noble search seeks the unborn/unaging/deathless release.

Its Madhyama-āgama parallel MĀ 204 preserves the core quest but differs in important narrative material, notably the Brahmā invitation episode.

### Historical-critical lesson
Do not infer:
- every narrative detail in one transmission is equally early;
- doctrinal core and narrative frame have identical historical confidence.

### Revision
For historical claims, separate:
- CORE DOCTRINAL STRUCTURE;
- NARRATIVE WRAPPER;
- SECTARIAN/TRANSMISSION VARIANT.

## 9. MN 39 — truthfulness of identity claims

MN 39 begins with a role claim:
- people call you ascetics;
- therefore practice qualities that make the designation true.

This adds a new reasoning dimension:
**identity words create standards of conduct.**

The path is then gradual:
- moral shame/compunction;
- purified conduct;
- contentment;
- sense restraint;
- mindfulness;
- abandoning hindrances;
- collectedness/jhāna;
- knowledge and release.

### Revision
The Buddha's reasoning is not only deconstructive toward identity.
It can also use conventional identity normatively:
> if you claim a role, embody the qualities that make the label true.

## 10. MN 21 + MN 39 — ethics and training are gradual but uncompromising in direction

The training is staged:
- one does not begin at the final attainment.

But the direction is not relativistic:
- non-harm;
- non-hatred;
- truthful conduct;
- restraint;
- clarity;
- release.

### Operational rule
Be flexible about training sequence, but not vague about the direction of development.

## 11. Parallel-text findings

### MN 72
Chinese and Tibetan parallels support the antiquity of the broad Vacchagotta undeclared-question complex.

### MN 21
MĀ 193 is a close parallel. EĀ 50.8 is only partial and appears to merge material also resembling MN 22.

Lesson:
- parallel existence raises confidence in a core;
- partial/merged parallels warn against assuming discourse boundaries were fixed identically across traditions.

### MN 19
MĀ 102 preserves a parallel thought-training account, though details in the jhāna sequence differ in transmission.

### MN 26
MĀ 204 lacks the Brahmā invitation found in the Pali version.

Lesson:
- doctrinal reasoning can be more stable than narrative embellishment.

### MN 39
MĀ 182 supports the graduated-training pattern as cross-traditional rather than uniquely Theravada.

## 12. Pali-English-Vietnamese translation findings

### MN 1: `maññati`
English:
- conceives;
- thinks of;
- identifies in relation to.

Vietnamese:
- "nghĩ đến";
- "nghĩ là";
- "nghĩ của ta" style renderings.

Risk:
a single gloss can hide the progression from perception to appropriation.

### MN 38: `viññāṇa`
Do not render in a way that suggests a permanent soul-like consciousness.
The discourse itself makes consciousness condition-dependent.

### MN 20: active control vocabulary
Modern "mindfulness" vocabulary can understate how active the text is.
The sequence includes replacement, drawback analysis, withdrawal of attention, relaxation of fabrication, and forceful restraint as last resort.

### MN 72: fire simile
Do not infer from "extinguished" a simplistic annihilationist metaphysics.
The discourse's point is that ordinary directional/existence predicates fail after the fuel-condition is gone.

## 13. Revised Buddha-reasoning architecture

The current stronger model:

1. Identify the actual problem.
2. Separate observable process from metaphysical story.
3. Detect appropriation / identity investment.
4. Ask what conditions the process.
5. Identify causal and ethical consequences.
6. Check whether the question's categories are valid.
7. Match intervention to mechanism.
8. Use graded training rather than one universal technique.
9. Preserve strong ethical direction: non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion, non-harm.
10. Distinguish doctrinal core from transmission-specific narrative.
11. Use conventional identity when useful, but test whether claimed roles are embodied.
12. End with a trainable or verifiable task.

## 14. What the adversarial cluster disproved

The following simplifications are now explicitly rejected:

- "Buddhism is just nonjudgmental awareness."
- "The Buddha taught idealism because the six senses are 'the all'."
- "The Buddha simply denied that a self exists."
- "Unanswered questions are questions the Buddha did not know."
- "Meditation is one generic technique."
- "Buddhist ethics is just reducing personal stress."
- "All canonical narrative details have equal historical status."
- "Identity language is always something Buddhism deconstructs."

## 17. Current conclusion

The adversarial cluster did not overturn the conditional/task-oriented model.
It made it stricter:

> Early Buddhist reasoning is conditional without reductionism, experiential without idealist overreach, ethically directive without dogmatism, psychologically interventionist without a single-technique ideology, and anti-reifying without collapsing into nihilism.

This remains a progressively tested reconstruction, not complete mastery of the canon.
