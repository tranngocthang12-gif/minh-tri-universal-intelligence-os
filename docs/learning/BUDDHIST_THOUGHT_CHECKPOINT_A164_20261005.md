## Central question

How should `nissaya / nissāya / anissita / anissaya` be understood across early Buddhist texts, and how can the Buddha teach Right View and a path while criticizing dependence on views, positions, and supports?

## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. MN 22 — `diṭṭhi-nissaya` is a harmful view-dependency

MN 22 asks whether there is any `diṭṭhi-nissaya`—a dependence/support based on view—such that sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair would not arise from it.

The answer is no.

This is direct evidence that:

- a view can function as a support/dependency;
- such dependency can become bound up with suffering;
- the problem is not merely that a proposition is cognitively present, but that one depends on it as a support.

**Guardrail:** `diṭṭhi-nissaya` is not equivalent to every occurrence of `diṭṭhi`. A view can be present without the text thereby saying it is a `nissaya`.

### 2. Snp 4.9 — relying on a view can generate confusion

In Snp 4.9, the line `diṭṭhiñca nissāya...` directly presents reliance on view.

The surrounding verses move through:

- grasped views;
- comparison as equal/superior/inferior;
- dispute;
- relinquishment of fixed positions.

This supports reading `nissāya` here as a dependence/support relationship rather than merely neutral possession of a view.

### 3. Snp 4.12 — support can underwrite judgment and dispute

Snp 4.12 describes people relying on what is:

- seen;
- heard;
- virtue/observance;
- sensed/known;

and then standing in judgment, pleased with their conclusion, declaring another person foolish or unskilled.

This is a particularly important Aṭṭhakavagga pattern:

**experience or practice becomes a support for a fixed evaluative position, which then structures superiority and dispute.**

The exact English rendering of `nissāya` remains context-sensitive.

### 4. SN 12.15 — Right View is explicitly taught without clinging/fixation

SN 12.15 does not reject Right View.

Instead, Right View sees arising and cessation while avoiding the extremes of existence/non-existence and without getting involved in clinging, fixation, and "my self".

This gives the crucial guardrail for A164:

**the early path does not oppose view as such to no-view; it opposes distorted or appropriated dependence while retaining right seeing.**

### 5. MN 22 raft simile — Dhamma is a means, not a permanent possession

The raft simile teaches that Dhamma is for crossing over, not for grasping.

A164 uses this as direct support for the distinction:

- a teaching/path can be genuinely useful;
- usefulness does not require converting the teaching into a permanent identity or support one refuses to release.

### 6. `nissaya` is not intrinsically negative across the canon

In Vinaya usage, `nissaya` can mean dependence/apprenticeship under a teacher.

In other contexts it can mean basis, support, reliance, or depending on.

Therefore the word itself is not a moral category meaning "bad attachment".

Context determines whether the support is:

- practical;
- causal;
- pedagogical;
- psychological;
- doctrinal;
- appropriative.

This lexical fact is essential to avoid over-reading Aṭṭhakavagga.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. The issue is not "support vs no support" in the abstract

A164 distinguishes at least four functions:

1. **causal support** — one condition supports another;
2. **pedagogical support** — reliance on a teacher or training structure;
3. **instrumental path support** — using a teaching or Right View to orient practice;
4. **appropriative dependency** — turning a view, identity, attainment, perception, or doctrine into something one must stand on as "mine / my truth / my position".

This fourfold map is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS, not a canonical taxonomy.

### B. Right View can function without becoming `diṭṭhi-nissaya`

The central synthesis from MN 22 + SN 12.15 is:

**Right View functions as an instrument of seeing and abandoning error; `diṭṭhi-nissaya` is what happens when a view becomes a support one appropriates, inhabits, or depends on in a way that sustains suffering.**

This is not a verbatim canonical definition, but it best preserves both sets of texts.

### C. "Use" and "dwell in" should remain distinct

A164 keeps the project distinction from A35:

- **use a view:** it helps identify cause/result, wrong/right, arising/cessation;
- **dwell in a view:** it becomes a fixed standpoint, basis of superiority, identity, or dispute.

The English contrast is analytic synthesis.

### D. Anissita/anissaya is not cognitive blankness

When Aṭṭhakavagga praises non-dependence, the safest reading is not:

"the sage has no knowledge, perception, discernment, or functioning views whatsoever."

Rather, the broader early material supports:

- not standing on a view as identity;
- not grasping it as supreme;
- not using it as a basis for ranking and conflict;
- remaining capable of knowing, seeing, and distinguishing.

This is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### E. Dependence on experience can be as problematic as dependence on doctrine

Snp 4.12 is especially important because the support can be:

- what one has seen;
- heard;
- practiced;
- sensed/known.

Thus A164 expands the practical guardrail:

**even true experiences or real attainments can become identity-supports if appropriated as "this proves I am superior / this alone is truth / others are fools."**

This is a modern application grounded in the Aṭṭhakavagga dispute pattern.

## LATER/PARACANONICAL — MILINDAPAÑHA

**Consulted:** YES.

Milindapañha uses `nissāya` in clearly non-pejorative ways, for example describing wholesome development as based on virtue.

This supports a crucial lexical correction:

**`nissaya` does not lexically mean defilement.**

Its moral force depends on what is relied upon and how.

Milindapañha's role here is later/paracanonical clarification only.

It does not override MN 22 or Aṭṭhakavagga.

## UNCERTAINTY / OPEN CHECK

1. The dedicated Aṭṭhakavagga lexical audit for `nissaya` remains open.
2. `nissaya`, `nissāya`, `anissita`, and related forms should not be collapsed without morphology/context checking.
3. "Dependency", "support", "reliance", "basis", and "standing on" are translation choices, not universally interchangeable English equivalents.
4. `diṭṭhi-nissaya` in MN 22 is a strong direct anchor, but not every occurrence of view elsewhere automatically instantiates the same compound.
5. "Using Right View without depending on it" is a cross-text formulation, not a single canonical sentence.
6. The exact relation among `nissaya`, `upādāna`, `nivesana`, and `samuggahīta` remains lexically open; they must not be treated as synonyms.
7. Aṭṭhakavagga lexical worker material for `saññā/maññati` has passed CI but was not merged at the time of this checkpoint because the required `test` status remained expected; it is not silently treated as canonical main.

## Practical understanding

A164 yields a diagnostic:

1. Is this belief or teaching functioning as a tool for seeing more clearly?
2. Or has it become something I need to defend as "my position"?
3. Does it reduce greed, hatred, delusion, and selfing?
4. Or does it generate superior/inferior comparison and dispute?
5. Can I revise or release the formulation when deeper seeing requires it?
6. Am I confusing confidence in a teaching with psychological dependence on being right?
7. Is the support temporary/instrumental, or has it become identity?

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION.

## Corrections preserved

- `nissaya` != attachment by definition;
- `nissaya` != always negative;
- `diṭṭhi` != `diṭṭhi-nissaya`;
- Right View != view-clinging;
- non-dependence != having no discernment;
- Dhamma as path-support != permanent possession;
- anissita/anissaya != nihilism;
- `nissaya`, `upādāna`, `nivesana`, `samuggahīta` remain distinct lexical items.
