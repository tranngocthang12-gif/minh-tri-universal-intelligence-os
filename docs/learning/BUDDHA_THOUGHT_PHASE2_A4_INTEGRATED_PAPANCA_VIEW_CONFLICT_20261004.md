## Research question

Can we build one defensible early-Buddhist model of how perception, feeling, craving, view, conceit, mine-making and papañca contribute to conflict without pretending that the texts give one universal linear chain?

## 1. Canonical anchors must remain separate

### MN 18 — cognitive expansion
The discourse gives:
sense base + object → consciousness → contact → feeling → perception → thinking → papañca.

It then says that, where there is nothing to relish, welcome or remain fastened to in relation to the conditions by which papañca-related perceptions/categories assail a person, the underlying tendencies to passion, resistance, views, doubt, conceit, desire for becoming and ignorance end, together with quarrels, disputes, accusations, divisive speech and false speech.

This is the clearest anchor for linking:
- cognitive expansion,
- affective fastening,
- latent tendencies,
- social conflict.

### Sn 4.11 Kalahavivāda — multiple causal routes to conflict
The poem traces quarrels/disputes through:
- what is dear,
- desire,
- pleasant/unpleasant,
- contact,
- name-and-form.

It also says:
- acquisitions/appropriations arise from desire;
- when desire is absent, mamatta/mine-making is absent;
- papañca-related constructions depend on perception.

This is not the same chain as MN 18.

### SN 35 material — relishing / welcoming / fastening
Across SN 35 discourses, sensory objects do not bind merely because they are cognized.
Bondage develops when the practitioner:
- relishes,
- welcomes,
- remains fastened.

Delight then leads to passion/fettering, and consciousness becomes dependent/sustained by the object.

This supplies a powerful bridge between sensory cognition and craving/clinging without requiring that cognition itself be pathological.

### SN 22 material — I-making / mine-making
SN 22.82/91 asks how there can be no:
- ahaṅkāra / I-making,
- mamaṅkāra / mine-making,
- underlying tendency to conceit.

The answer is to see all aggregates with right understanding as:
- not mine,
- not I,
- not my self.

This anchors the selfing model in the aggregates without inventing a hidden self-process outside the texts.

### Sn 4.8–4.14 — identity, truth-possession and social conflict
The Aṭṭhakavagga repeatedly targets:
- being entrenched in views,
- “only this is true” positioning,
- equal/superior/inferior comparison,
- praise and defeat,
- doctrinal possession,
- quarrel.

The point is not “thought must stop,” but that cognition can be appropriated into identity and status.

## 2. The project must NOT merge these into one canonical chain

Reject the formula:

contact → feeling → perception → thought → papañca → craving → clinging → self → conflict

as though one sutta or one early stratum states it.

Instead, retain several analytical cuts:

### Cognitive cut
contact → feeling → perception → thinking → papañca

### Craving/clinging cut
contact → feeling → craving → clinging → becoming

### Conflict cut
dear/desire → appropriation/mine-making → conceit/comparison → quarrel

### Selfing cut
aggregates → I-making / mine-making / conceit / view

The texts overlap but do not collapse.

## 3. Integrated recursive model — SYNTHESIS ONLY

A defensible project model is:

sensory event
→ feeling tone
→ recognition/perception
→ thought
↔ relishing / resistance / indifference-with-ignorance
↔ conceptual expansion / papañca
↔ appropriation: “mine”
↔ self-positioning: “I am / I am this”
↔ comparison: equal / superior / inferior
↔ view-position / “our truth”
→ speech/action for defense, victory or exclusion
→ quarrel/conflict
→ new sensory events and feeling
→ feedback into the loop.

This is intentionally recursive.

Why recursive?
Because:
- papañca can arise from thought;
- self-reference can also appear as part of papañca;
- desire feeds appropriation;
- appropriation and comparison feed dispute;
- conflict produces new feeling that can feed craving/resistance again.

No claim is made that every episode contains every node.

## 4. The crucial hinge is not cognition but fastening

The most consistent intervention language across the texts is not:
“do not perceive”
or
“do not think.”

It is closer to:
- do not relish,
- do not welcome,
- do not remain fastened,
- do not make mine/I/self out of conditioned processes,
- do not measure self as better/equal/worse,
- do not turn doctrine into a status possession.

This is the strongest integrative insight from Phase 2A.

## 5. Intervention points — source-grounded but project-synthesized

### Point 1 — feeling
SN 36 material:
pain/pleasure can occur without compulsory second-order reaction.

Practice implication:
distinguish feeling from the next move.

### Point 2 — relishing / resistance
MN 18 and SN 35:
the practitioner can fail to feed the process by not relishing, welcoming or fastening to the object.

Practice implication:
observe the moment experience becomes “I need this / I must get rid of this.”

### Point 3 — perception/thought
MN 18:
perception and thought are part of cognition; papañca is downstream of them.

Practice implication:
do not treat every thought as pathology; notice when thought shifts into self/world expansion.

### Point 4 — appropriation
SN 22:
see the aggregates as not mine, not I, not self.

Practice implication:
locate the transformation from “there is an experience” to “this is mine / this says who I am.”

### Point 5 — comparison/conceit
Sn 4.9:
equal/superior/inferior comparison supplies a basis for quarrel.

Practice implication:
notice status measurement before it becomes attack/defense.

### Point 6 — view-position
Sn 4.8–4.13:
doctrine can become a possessed position tied to praise, humiliation and victory.

Practice implication:
test whether a view can be revised without experienced loss of identity.

### Point 7 — speech
MN 18 explicitly links the stopping of the underlying process to the ending of:
- arguments,
- quarrels,
- accusations,
- divisive speech,
- false speech.

Practice implication:
speech is not merely an external moral add-on; it is where cognitive-affective selfing becomes socially causal.

## 6. Cross-tradition status

### Strong structural support
- MN 18 has Āgama parallels.
- Aṭṭhakavagga has the Arthapada/T198 recension.
- Māgandiya material is independently preserved in T198.
- SĀ 551 preserves an explanatory tradition relating Arthapada/Māgandiya material to craving, attachment, views and non-contention.

### Caution
Exact one-to-one lexical mapping remains incomplete for:
- papañca,
- pacceka-sacca,
- several Chinese view/attachment terms,
- Sn 4.8/4.12/4.13/4.14 line-level T198 parallels.

Structural convergence raises confidence.
It does not establish verbatim historical-Buddha wording.

## 7. Mi Tiên stress-test

Milindapañha’s distinction between:
- scholars who refute/counter-refute and admit error without anger,
and
- kings who punish dissent,

supports an important clarification:

**rational disagreement is not the same as the conflict criticized in Aṭṭhakavagga.**

The pathology is:
- identity defense,
- anger,
- coercion,
- prestige competition,
- refusal to revise.

**Status:** MILINDA_COMPATIBLE / later clarification.

## 8. What Phase 2A now establishes

### EBT_STRONG
- perception, thought and papañca are distinguishable;
- papañca is linked with conflict;
- sensory cognition becomes bondage through relishing/fastening, not merely through appearing;
- selfing includes I-making, mine-making and conceit;
- comparison better/equal/worse can feed dispute;
- view-clinging and status competition are important conflict mechanisms.

### CROSS-TRADITION STRONG AT STRUCTURAL LEVEL
- anti-grasping/non-contention architecture is not confined to one Pāli recension;
- selected Arthapada/Māgandiya structures survive in Chinese transmission.

### EBT_PROBABLE / PROJECT SYNTHESIS
- conflict is best modeled as a recursive feedback network joining cognition, affect, appropriation, comparison and speech.
- papañca is better understood as a specific self/world-expanding mode than as generic thinking.

### OPEN
- exact lexical core of papañca;
- exact causal ordering among taṇhā, upādāna, māna, diṭṭhi and papañca across all contexts;
- whether a single more abstract common model can be reconstructed without importing later Abhidhamma systematics.

## 9. Practical falsifiability

If this synthesis is approximately correct, then in lived conflict we should often be able to detect several separable stages/functions:

- bare event,
- pleasant/unpleasant/neutral tone,
- labeling/interpretation,
- relishing or resistance,
- “mine/I” loading,
- comparison,
- rehearsed story,
- defensive speech/action.

The model would be weakened if careful observation repeatedly showed:
- conflict with no appropriation/comparison/defense component at all,
or
- liberation texts treating all perception/thought as inherently defiled.

Current textual evidence does not support those alternatives.

## 10. Phase 2A conclusion

The Buddha’s conflict analysis is not anti-intellectual.

It is better described as:
**anti-appropriation of cognition into self/status structures that generate craving, defense and quarrel.**

The practical goal is not cognitive blankness.
It is cognition without bondage.

This is a SYNTHESIS, not a canonical quotation.
