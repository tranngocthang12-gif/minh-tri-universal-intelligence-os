## Central question

How can early Buddhist teaching affirm right view while also warning against attachment to views, partisan truth-claims, and dependence on positions?

Milindapañha 3.1.7–8 distinguishes reasoning from wisdom and presents reasoning as grasping/comprehending while wisdom cuts off defilements. Mil 3.7.6 adds an investigation-of-truth role for wisdom. ## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. SN 12.15 — right view is explicitly affirmed

SN 12.15 directly answers the question "what is right view?"

It describes right view as seeing arising and cessation with right wisdom, avoiding the extremes "everything exists" and "everything does not exist", and not clinging to attachments, fixations, or "my self".

Therefore the claim:

`the Buddha rejected all views`

is false as a blanket formulation.

Early discourse explicitly teaches `sammādiṭṭhi` / right view.

### 2. MN 22 — correct grasp of teaching vs clinging to the teaching

MN 22 gives two key controls:

- teachings wrongly grasped can harm, like a snake wrongly grasped;
- the Dhamma is like a raft: useful for crossing, not for carrying around as an object of attachment after its function is fulfilled.

This shows that a teaching or view can be:
- correctly understood and used;
- incorrectly grasped;
- relinquished when its function is complete.

Therefore "non-clinging to views" cannot simply mean "never use any view or teaching".

### 3. MN 95 — preserving truth is not the same as claiming exclusive truth

MN 95 distinguishes:
- faith;
- preference/approval;
- oral tradition;
- reasoning;
- reflective acceptance of a view;

from actual discovery/awakening to truth.

A person preserves truth by honestly stating the basis of their conviction without jumping to the conclusion:

"only this is true; everything else is wrong."

This is direct early-text support for epistemic restraint.

### 4. Snp 4.8 Pasūra — view-clinging, praise, defeat, and debate

Snp 4.8 portrays disputants who:
- hold tightly to their position;
- claim "this alone is true";
- enter debate seeking praise;
- become elated by victory and distressed by defeat;
- oppose view against view.

The discourse does not merely criticize false propositions. It highlights attachment, rivalry, status, and identity around views.

### 5. Snp 4.9 Māgaṇḍiya — neither purity through view nor purity through no-view

Snp 4.9 is especially important because it blocks two symmetrical mistakes.

The discourse says purity is not attained merely:
- by view, learning, knowledge, or precepts/observances;
- nor by simply lacking these.

It then speaks of relinquishing and not taking them up as a basis of dependence, while continuing to criticize reliance on a view as the standpoint from which one questions.

Therefore:
- "having a view is liberation" is wrong;
- "having no view is liberation" is also wrong.

### 6. Snp 4.12 Cūḷabyūha — personal/sectarian truth-claims generate mutual foolishness

Snp 4.12 describes people dwelling on their own views and claiming that their doctrine is true while opponents are foolish.

Its central diagnostic is not that truth is unreal.

Rather, competing parties each absolutize their own position and use truth-language to establish superiority over opponents.

This supports a distinction between:
- truth;
- one's claim to possess truth exclusively;
- identity investment in that claim.

### 7. `nissaya` / reliance as a functional issue

Across the Aṭṭhakavagga material used here, the key problem is not only propositional content but what the mind depends on, leans on, or takes as a basis.

Snp 4.9 explicitly says one should become peaceful without dependence after relinquishing the relevant bases.

A163 therefore treats `nissaya` primarily as dependence/reliance in context, not as a slogan meaning "all support is forbidden".

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Right view and non-clinging are not contradictory

A163's central synthesis is:

**Right view is a functional mode of seeing that corrects error and guides practice; view-clinging occurs when the view is appropriated, absolutized, defended as identity, or used for status and conflict.**

This is not one verbatim canonical sentence. It is a synthesis from SN 12.15, MN 22, MN 95, and Aṭṭhakavagga.

### B. Three levels must be separated

1. **View as orientation/tool** — e.g. right view in SN 12.15.
2. **Truth-claim** — a proposition one currently holds to be true.
3. **View-clinging / identity investment** — "my view", "only this is true", superiority/inferiority, praise/defeat dynamics.

Collapsing these into one category creates two opposite errors:
- dogmatism;
- anti-intellectual "no views at all" relativism.

### C. "Only this is true" is not automatically condemned in every logical context

MN 95 criticizes premature exclusive certainty when one's basis is only faith, tradition, reasoning, or reflective acceptance.

Snp 4.8 and 4.12 criticize exclusive truth-claims embedded in rivalry and grasping.

A163 therefore does **not** infer that every categorical truth statement is forbidden.

The stronger supported claim is:
**exclusive certainty unsupported by direct knowledge, combined with attachment and opposition, is epistemically and ethically dangerous.**

### D. The Buddha's anti-dogmatism is not relativism

SN 12.15 explicitly teaches right view.
MN 95 distinguishes mere belief from discovery of truth.
MN 22 distinguishes correct from incorrect grasp of teaching.

Therefore early Buddhist anti-dogmatism does not imply:
- all views are equally true;
- there is no truth;
- evidence and correct understanding do not matter.

### E. Non-reliance is not cognitive emptiness

Aṭṭhakavagga's `anissāya` / not depending should be read functionally:
not building identity, security, superiority, or final refuge on a conceptual position.

This does not require a person to be unable to:
- distinguish wholesome/unwholesome;
- recognize right/wrong view;
- state what evidence supports;
- use conceptual teachings provisionally.

This is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS.

## LATER/PARACANONICAL — MILINDAPAÑHA

### Mil 3.1.7–8 — reasoning vs wisdom

Milindapañha distinguishes reasoning from wisdom.

Reasoning apprehends/graspingly comprehends an object; wisdom is compared to the sickle that cuts.

Role in A163:
- helps clarify why having a well-formed conceptual position is not identical to liberating wisdom;
- supports the distinction between intellectual correctness and transformation of defilement.

### Mil 3.7.6 — investigation of truth

Milindapañha says wisdom becomes effective through investigation of truth.

Role:
- supports active inquiry rather than passive authority-dependence;
- does not override MN 95's early-text distinction between reasoning and discovery of truth.

## UNCERTAINTY / OPEN CHECK

1. `diṭṭhi` is context-sensitive. Not every occurrence means wrong view.
2. `nissaya` and related verbal forms require passage-level lexical control; do not force one English gloss everywhere.
3. `pacceka-sacca` in Snp 4.8 is difficult and translation-sensitive; "personal truth", "individual truth", and "idiosyncratic truth" are interpretive renderings.
4. Aṭṭhakavagga's anti-dispute language should not be overextended into a ban on all reasoned disagreement; MN 58 and other texts preserve a role for truthful, beneficial correction.
5. The relationship between provisional right view and complete release from all dependence deserves a dedicated path-stage audit.
6. Full lexical audit for `diṭṭhi`, `sacca`, and `nissaya` remains OPEN.

## Practical understanding

When holding a belief or doctrine, ask:

1. What is the evidence for this?
2. Am I reporting my current basis honestly, as MN 95 requires?
3. Is this view helping me see arising, cessation, causality, and suffering more clearly?
4. Have I converted the view into "my side", "my intelligence", or "my worth"?
5. Does disagreement produce curiosity or status-threat?
6. Am I defending truth, or defending myself through a truth-claim?
7. If better evidence appears, can I revise the view without feeling personally diminished?
8. Even if the view is useful, am I mistaking the raft for the destination?

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION, not a canonical eight-step list.
