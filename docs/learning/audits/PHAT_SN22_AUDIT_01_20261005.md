## TEXT_ATTESTED

1. **SN 22.1–8 — appropriation, change, distress, and release from attachment**
   - SN 22.1 and SN 22.7 directly use self-identification schemas around the aggregates; when what is identified with changes, mental distress/agitation follows.
   - SN 22.8 directly uses the triad equivalent to: “this is mine / I am this / this is my self”; not regarding aggregates in this way removes the stated sorrow/distress consequence.
   - SN 22.2–4 directly place desire/lust/delight/craving and their relinquishment in relation to the five aggregates; SN 22.4 explicitly connects their fading/cessation/giving up with a mind well liberated.
   - SN 22.5–6 directly teach origination/disappearance of the aggregates and a causal sequence from delight to clinging, becoming, birth, aging/death, with the reverse cessation sequence.

2. **SN 22.9–21 — impermanence, suffering, not-self, non-appropriation, arising/cessation**
   - SN 22.9–11 apply impermanence, suffering, and not-self respectively across past/future/present aggregates.
   - SN 22.12–17 directly connect seeing the aggregates as impermanent/suffering/not-self with disenchantment/dispassion/release; SN 22.15–17 explicitly use the negative appropriation formula “not mine / not I / not my self.”
   - SN 22.18–20 extend the analysis to the causes/conditions giving rise to the aggregates.
   - SN 22.21 describes the aggregates as impermanent, conditioned/dependently arisen and liable to cessation/fading.

3. **SN 22.22 — burden**
   - The burden is the five aggregates subject to clinging.
   - Taking up the burden is craving that makes for renewed existence.
   - Laying down the burden is the remainderless fading/cessation/relinquishment/release/non-reliance regarding that craving.
   - The text also calls the burden-carrier a `puggala` (“person/individual”) identified by conventional name/clan. This wording must be preserved rather than silently converted into either a permanent-self doctrine or a denial that the discourse uses person-language.

4. **SN 22.45, SN 22.59 — not-self, non-appropriation, dispassion, liberation**
   - SN 22.59 directly predicates each aggregate as `anattā`; if an aggregate were self, the stated command “let it be thus / let it not be thus” would be possible, but it is not.
   - SN 22.59 directly gives impermanence → suffering/changeability → unfitness for “this is mine / I am this / this is my self,” followed by right seeing, disenchantment, dispassion, and release.
   - SN 22.45 likewise connects the aggregate analysis with dispassion and liberation by non-clinging.

5. **SN 22.48 — five aggregates are not lexically identical to five clinging-aggregates**
   - The discourse explicitly distinguishes the five aggregates from the five aggregates subject to clinging. Therefore `pañcakkhandha` and `pañcupādānakkhandha` must not be collapsed as if they were interchangeable expressions in every context.

6. **SN 22.80, SN 22.82 — clinging and the aggregates**
   - SN 22.80 directly links grasping/insisting on the aggregates to continued existence and the causal sequence leading onward to rebirth, aging/death, and suffering.
   - SN 22.82 explicitly rejects both “clinging is simply the same thing as the five clinging-aggregates” and “clinging is wholly separate from them”; in that context, desire-and-passion (`chandarāga`) for them is the clinging there.

7. **SN 22.79, SN 22.89, SN 22.91, SN 22.102 — recollection, self-view, residual ‘I am’**
   - SN 22.79 says recollection of past abodes is recollection in terms of one or another of the five aggregates subject to clinging.
   - SN 22.89 directly distinguishes not regarding any of the five clinging-aggregates as self/belonging-to-self from still having a residual “I am” conceit/desire/underlying tendency; contemplating arising and passing of the five clinging-aggregates is part of the removal of that residue.
   - SN 22.91 directly connects right seeing of every aggregate as “not mine / I am not this / not my self” with absence of I-making, mine-making, and the underlying tendency to conceit.
   - SN 22.102 directly says developed perception of impermanence uproots the conceit “I am,” among other defilements.

8. **SN 22.95 — similes and insubstantiality**
   - Form is compared to foam, feeling to a bubble, perception to a mirage, formations to a banana trunk, consciousness to a magic trick.
   - Proper examination finds them empty/void/without substance; the discourse then moves to disenchantment, dispassion, and release.
   - This does **not** textually license the nihilistic claim “the aggregates do not exist.”

9. **SN 22.101 — release requires cultivation**
   - Wanting liberation by non-clinging is not presented as sufficient by itself; development of the path/wholesome factors is required for destruction of the taints.

10. **SN 22.151 — grasping and the appropriation triad**
    - When an aggregate is grasped/insisted upon, the view “this is mine / I am this / this is my self” is produced; seeing the aggregate as impermanent/suffering/perishable and not grasping it removes that construction.

## SYNTHESIS_ONLY / PRIOR CLAIMS THAT MUST STAY SYNTHESIS

- **A132:** “prudent danger-awareness vs identity-threat fear” is a useful cross-text distinction. SN 22.7 directly gives self-identification + aggregate change → fear/distress/anxiety/agitation, but does not itself teach the full modern prudence taxonomy.
- **A133:** “effective agency vs total control,” “control fantasy,” and the chain appropriation → demand for control → deviation/change → fear/frustration are cross-text analytical models. SN 22.59 supplies a conditional commandability argument; it does not provide a complete theory of agency or control.
- **A135/A136:** “genuine progress can still be appropriated by asmimāna” and “humility/non-attachment can become an identity” are applications/syntheses from material including SN 22.89, not verbatim claims of SN 22.89.
- **A145:** “memory itself is not the enemy,” and the distinction among factual recollection, affective reactivation, proliferative replay, and identity-binding, are cross-text synthesis. SN 22.79 directly supports only the narrower aggregate/recollection point. The project currently labels this synthesis correctly.
- Any blanket equation `five aggregates = five clinging-aggregates` is not text-attested by SN 22.48.
- Any reading of SN 22.95 as “nothing exists” is an overreach beyond the text.

## LEXICAL_UNCERTAINTY / TRANSLATION GUARDRAILS

- `khandha`: aggregate/group; `upādānakkhandha`: aggregate subject to clinging / clinging-aggregate. Translation varies; SN 22.48 shows they are not simply identical expressions.
- `rūpa`: usually “form”; should not automatically be narrowed to “body” in every aggregate context.
- `vedanā`: feeling/feeling-tone; not identical to the modern umbrella category “emotion.”
- `saññā`: perception/recognition; not one-to-one with autobiographical memory.
- `saṅkhārā`: formations/fabrications/volitional activities/choices depending context; no single English gloss should be promoted as exclusive.
- `viññāṇa`: consciousness; contextual analysis still matters.
- `dukkha`: suffering/stress/unsatisfactoriness are translation choices; one English gloss is not absolute.
- `anattā`: “not-self” is the safest audit-level rendering here. Vietnamese “vô ngã” is conventional, but the checked passages should not be expanded without argument into the separate universal metaphysical slogan “nothing/self absolutely does not exist.”
- `etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā`: “this is mine / I am this (this I am) / this is my self”; negative: `netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā`.
- `nibbidā`: disenchantment/revulsion/disillusionment; not necessarily emotional disgust.
- `virāga`: dispassion/fading of passion; not mere indifference/apathy.
- `upādāna / upādāya / anupādāya`: clinging/grasping/appropriating/non-clinging; syntax and context must control translation.
- `chanda` in SN 22.82 is rendered “desire” in context; `chandarāga` is desire-and-passion/greed. Do not globally equate every `chanda` with `taṇhā`.
- `asmimāna`: “I am” conceit; distinguish from explicit identity-view and from ordinary conventional first-person speech.
- `puggala` in SN 22.22: person/individual. The discourse uses the term but does not in that passage settle every later metaphysical question about personhood.
