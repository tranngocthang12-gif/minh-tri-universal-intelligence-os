## 2. New reasoning result — multiple valid taxonomies

SN 36.19 is methodologically important.

Two interlocutors dispute whether there are:
- two feelings;
or
- three feelings.

The Buddha says both modes are valid and explicitly states that he has taught:
- two;
- three;
- five;
- six;
- eighteen;
- thirty-six;
- one hundred and eight feelings.

SN 36.22 expands these schemes.

### Rule added
**DO NOT REIFY A PEDAGOGICAL TAXONOMY.**

A Buddhist analytical list may be:
- context-sensitive;
- generated by a chosen axis of classification;
- valid alongside another non-identical classification.

Therefore:
"the text gives N categories"
does not automatically mean:
"reality ultimately contains exactly N ontological kinds."

This is one of the clearest canonical guards against scholastic over-literalization.

## 3. SN 22.22 — conventional person-language is real textual data

SN 22.22 defines:
- burden = five aggregates subject to clinging;
- burden-bearer = the person, named conventionally;
- taking up burden = craving;
- laying it down = cessation of craving.

### Tension
This sits beside:
- not-self analysis;
- refusal to locate a permanent self in aggregates;
- anti-annihilation teaching.

### Current disciplined reading
Do not infer from `puggala/person`:
- an eternal soul;
- a hidden metaphysical carrier.

But also do not rewrite the discourse as if it never used person-language.

### Rule added
**CONVENTIONAL AGENT LANGUAGE DOES NOT SETTLE ULTIMATE ONTOLOGY.**

The text can use "person" for practical/ethical narration while the nature/status of a supposed permanent self remains denied or undeclared in the relevant analyses.

Status:
PARTIAL_RESOLUTION / HISTORICALLY CONTROVERSIAL.

## 4. SN 22.53 strengthens the non-soul consciousness model

SN 22.53 says consciousness cannot coherently be described as:
- coming;
- going;
- passing away;
- arising;
- growing;
- increasing

apart from form, feeling, perception and formations.

With passion/delight as support, consciousness can become established and grow.
When support is cut, it becomes unestablished and released.

### Implication
This strengthens:
**consciousness is relational/conditioned, not a free-standing soul-substance.**

It also warns against taking rebirth-consciousness language from other texts as proof of an independently existing carrier.

## 5. SN 35.232 — the fetter is not eye or form

In the six-sense analysis, neither:
- the sense faculty;
nor
- its object
is itself the fetter.

The fetter is the desire/passion that arises dependent on both.

### Reasoning update
**DO NOT BLAME THE INTERFACE OR THE OBJECT; LOCATE THE REACTIVE BOND.**

This prevents two mistakes:
- ascetic hatred of sensory objects as intrinsically evil;
- naive assumption that liberation requires sensory nonexistence.

## 6. SN 36.21 — not every feeling is caused by past kamma

The Moḷiyasīvaka discourse lists multiple causes for painful/pleasant experiences, with kamma among them rather than the sole cause.

### Guard
Reject:
**EVERY EXPERIENCE = DIRECT RESULT OF PAST KAMMA.**

This is important for both philosophy and ethical interpretation.

## 7. SN 46 — awakening factors are condition-sensitive

The collection repeatedly links awakening factors to:
- nutrition/conditions;
- wise attention;
- hindrance reduction;
- suitable meditation themes;
- calming vs energizing functions.

### Strong synthesis
The seven factors are not only an attainment checklist.
They form a **regulatory system**:
- mindfulness tracks;
- investigation discriminates;
- energy activates;
- rapture energizes;
- tranquility settles;
- samādhi unifies;
- equanimity balances.

### Guard
No one factor should be maximized independently in every state.
