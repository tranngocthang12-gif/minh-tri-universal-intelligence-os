# EARLY BUDDHIST CANON AUTO STUDY PROGRAM — 2026-10-04

**Status:** ACTIVE STUDY PROGRAM / SOURCE-CRITICAL / NO CLAIM OF COMPLETE MASTERY  
**Purpose:** Build a durable MINH TRI study track for understanding the earliest recoverable Buddhist teachings through the Pali Nikayas, parallel Agamas, Vinaya context, and careful Pali-English-Vietnamese comparison.

## 1. Epistemic boundary

This program does **not** assume that every line in the extant Pali Canon is a verbatim transcript of the historical Buddha.

Working hierarchy:

1. Earliest recoverable discourse material shared across independent transmission lines.
2. Pali Nikayas and close parallels in the Chinese Agamas / surviving Indic fragments / Tibetan parallels.
3. Vinaya material useful for institutional and historical context.
4. Early Khuddaka material with strong comparative support.
5. Later canonical/systematic materials.
6. Commentaries and later Theravada interpretation.
7. Modern teachers and secondary interpretation.

When a doctrine appears in several independent early parallels with stable structure, confidence in its antiquity rises. When it appears only in a later stratum, the program marks it as later/uncertain rather than silently attributing it to the historical Buddha.

## 2. Canon map

### Vinaya Pitaka
Use for:
- monastic discipline;
- institutional design;
- conflict handling;
- social and historical context;
- examples of the Buddha's practical governance.

Do not treat every later Vinaya narrative as equally early without comparison.

### Sutta Pitaka

#### Digha Nikaya — Long Discourses
Use for:
- large doctrinal syntheses;
- dialogues with rival schools and social elites;
- cosmology, ethics, politics and social context;
- narrative framing.

#### Majjhima Nikaya — Middle Length Discourses
Use for:
- detailed reasoning;
- meditation instructions;
- diagnostic conversations;
- psychology of craving, views and practice.

#### Samyutta Nikaya — Linked Discourses
Highest priority for doctrinal structure:
- dependent origination;
- five aggregates;
- six sense fields;
- feeling;
- path factors;
- awakening factors;
- four noble truths.

#### Anguttara Nikaya — Numbered Discourses
Use for:
- practical lists;
- lay ethics;
- training heuristics;
- governance;
- gradual cultivation.

#### Khuddaka Nikaya — Minor Collection
Stratify rather than treating as one historical layer.
Early/high-priority comparative material includes:
- Dhammapada;
- Udana;
- Itivuttaka;
- Sutta Nipata;
- Theragatha;
- Therigatha.

Other Khuddaka works may be later and should be dated/assessed separately.

### Abhidhamma Pitaka
Study as an important later systematization of Theravada thought, not automatically as the earliest layer of the Buddha's own discourse.

For every discourse, record:
- text ID;
- audience;
- problem/question;
- key causal structure;
- practice prescription;
- claimed result;

- parallel texts;
- translation disagreements;

### Phase C — Parallels
For important doctrinal claims, compare:
- Pali Nikaya version;
- Chinese Agama parallel;
- Sanskrit/Prakrit fragment when available;
- Tibetan parallel when available.

The goal is not to pick a winner by tradition, but to identify:
- common core;
- likely oral-transmission variation;
- school-specific additions;
- translation artifacts.

### Phase D — Vinaya and historical context
Use Vinaya to understand how principles were operationalized in community life.

### Phase E — Abhidhamma/commentaries
Only after the early discourse map is stable.
Classify each later interpretation as:
- clarification compatible with early texts;
- plausible development;
- sectarian/systematic development;
- conflict/uncertain.

### Layer 2 — English
Use more than one translation when meaning is contested.
Primary comparison set:
- Bhikkhu Sujato / SuttaCentral;
- Bhikkhu Bodhi where available;
- Thanissaro Bhikkhu where useful;
- Pali Text Society translations as historical comparison.

Do not assume modern-sounding English is automatically more exact.

### Layer 3 — Vietnamese
Primary base:
- Hoa thuong Thich Minh Chau for the four Nikayas and available Khuddaka material.

When Vietnamese wording is opaque:
1. check the Pali term;
2. check at least one modern English translation;
3. check another Vietnamese rendering if available;
4. record the semantic range instead of forcing one Vietnamese equivalent.

Example discipline:
- anatta is not flattened into a vague slogan;
- dukkha is not reduced to ordinary "pain";
- sankhara is not given one fixed Vietnamese/English word in every context;
- samadhi/jhana are read in their technical context;
- vinnana is interpreted by passage, not by a metaphysical theory imported from elsewhere.

### Dictionary layer
Use:
- Digital Pali Dictionary;
- Pali Text Society Pali-English Dictionary;
- Critical Pali Dictionary / A Dictionary of Pali when needed.

Dictionary meaning never overrides syntax and context.

## 5. How to infer the Buddha's way of thinking

Do not extract "philosophy" as isolated propositions only. Track the recurrent problem-solving form.

### A. Diagnostic rather than speculative
Typical structure:
1. identify suffering/problem;
2. identify conditions that generate it;
3. show cessation when those conditions cease;
4. prescribe a trainable path.

This is clearest in the Four Noble Truths and dependent origination.

### B. Conditional/causal
Core question:
"What conditions this experience, and what changes when the condition changes?"

Avoid converting dependent origination into a single rigid metaphysical diagram when the texts use conditionality at several scales.

### C. Phenomenological
The teaching repeatedly analyzes experience into observable processes:
- body/form;
- feeling;
- perception;
- formations/choices;
- consciousness;
- senses/contact;
- craving/clinging.

The point is practical insight into attachment and release, not merely taxonomy.

### D. Anti-reification
Do not turn changing conditioned processes into a permanent essence.
Not-self analysis tests whether a phenomenon is:
- controllable as "mine";
- stable;
- fit to identify as "I/me/my self".

### E. Pragmatic truth orientation
A view is evaluated partly by what it does:
- does it increase greed, hate, delusion?
- does it support ethics, collectedness and wisdom?
- does it lead toward release?

This is not the same as "anything useful is true"; textual, causal and experiential evidence still matter.

### F. Gradual training
Repeated sequence:
- ethical restraint;
- sense restraint / moderation;
- mindfulness and clear comprehension;
- collectedness;
- insight;
- dispassion;
- release.

### G. Middle-way reasoning
Avoid pairs of extremes when both rest on a mistaken framing.
Examples include:
- sensual indulgence vs self-mortification;
- eternalist vs annihilationist identity theories;
- passive fatalism vs absolute self-control.

### H. Epistemic discipline
The early texts often distinguish:
- what is heard;
- what is reasoned;
- what is directly known;
- what is to be practiced and verified.

AN 3.65 should be read in full context: it is not a charter for believing only one's private opinion.

### I. Ethical intentionality
Action is evaluated centrally through intention and its consequences for suffering, character and future experience.
Ethics is not detached from psychology.

### J. Liberation as deconditioning
The practical arc repeatedly moves:
contact/feeling -> craving -> clinging -> becoming/suffering,
and training weakens or ends the relevant conditions.

## 6. Doctrinal synthesis map

Do not study these as separate doctrines. Link them:

- Four Noble Truths = overall diagnostic architecture.
- Dependent Origination = conditional mechanics of arising/cessation.
- Five Aggregates = analysis of what is appropriated as self.
- Six Sense Bases = interface of experience.
- Feeling = key hinge before craving.
- Craving/Clinging = appropriation dynamics.
- Three Characteristics = modes of insight into conditioned phenomena.
- Eightfold Path = transformation program.
- Three Trainings = ethics / collected mind / wisdom compression of the path.
- Four Establishments of Mindfulness = observation/training framework.
- Awakening Factors = dynamics of mature practice.
- Brahmaviharas = cultivation of non-hostile boundless attitudes.
- Nibbana = cessation/release target, to be studied through all textual usages rather than one metaphysical slogan.

## 7. Common interpretation traps

Reject or flag:
- treating Theravada commentarial doctrine as automatically identical with earliest discourses;
- treating all Khuddaka texts as one chronological layer;
- reading only Dhammapada quotations and inferring the whole system;
- turning anatta into a simplistic metaphysical slogan;
- translating dukkha as only "suffering" without range/context;
- reading dependent origination only as a 12-link three-life chart;
- treating Kalama Sutta as "believe whatever you personally feel";
- assuming meditation technique labels used today map one-to-one onto ancient terminology;
- importing Mahayana, later Theravada, Hindu or modern psychological categories into an early passage without marking the move;
- treating one English or Vietnamese translation as the original.
