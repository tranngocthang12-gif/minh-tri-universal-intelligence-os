## A148 — confidence without credulity

Central question:
How does the Buddha distinguish confidence (saddhā/pasāda) from credulity, dogmatic certainty, and dependence on authority, and how can confidence grow together with investigation rather than replace it?

### TEXT_ATTESTED

1. SN 48.10 defines the faculty of faith/confidence in terms of confidence in the Tathāgata's awakening. In the same five-faculty system, faith stands alongside energy, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom. It is therefore not presented as a replacement for wisdom.
2. MN 95 distinguishes faith, preference, oral tradition, reasoning, and reflective acceptance from direct knowledge. A person can preserve truth while acknowledging “this is my faith” without prematurely claiming exclusive certainty.
3. AN 3.65 (Kesamutti/Kālāma) rejects mere reliance on oral tradition, lineage, hearsay, collections of texts, reasoning alone, apparent competence, or “this ascetic is our teacher.” It then evaluates qualities by whether they are wholesome/blameless, praised by the wise, and lead to welfare and happiness when undertaken.
4. MN 47 instructs a disciple to examine the Tathāgata rather than accept merely on reputation, providing an early model in which confidence can be supported by investigation.
5. Stream-entry formulas of confirmed/verified confidence (aveccappasāda) show that Buddhist confidence can deepen as seeing and practice mature; early confidence and later confirmed confidence should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated state.

### SADDHĀ != CREDULITY

A148's central distinction:

- saddhā/confidence: a trust or confidence that can motivate practice and coexist with examination;
- credulity: accepting a claim merely because it is asserted, traditional, attractive, or authoritative;
- dogmatic certainty: treating one's present confidence as if it were already final knowledge;
- confirmed confidence: confidence strengthened by seeing, practice, and realization.

These English categories are CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS; they are not a canonical fourfold taxonomy.

### FAITH CAN START BEFORE FULL KNOWLEDGE

If direct knowledge were required before any confidence, one could never begin training.

A148 therefore allows a provisional function:
confidence can make practice possible before complete verification.

But MN 95 prevents promotion:
faith is not yet direct knowledge merely because it is sincere.

Thus:
**confidence can be a starting condition without being the final epistemic warrant.**

This wording is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### AUTHORITY CAN GUIDE WITHOUT BECOMING INFALLIBLE

AN 3.65 is often oversimplified into “believe nothing except personal experience.”

A148 rejects that simplification.

The discourse itself includes reference to:
- what the wise censure or praise;
- what leads to harm/suffering or welfare/happiness when practiced.

So the guardrail is not “ignore teachers.”
It is:
**teacher-status alone is insufficient proof.**

Authority may supply:
- a hypothesis;
- a practice;
- a warning;
- a body of accumulated experience.

But its claims remain open to examination appropriate to the domain.

### CONFIDENCE AND INVESTIGATION CAN GROW TOGETHER

A148 models a healthy loop:

initial confidence
-> willingness to listen/practice
-> observation and investigation
-> clearer understanding
-> confidence becomes better grounded
-> deeper practice.

This exact loop is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

A distorted loop is:
authority
-> belief
-> identity
-> defense against contrary evidence.

### MN 47 — EXAMINE THE TEACHER

MN 47 is important because the disciple is instructed to investigate qualities of the Tathāgata, including whether defiled states are observable, rather than relying purely on charisma.

A148 therefore preserves:
reverence != suspension of discernment.

### CONFIDENCE IS NOT THE SAME AS CERTAINTY-FEELING

A person may feel extremely certain and still be wrong.
A person may hold a well-grounded view while remaining open to correction.

Therefore subjective certainty is not itself the criterion of truth.

This is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS supported by MN 95 and A147.

### FAITH + WISDOM

SN 48.10 places faith and wisdom in one coordinated system.

A148's guarded synthesis:
- faith provides trust and motivational orientation;
- wisdom discriminates, understands, and eventually directly knows;
- mindfulness keeps the process present;
- energy carries it forward;
- concentration stabilizes it.

This allocation is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS, not a verbatim SN 48.10 explanation.

### PRACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC

When believing a teacher, text, or doctrine:
1. What exactly do I currently believe?
2. Why do I believe it?
3. Is the basis faith, testimony, inference, practice-result, or direct knowledge?
4. Am I claiming more certainty than that basis permits?
5. What would count as relevant contrary evidence?
6. Does the teaching reduce greed, hatred, and delusion when practiced?
7. Is it consistent with non-harm and the broader path?
8. Am I protecting truth, or protecting my teacher/group/self-image?
9. Can confidence survive honest questioning?
10. Has practice made the confidence better grounded, or merely stronger emotionally?

### MILINDAPANHA — MANDATORY

Consulted: YES.

Milindapanha's discussion of faith describes a clarifying/serening function and an aspirational function, illustrated with water-clearing and crossing imagery in later tradition.

Role:
- later/paracanonical support for faith as a constructive mental quality that enables movement rather than blind submission;
- used only to clarify function, not to define early canonical saddhā exhaustively.

Guardrail:
Milindapanha does not override SN 48.10, MN 95, AN 3.65, or MN 47.

### CORRECTIONS PRESERVED

- saddhā != blind faith;
- confidence != direct knowledge;
- questioning != lack of faith by definition;
- authority != proof;
- AN 3.65 != “trust only private experience”;
- reasoning alone != final warrant;
- verified confidence != social conformity;
- emotional certainty != truth;
- Milindapanha remains later/paracanonical support.
