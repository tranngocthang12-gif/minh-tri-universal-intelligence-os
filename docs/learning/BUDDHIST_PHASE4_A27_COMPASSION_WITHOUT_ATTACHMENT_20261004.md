# PHASE 4 — A27 — COMPASSION WITHOUT ATTACHMENT

**Status:** CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Question

If compassion genuinely wants another being to be free from suffering, how is that different from craving for that person to become a certain way?

## Primary-text findings

### MN 62
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: cultivating compassion is said to abandon cruelty.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the same training set places compassion beside loving-kindness, appreciative joy, and equanimity.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: compassion is best tracked here by what it removes—cruelty—rather than by treating every caring wish as taṇhā.

### MN 137
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the Teacher teaches out of compassion, seeking disciples' welfare, while remaining free from possessive satisfaction or dissatisfaction when they follow or do not follow.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: compassionate concern can coexist with equanimity about another person's response; therefore compassion need not entail ownership of the outcome.

### SN 36.6
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: painful feeling need not produce the second mental arrow in a learned noble disciple.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: seeing another's suffering can be painful or moving without requiring reactive appropriation, panic, or possessive control.

### SN 12.52
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: when attention dwells on the gratification of things that fuel grasping, craving grows; when drawbacks are seen, craving ceases and the downstream sequence ceases.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: taṇhā is structurally tied to gratification, grasping, and continued becoming, not simply to every form of directed concern.

### SN 12.64
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: passion, delight, and craving provide conditions in which consciousness becomes established/grows and renewed becoming develops.
- Guardrail: do not generalize this into "all desire is craving" or "all intentional concern feeds rebirth."

### SN 45.8
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: right intention includes renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: right effort requires actively generating enthusiasm/effort for abandoning unskillful qualities and developing skillful qualities.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: the path therefore contains directed motivation and active concern; Buddhist training cannot be reduced to eliminating all wanting indiscriminately.

### SN 45.2
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: good friendship supports development of the Noble Eightfold Path.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: compassion and wise care are relational rather than isolated; non-attachment does not imply withdrawal from helping relationships.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer

### Mil 5.2.7
- `MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC`: Nāgasena compares the Tathāgata to earth and a rain cloud that support beings without the possessive thought "these are mine."
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: this is a clear later model of care without appropriation: benefiting another does not require owning the person or their response.

## Main conclusion

`CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`:
Compassion and craving differ less by the superficial grammar "I want something" than by causal structure and mental root. Compassion tends toward the reduction of suffering and abandonment of cruelty, can coexist with equanimity, and need not demand ownership of another's choices. Craving is bound up with gratification, appropriation, grasping, and the demand that experience conform to one's thirst. Compassion can act strongly while relinquishing possessive control.

Useful distinction:
- compassion: "May suffering be reduced; what can wisely be done?"
- attachment disguised as care: "You must become what I need you to become."
- indifference: "Your suffering is not my concern."

This three-way distinction is a project interpretation, not a verbatim canonical formula.

## Guardrails

- Do not say every desire is taṇhā; right effort itself includes active motivation.
- Do not equate compassion with emotional distress about another person's distress.
- Do not use non-attachment as an excuse for inaction where help is possible and appropriate.
- Do not convert care into entitlement to control another person.
- Milindapañha remains a later dialectical layer, not an early-Nikāya quotation.
