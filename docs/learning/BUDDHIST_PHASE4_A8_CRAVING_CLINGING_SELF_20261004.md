# PHASE 4 — A8 — FROM CRAVING TO CLINGING: HOW "I / MINE" IS BUILT

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: What changes when taṇhā becomes upādāna, and how can self-identification be constructed without a permanent self?

SN 12.2:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: craving is analyzed in six classes tied to sense objects.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: clinging is analyzed as four kinds: sensual clinging, clinging to views, clinging to rules/observances, and clinging to a doctrine of self.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: upādāna is not merely "more wanting"; it is wanting stabilized into appropriation, commitment, and identity-bearing grasping.

MN 44:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: self-identification is the five clinging-aggregates.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: clinging is neither simply identical with the five aggregates nor something completely separate; desire and passion regarding the five clinging-aggregates is the clinging there.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: self-identification view treats each aggregate through patterns such as self = aggregate, self possesses aggregate, aggregate in self, or self in aggregate.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: "I / mine" is constructed by the relation of appropriation to conditioned aggregates, not by discovering an extra permanent entity behind them.

SN 22.22:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the burden is the five clinging-aggregates; the burden-bearer is conventionally called the person; taking up the burden is craving leading to renewed becoming; laying it down is the cessation/relinquishment of that craving.
- Guardrail: ordinary person-language is retained. This does not by itself establish a later formal two-truths doctrine.

SN 22.59:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: all five aggregates are not-self and are to be seen as "not mine, not I, not my self"; such seeing leads to disenchantment, dispassion, and liberation.
- Bounded SN 22 audit slice: SN 22.22 and SN 22.59 are audited for the A8 question only. The full SN 22 ID-level backlog remains open.

SN 12.52:
- TEXT_ATTESTED: attending to gratification in clingable phenomena grows craving, then clinging, becoming, birth, and the mass of suffering.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: the move from craving to clinging is a transformation in relation to experience: "wanting this" hardens into "this is mine / this view is mine / this identity is me."

MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC:
- Nāgasena's chariot argument preserves ordinary designation while rejecting a permanent individuality hidden behind the parts.
- King Milinda's challenge ("who acts, keeps precepts, receives consequences?") is answered without requiring a permanent substance-person.
- Use this as an argument stress-test, not as proof that every later conventional/ultimate distinction is already fully formulated in the earliest texts.

Main conclusion:
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: craving is the thirst toward experience; clinging is the taking-up and stabilization of that thirst into possession, view, practice, or self-identification. The aggregates need not contain a permanent self for "I / mine" to be generated; appropriation itself can make a conditioned process function as an identity.
