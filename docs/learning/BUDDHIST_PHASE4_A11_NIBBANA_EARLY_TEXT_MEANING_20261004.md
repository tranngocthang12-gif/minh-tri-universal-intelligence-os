# PHASE 4 — A11 — WHAT IS NIBBĀNA IN THE EARLY TEXTS?

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: Is Nibbāna merely cessation, the unconditioned, or a "thing"? How should positive and negative language be read without turning Nibbāna into either a substance or sheer nonexistence?

## SN 43
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the unconditioned (asaṅkhata) is defined as the ending of greed, hate, and delusion.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the discourses distinguish the unconditioned from the paths leading to it; different practices are named as paths to the unconditioned.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: SN 43 also supplies a family of names for the goal, including peace, deathless, ending of craving, extinguishment, dispassion, purity, release, refuge, and not-clinging.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this lexical field is functional and liberative; it does not require positing a permanent metaphysical substance.

## AN 3.47 / unconditioned characteristics
- TEXT_ATTESTED: conditioned phenomena are marked by evident arising, vanishing, and alteration while persisting.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: for the unconditioned, arising is not evident, vanishing is not evident, and alteration while persisting is not evident.
- Guardrail: this text distinguishes conditioned from unconditioned; it does not by itself define a substance-ontology.

## Ud 8.1 and Ud 8.3 / Iti 43
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Ud 8.1 speaks of a dimension where ordinary elemental, cosmological, and coming/going/arising/passing-away categories do not apply; it identifies this as the end of suffering.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Ud 8.3 and Iti 43 speak of an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unfabricated/unconditioned escape from the born, become, made, fabricated.
- Guardrail: positive grammar such as "there is" does not automatically settle the metaphysical category of Nibbāna as an independently existing substance.

## Iti 44 — route correction/addition
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Iti 44, not Iti 43, is the discourse on the two Nibbāna elements: with residue and without residue.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: with residue, an arahant's five sense faculties remain; agreeable/disagreeable experience and pleasure/pain can still occur, while greed, hate, and delusion are ended.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: without residue, the discourse speaks of all that is felt, no longer relished, becoming cool; the verse says all modes of being cease.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: these are two reference conditions of extinguishment in relation to the living arahant and the ending of the remaining psychophysical process, not evidence that there are two different eternal "places".

## MN 26
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the noble search seeks the unborn, unaging, unailing, deathless, sorrowless, undefiled, unsurpassed security from bondage—Nibbāna.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: early texts speak of Nibbāna both negatively (ending, cessation, no arising) and positively (security, peace, deathless). Neither register alone should be absolutized.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Nāgasena speaks of entering the "City of Nibbāna", described as birthless, ageless, deathless, cooled, blissful, and fearless. This is an explanatory metaphorical layer that protects the goal from being reduced to sheer nihilistic absence.
- Guardrail: the City metaphor is later and should not be back-projected as an early-Nikāya ontological definition.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
In the early texts, Nibbāna is most securely characterized by a cluster of functions and contrasts: the ending of greed, hate, and delusion; the ending of craving and suffering; the unconditioned in contrast to conditioned arising/vanishing/change; the unborn/unmade/unfabricated escape; peace, security, deathless, release. The texts resist both reduction to a permanent self-like substance and reduction to sheer annihilative nonexistence.

## Important route corrections
1. Iti 43 parallels the unborn/unmade/unconditioned escape language.
2. Iti 44 is the direct source for the two Nibbāna elements with and without residue.
3. Use AN 3.47/48 numbering with edition/interface caution; cite the conditioned/unconditioned characteristic passage by content when numbering differs.
