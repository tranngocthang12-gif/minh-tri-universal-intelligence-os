## Research question

How do taṇhā/upādāna, diṭṭhi/māna/mamatta and papañca interact? Is selfing best understood as one downstream chain, or as a recursive network of craving, grasping, construing and comparison?

## TEXTUAL FINDING

### 1. SN 35.248 / SĀ 1168: self-referential construing appears simultaneously as maññita, papañcita and māna-related activity

SN 35.248 treats thoughts such as:
- “I am,”
- “I am this,”
- “I shall be / shall not be,”
- “I shall be formed / formless,”
- future identity in terms of perception,

as multiple overlapping processes: conceiving (maññita/maññanā), agitation, proliferation (papañcita) and conceit (māna).

The sutta then gives the operational principle:
- when one conceives, one is bound by Māra;
- not conceiving, one is free from that bond.

This is strong evidence that selfing is not represented as one single mental factor at one point in a fixed chain. The same self-referential content can be described under several interacting modes.

### 2. SN 22.89 / SĀ 103: self-view and residual “I am” are not identical

Khemaka does not identify any aggregate as self or as belonging to self, yet residual:
- “I am” conceit,
- “I am” desire,
- “I am” underlying tendency

remain in relation to the five clinging aggregates.

This directly blocks a model in which removing an explicit self-view automatically removes all selfing. It also implies that diṭṭhi, taṇhā/chanda and māna/anusaya can dissociate to some extent.

### 3. Sn 4.9 Māgandiya: comparison and dispute are tied to self-positioning

Sn 4.9 links construing oneself as:
- equal,
- superior,
- inferior

with dispute. The person no longer moved by those three comparisons has no basis for that type of quarrel.

The discourse also rejects purification by simply grasping views, learning, knowledge, rites or vows, while also rejecting the opposite simplification that mere absence of views/learning is itself purity.

This supports the distinction between using knowledge/view and taking it as identity or support.

### 4. Sn 4.11 Kalahavivāda: possessiveness, desire, perception and papañca are connected but not arranged as one universal linear chain

Sn 4.11 traces quarrel/conflict backward through desire and related conditions. It states:
- acquisitions/appropriations arise from desire;
- when desire is absent there is no mamatta (“mine-making”/possessiveness);
- papañca-related constructions have saññā as their condition.

This does not give one complete formula of selfing, but it links:
desire ↔ appropriation/mine-making
and
perception ↔ papañca.

The text therefore supports multiple conditional entry points into the same conflict-producing structure.

### 5. Sn 4.14 Tuvaṭaka: “I am the thinker/knower” is presented as a root of papañca-related constructions

The opening instruction targets the root idea represented by the phrase commonly rendered along the lines of “I am a thinker/wise one” (mantā asmīti), described as the root of papañca-related constructions.

The exact philology of mantā is debated, so the safe conclusion is not a fixed translation, but that the verse directly associates a self-referential cognitive stance with the root of papañca.

### 6. The emerging picture is recursive, not merely sequential

The texts support distinct but overlapping relations:
- feeling can condition craving;
- craving/desire can condition grasping/appropriation;
- appropriation creates “mine” structures;
- self-referential construing can be described as maññanā, papañca and māna;
- comparison feeds dispute;
- views can themselves become objects of grasping;
- residual “I am” can survive after explicit self-view weakens;
- perception feeds papañca, which can in turn generate more self-referential categories and conflict.

Therefore a single universal formula such as:

vedanā → taṇhā → upādāna → papañca → self

is not justified.

A better working model is a **recursive network** in which several conditioned processes can reinforce each other.

## WORKING SYNTHESIS

A non-canonical model:

sensory contact / feeling
↘
craving / aversion / compensatory seeking
↘
appropriation: “mine”
↔ self-construal: “I am / I am this”
↔ comparison: equal / superior / inferior
↔ views and identity commitments
↔ papañca: expanding self/world narratives
↘
conflict, defense, further feeling and craving

This is a synthesis for testing, not a formula stated verbatim in any single sutta.

## PARALLEL STATUS

- SN 22.89 ↔ SĀ 103: strong parallel support for residual “I am” after explicit self-view is weakened.
- SN 35.248 ↔ SĀ 1168: direct parallel family preserving the self/future-existence cluster; exact mapping of every Pāli technical term should remain cautious.
- Sn 4.9/4.11/4.14 belong to Aṭṭhakavagga; Arthapada/T198 parallels remain important for checking whether these structures survive outside the Pāli recension.
- The T198 alignment for each exact verse cluster remains incomplete and is the next philological task.

## MILINDA STATUS

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE
Milindapañha’s chariot analysis is compatible with the distinction between conventional person-language and lack of an invariant self-substance.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE
Its continuity examples (lamp, milk transformations, child-to-adult) fit a process model in which causal continuity does not require an identical essence.

### MILINDA_EXTENSION
Milinda tends to analyze mental functions more systematically than the early suttas. This is useful, but should not be allowed to freeze the recursive early-text material into a later fixed ontology of discrete mental factors.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Selfing is just wrong belief.” — SN 22.89 shows residual “I am” after explicit self-identification is denied.
2. “Selfing is just craving.” — the texts distinguish craving/desire, conceit, views, underlying tendencies, construing and papañca.
3. “Papañca causes all selfing in one direction.” — current evidence also shows self-referential stances can be roots/contents of papañca.
4. “No-self means no conventional person.” — not supported; conventional person-language remains functional.
5. “Freedom means deleting perception or thought.” — contradicted by wholesome perception, right view and trained thought elsewhere in the early texts.
6. “One canonical chain explains all cognition.” — contradicted by the coexistence of several analytical schemas and reciprocal conditionality elsewhere in SN 12.

## CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- explicit self-view and residual “I am” are not identical;
- self-referential construing overlaps with conceit and papañca;
- equal/superior/inferior comparison is a conflict-generating self-positioning process;
- desire is closely linked with appropriation/mine-making;
- the early-text material does not support reducing selfing to one single mental event.

**EBT_PROBABLE**
- selfing is best modeled as a recursive network rather than a single downstream chain.
- papañca both expresses and reinforces self/world construction.

**OPEN**
- exact causal direction among taṇhā, upādāna, papañca and māna in all contexts;
- precise semantic relation between papañca, papañcasaññāsaṅkhā and self-referential thought across all early strata;
- full Arthapada/T198 confirmation for Sn 4.9/4.11/4.14.

## PRACTICAL MEANING

When conflict arises, do not look only for “the wrong belief.”

Observe several layers:
- feeling tone;
- wanting / resisting;
- “mine”;
- “I am this kind of person”;
- comparison;
- defending a view/status;
- story expansion across past/future;
- renewed emotional reaction.

The practical hypothesis is that weakening any one of these links can reduce the feedback loop, but the texts do not authorize claiming that there is only one intervention point.
