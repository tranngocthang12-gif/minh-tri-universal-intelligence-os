# PHASE 4 — A16 — RESIDUAL "I AM": SAKKĀYA-DIṬṬHI VS ASMIMĀNA

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: How can self-view cease while the residual conceit "I am" remains? What exactly is being removed at different stages of insight?

## SN 22.89 — Khemaka
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Khemaka does not regard any of the five grasping aggregates as self or as belonging to self, yet he is not yet fully liberated.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: with regard to the five grasping aggregates, a residual conceit "I am", desire "I am", and underlying tendency "I am" still remain.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Khemaka explicitly refuses to identify "I am" with any aggregate or with something apart from the aggregates.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: contemplating the arising and passing away of the five grasping aggregates uproots this residual "I am" conceit/desire/tendency.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the stained-cloth analogy shows how a coarse impurity can be removed while a subtler residual scent remains.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: self-view and residual selfing are therefore distinct layers; correct non-self doctrine does not automatically erase the felt/affective/conceit structure of "I am".

## MN 44 / SN 22.47 / SN 22.59
- TEXT_ATTESTED: sakkāya-diṭṭhi takes the aggregates through identity/possession/inclusion patterns such as "form is self", "self possesses form", "form is in self", or "self is in form", and similarly for the other aggregates.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: SN 22.59 instructs seeing each aggregate as "not mine, not I, not my self", leading to disenchantment, dispassion, and liberation.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: sakkāya-diṭṭhi is a doctrinal/structural identification; asmimāna is subtler because it can remain after those explicit identity positions have been abandoned.

## Aṭṭhakavagga bounded lexical audit
### Snp 4.6 Jarā
- TEXT_ATTESTED / lexical: `maññati` occurs in "what a person conceives 'this is mine'" and again where the cleansed person does not conceive in terms of what is seen/heard/sensed.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: this lexical field closely matches the movement from perception to appropriation: not merely seeing an object, but construing it as "mine" or in relation to self.
- AUDIT BOUNDARY: this closes only a bounded `maññati` slice in Snp 4.6.

### Snp 4.11 Kalahavivāda
- TEXT_ATTESTED / lexical: `papañcasaṅkhā` appears in the formula `saññānidānā hi papañcasaṅkhā`, linking proliferating/classifying elaborations to perception.
- SOURCE CAUTION: the exact semantic range of `papañca` is contested across translators and scholarship; do not reduce it to a single English gloss such as "thinking too much".
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: for this learning track, `papañca` is treated cautiously as the elaborative/objectifying process that can feed identity, distinction, and conflict, without claiming a single exhaustive lexical definition.
- AUDIT BOUNDARY: this closes only a bounded `papañca` slice in Snp 4.11. Wider Aṭṭhakavagga coverage remains open.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: the chariot/Nāgasena discussion distinguishes conventional designation from a permanent person-substance.
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: a later pitcher simile explicitly warns that one who has reached high attainment in learning and practice should not generate conceit or arrogance.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: Mi Tiên therefore helps distinguish two errors: reifying the conventional person into a substance, and then rebuilding selfhood as conceit around learning or attainment.
- Guardrail: Milindapañha is later than the early Nikāya layer and is used here as dialectical clarification, not as direct evidence for the earliest wording.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
`sakkāya-diṭṭhi` and `asmimāna` are not identical. The first is the view-structure that identifies the aggregates as self or as belonging to self. The second is a subtler residual self-referential stance—the felt conceit "I am"—that may persist even when one no longer endorses an explicit self-theory. SN 22.89 shows that final uprooting occurs through continued contemplation of arising and passing away until even this residual appropriation loses its basis.

## Guardrails
- Do not equate ordinary first-person grammar ("I walk", "I speak") with asmimāna by itself.
- Do not say "there is no person at all"; early texts and Milindapañha still preserve conventional person-language.
- Do not treat every use of `maññati` or `papañca` as identical with asmimāna; they are related analytical fields, not synonyms.
- Do not infer that understanding anattā conceptually equals arahantship.
