## Research question

If selfing is not removed merely by adopting the proposition “there is no self,” what counts as liberating knowledge in the early texts? How should sammādiṭṭhi (right view), diṭṭhupādāna (clinging to views), paññā (discernment/wisdom), and yathābhūta-ñāṇa-dassana be related?

## TEXTUAL FINDING

### 1. MN 9 and its parallels: right view is a causal diagnostic method, not merely one metaphysical proposition

MN 9 repeatedly defines a noble disciple with right view in terms of understanding:
- a phenomenon/problem,
- its origination,
- its cessation,
- the path leading to its cessation.

This framework is applied to wholesome/unwholesome conduct, nutriment, the four truths, and many dependent-arising factors.

The major architecture has parallels in MĀ 29, SĀ 344, and a Sanskrit fragment. Comparative work shows that the Pāli, Chinese, and Sanskrit versions share the core progression through these analytical topics, although ordering and some sections differ.

Methodological correction: the repeated Pāli passage that immediately couples right view with fully uprooting greed/aversion, “I am” conceit, ignorance, and ending suffering is absent from the Chinese and Sanskrit parallels and has been judged textually problematic by comparative scholarship. Do not use that repeated Pāli formula as unqualified cross-tradition proof that the definition of right view itself already equals arahantship.

### 2. MN 117 and parallels: right view leads, but the Pāli two-level technical taxonomy is not equally early in all witnesses

MN 117 presents right view as the forerunner of the path: it discerns wrong as wrong and right as right, and is coordinated with right effort and right mindfulness.

The role of right view as precursor to the path is preserved in MĀ 189 and the Tibetan parallel.

The Pāli MN 117 additionally distinguishes:
- right view “with effluents,” associated with merit/acquisitions;
- noble, undefiled/transcendent right view as a path factor.

This exact twofold technical analysis is not preserved in the Chinese and Tibetan parallels. Therefore:
- **EBT_STRONG:** right view is directional/diagnostic and coordinates the path;
- **EBT_PROBABLE / PĀLI-SPECIFIC FORMULATION:** the exact sāsava/anāsava two-tier taxonomy in MN 117.

### 3. Right view and view-clinging are not the same thing

MN 9 defines diṭṭhupādāna as one of the four kinds of clinging and states that the noble eightfold path is the way to abandon clinging.

This is crucial:
- the path includes right view;
- the path is used to abandon clinging to views.

Therefore early Buddhist thought cannot be summarized as either:
- “all views are equally bad,” or
- “correct doctrine should be possessed as identity.”

MN 22’s raft simile reinforces the instrumental principle: Dhamma is for crossing, not holding onto. A view/teaching can be functionally right and still become an object of clinging if appropriated.

### 4. SN 22.89: liberating insight concerns arising and passing, not merely replacing one proposition with another

Khemaka still has residual “I am” conceit/desire/tendency despite no longer identifying a specific aggregate as self.

The remaining residue is removed by repeatedly attending to:
- what form is, its origination, its disappearance;
- likewise feeling, perception, formations, consciousness.

This supports a distinction between:
- **propositional correction**: no longer explicitly identifying an aggregate as self;
- **transformative seeing**: sustained seeing of arising/disappearance that removes residual selfing tendencies.

### 5. SN 12.23: knowledge and vision is conditioned and embedded in training

SN 12.23 states that the ending of effluents is for one who knows and sees, not one who does not know and see. It defines the relevant seeing through the aggregates in terms of their nature, origination, and disappearance.

It then gives a positive conditional sequence:
dukkha → confidence → joy → rapture → calm → pleasure → concentration → knowledge-and-vision “as it has come to be/as it really is” → disenchantment → dispassion → release → knowledge of ending.

Important implication:
liberating knowledge is not depicted as free-floating intellectual assent. It is conditionally supported by the path, especially concentration, and leads onward to disenchantment, dispassion, and release.

### 6. “Knowing as it is/as it has come to be” should not be reduced to passive description

The phrase yathābhūta-ñāṇa-dassana is translated in more than one way (“as it really is,” “as it has come to be”). Regardless of translation choice, the surrounding early-text usage in SN 12.23 and SN 22.89 is strongly process-oriented:
- origination,
- disappearance,
- conditioned arising,
- conditioned cessation.

Working conclusion:
the liberating function is causal/process insight, not mere cataloguing of static objects.

## SYNTHESIS

A working non-canonical model:

right view
= correctly orienting inquiry toward
problem / cause / cessation / practice
+
discerning skillful from unskillful
+
seeing conditioned arising and passing

right effort
= abandons what is seen as wrong/unskillful and develops the skillful

right mindfulness
= keeps the task and distinction present

concentration
= stabilizes the conditions for penetrative knowing/seeing

paññā / knowledge-and-vision
= sees the relevant processes deeply enough to undermine craving, appropriation and “I am” tendencies

release
= not a better identity built from the right doctrine, but the ending of the processes that require clinging to identity.

This is a synthesis from several discourses, not a single canonical formula.

## RIGHT VIEW VS VIEW-CLINGING

The emerging distinction:

**Right view**
- evaluates causes and consequences;
- identifies suffering and its conditions;
- guides practice;
- can revise wrong view;
- contains a functional orientation toward cessation.

**View-clinging**
- appropriates a position as possession/identity;
- treats holding the formulation as the goal;
- generates defense/comparison/conflict;
- resists letting the tool go after its function is complete.

Thus the Buddha’s critique of views is best read as a critique of wrong views and clinging/identity around views, not as a prohibition on all discernment or all provisional views.

## MILINDA STATUS

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE
Milindapañha distinguishes yoniso manasikāra/manasikāra from paññā. Nāgasena says attention and wisdom are not the same. In the barley-reaper simile, attention “takes hold” of the mind and wisdom “cuts” defilements.

This fits the early-text pattern in which attending/observing is necessary but not identical with liberating discernment.

### MILINDA_COMPATIBLE / LATER SYSTEMATIZATION
Milindapañha further describes wisdom as illuminating: it dispels the darkness of ignorance and makes the noble truths clear.

This is compatible with MN 9/SN 12.23, but its neat functional definitions are later systematization and should not be read back as exact historical definitions used by the Buddha.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Liberation means believing the correct metaphysical proposition.” — SN 22.89 shows explicit self-view can weaken while residual selfing remains.
2. “All views are bondage, therefore right view is unnecessary.” — MN 117 and path formulas make right view a prerequisite/path factor.
3. “Once one has right view, one should cling to it forever as identity.” — diṭṭhupādāna is clinging to be abandoned; MN 22’s raft principle blocks possession of Dhamma as the goal.
4. “Knowledge alone in the abstract liberates.” — SN 12.23 embeds liberating knowing in a cultivated sequence including confidence, joy, calm and concentration.
5. “MN 117’s exact two-tier right-view taxonomy is cross-tradition certain.” — the Chinese and Tibetan parallels do not preserve that exact technical distinction.
6. “Every repeated arahantship formula in MN 9 is original to the common ancestor.” — comparative evidence makes this uncertain; the Chinese/Sanskrit parallels lack the repeated passage.

## CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG**
- right view is a path factor and directional precursor;
- right view involves causal/ethical discrimination rather than mere slogan repetition;
- view-clinging is itself a form of clinging to be abandoned;
- seeing arising and passing is central to removing residual “I am” tendencies;
- liberating knowledge is embedded in cultivated conditions and leads to disenchantment/dispassion/release.

**EBT_PROBABLE**
- the most faithful high-level description of liberating knowledge is “causal-process insight with practical orientation toward cessation.”
- right view functions like a self-transcending tool: it is used to undermine the very clinging mechanism that could appropriate it.

**OPEN / TEXT-CRITICAL**
- exact historical status of the Pāli-only twofold “right view with effluents / noble undefiled right view” analysis in MN 117.
- exact historical status of the repeated arahantship refrain in the Pāli MN 9.
- best philological translation of yathābhūta in all contexts.

## PRACTICAL MEANING

The training question is not only:
> “What doctrine do I believe?”

but:
- What am I treating as the problem?
- What condition is feeding it?
- What happens when that condition is absent?
- What action/practice changes the condition?
- Am I using the teaching to investigate, or using the teaching to construct an identity?
- Can I see arising and passing in experience rather than merely recite “impermanent/no-self”?

A useful practical distinction:
**attention notices and holds the investigation; wisdom changes the relationship to the process by seeing enough to cut attachment.**
This wording is partly Milinda-inspired and remains a practical model, not a direct canonical definition.
