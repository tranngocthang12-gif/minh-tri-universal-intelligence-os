## A143 — wise attention inside relationships and emotion

Anchors: MN 2, SN 46.51, AN 5.161, prior A86–A88.

Core:
- wise attention is not positive thinking or denial;
- attention can feed or starve hindrances;
- in anger, distinguish the factual harm from repeated fixation on the irritation-sign;
- in attachment, preserve value without converting it into possession;
- in compassion, attend to suffering, causes, agency, and actual possible help rather than a savior identity;
- in praise/fear, separate information and real danger from self-construction.

Milindapanha:
Consulted as later/paracanonical clarification that attention/reasoning functions are distinct from wisdom's cutting function; no independent Milindapanha definition of relational yoniso manasikāra is promoted.

## A144 — from selected feature to papañca and relational conflict

Central question:
After attention selects a feature, how do perception, interpretation, repetition, imagined motives, and self-positioning turn a small relational event into a large inner conflict?

### TEXT_ATTESTED

1. MN 18 gives the central sequence: dependent on sense base and object, consciousness arises; their meeting is contact; with contact as condition there is feeling; what one feels one perceives; what one perceives one thinks about; what one thinks about one proliferates; from that, the categories/perceptions associated with papañca assail the person regarding past, future, and present sense experience.
2. MN 18 also states the cessation-side condition: where there is nothing to delight in, welcome, or hold to, the underlying tendencies and conflict-producing processes cease. This ties papañca not merely to “having many thoughts” but to grasping/reactive involvement.
3. Prior A126 remains a guardrail: papañca is not safely reduced to the modern phrase “overthinking,” saññā is not identical to autobiographical memory, and maññanā/self-appropriation must not be silently collapsed into papañca as if the terms were synonyms.
4. Aṭṭhakavagga conflict material, especially quarrel/dispute passages, stress-tests the relational application: disputes are bound up with desire, what is held dear, comparison, positions, and the structures by which people become opposed. This is supporting early-text context, not a lexical definition of papañca.
5. A143 wise-attention material supplies the upstream intervention: what is repeatedly selected for attention can feed hindrances before full proliferation develops.

### EVENT != STORY

A144 distinguishes:
- event: what was actually seen/heard/remembered;
- perception/recognition: how it was identified;
- thought: what was mentally formulated about it;
- proliferation: expansion into chains, categories, comparisons, imagined implications, and repeated scenarios;
- self-positioning: organizing the whole construction around “me,” “mine,” “what they did to me,” or “what kind of person they are.”

This five-layer map is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS, not a canonical five-step taxonomy.

### HOW A SMALL EVENT BECOMES A LARGE INNER CONFLICT

A guarded relational application:

“they did not reply”
-> unpleasant feeling
-> “they are ignoring me”
-> “they do not respect me”
-> “they always treat me this way”
-> imagined motives and future scenarios
-> “I am being devalued”
-> resentment, fear, defensive speech/action.

Only the first event in this illustration is directly observable; the later steps contain interpretation and proliferation.

The example is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION, not a canonical quotation.

### PAPAÑCA DOES NOT MEAN ALL THINKING

MN 18 itself includes vitakka/thought before papañca.

Therefore A144 rejects:
“all thought is papañca.”

Planning, remembering, analyzing, and ethical reflection can occur without the same proliferative self-conflict structure.

The practical question is not:
“Am I thinking?”
but:
“Is thought expanding into grasping, comparison, self-positioning, hostility, and compulsive replay?”

This formulation is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### IMAGINED MOTIVES

Relational conflict is often intensified by treating inferred motives as observed facts.

A144 distinguishes:
- observed act: “they said/did X”;
- inference: “they intended Y”;
- proliferation: repeated elaboration of Y into character judgments, future predictions, and identity-threat.

The early texts do not provide a modern technical category called “mind-reading.”
This is a guarded CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION of MN 18's thought/proliferation process.

### PAST AND FUTURE AS FUEL

MN 18 explicitly extends papañca-related categories across past, future, and present experience.

In relational application:
- past: replay old injuries;
- future: imagine retaliation, abandonment, humiliation, or loss;
- present: reinterpret current cues through the established story.

Thus the original event can end while the conflict continues through re-presentation.

This is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS anchored in MN 18's temporal range.

### SELF-POSITIONING

A126 showed how MN 1 and SN 22.89 help explain appropriation.

A144 keeps terms distinct but uses them together:
papañca can generate an increasingly elaborate field;
maññanā/māna/upādāna can organize that field around “I/me/mine,” rank, and possession.

This combined model is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS and must not be presented as one canonical chain.

### ATTHAKAVAGGA STRESS-TEST

The Aṭṭhakavagga repeatedly warns about dispute, positions, comparison, and attachment to what is held as true or dear.

A144 uses this as a check:
if a relational interpretation increasingly produces “my side/your side,” victory/defeat, superior/inferior, and fixation on a standpoint, the process is moving in the same conflict-generating direction.

Guardrail:
Aṭṭhakavagga material is not used to redefine papañca lexically.

### INTERRUPTING THE CASCADE

A144 yields multiple intervention points:
1. return to the observable event;
2. name feeling without turning it into verdict;
3. distinguish perception/label from fact;
4. mark inference as inference;
5. stop repeated rehearsal when it adds no actionable truth;
6. test whether “me/mine/status” has entered;
7. return to wise attention: what reduces greed, hatred, delusion while preserving the facts?
8. choose right speech/action from the clarified situation.

This exact sequence is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS.

### MILINDAPANHA — MANDATORY

Consulted: YES.

No decisive Milindapanha passage is used to define papañca.

Relevant later support is limited to:
- question decomposition and distinction-making;
- conventional designation/chariot reasoning as a guardrail against turning constructed labels into fixed essences;
- reasoning-vs-wisdom distinctions.

Role: later/paracanonical support only.

Guardrail:
Milindapanha does not override MN 18 or supply the lexical meaning of papañca.

### CORRECTIONS PRESERVED

- papañca != all thinking;
- papañca != simply modern “overthinking”;
- saññā != memory one-to-one;
- inferred motive != observed fact;
- stopping proliferation != suppressing necessary analysis;
- returning to facts != denying feeling;
- self-positioning terms and papañca are related in synthesis but not treated as synonyms;
- Aṭṭhakavagga is a conflict stress-test, not a lexical dictionary for papañca;
- Milindapanha adds no decisive independent papañca definition here.
