# PHASE 4 — A28 — TAṆHĀ VS WHOLESOME MOTIVATION / CHANDA

**Status:** CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Question

If the path ends craving, why does the path itself require desire, effort, aspiration, and determination? Is every "wanting" taṇhā?

## Primary-text findings

### SN 56.11
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the origin of suffering is identified as craving that leads to renewed becoming, accompanied by passion/delight and relishing here and there.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: the discourse specifies sensual craving, craving for becoming, and craving for non-becoming.
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: cessation is the fading, relinquishment, release, and letting go of that craving.
- Guardrail: the target of cessation here is not every possible motivational state indiscriminately.

### SN 45.8
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: right effort explicitly includes generating desire/enthusiasm, striving, arousing energy, and applying the mind to prevent unskillful states, abandon arisen unskillful states, arouse skillful states, and maintain/develop arisen skillful states.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: the path therefore cannot mean "eliminate all wanting immediately," because its own practice uses directed motivation.

### SN 51.13 / SN 51.1
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: a basis of power can be founded on chanda/desire, together with concentration and active effort.
- `LEXICAL_GUARDRAIL`: chanda is context-sensitive; the word should not be treated as automatically wholesome in every occurrence. It can be cultivated toward the path, while related compounds such as sensual desire occur in unskillful contexts.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: the important distinction is functional and causal, not the English word "desire" by itself.

### AN 6.63
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: intention (`cetanā`) is identified with kamma in the sense that, having intended, one acts by body, speech, and mind.
- Guardrail: intention, chanda, and taṇhā are not interchangeable terms. A motivated act may involve intention without thereby being identical with craving.

### SN 12.52
- `TEXT_ATTESTED`: craving grows when attention dwells on gratification in what can be clung to; when drawbacks are seen, craving ceases and downstream clinging/becoming cease.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: taṇhā is marked by its relation to gratification, appropriation, and renewed becoming, whereas path-motivation is directed toward abandoning the conditions of suffering.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer

### Mil 7.3.8
- `MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC`: Nāgasena praises the practitioner who, like a pilot, works day and night with continuous zeal and effort in regulating the mind and distinguishing good from bad.
- `CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`: the later dialectic reinforces the early-text point that liberation training requires energetic motivation; the problem is not motivation as such but motivation rooted in and feeding craving, ignorance, and appropriation.

## Main conclusion

`CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS`:
Taṇhā is not simply the grammatical fact of "wanting X." In the dependent-origination and noble-truth framework, it is craving tied to gratification, passion/delight, appropriation, and renewed becoming. Wholesome chanda/path motivation is a task-oriented willingness or enthusiasm to abandon unskillful states, cultivate skillful states, understand, and release. It is evaluated by what it is rooted in, what it takes as its object, and what causal direction it produces.

Useful diagnostic:
- taṇhā: "I need this to satisfy/complete/secure me; I grasp it as mine or as what I must become."
- wholesome path-motivation: "This is skillful and leads toward abandoning harm and delusion; cultivate it."
- mere absence of motivation: not liberation; it may simply be laziness or indifference.

## Important nuance

Do not simplify this into "taṇhā = bad desire, chanda = good desire." `Chanda` is context-sensitive, and the texts evaluate mental states by context, object, roots, and results. The safe claim is narrower: right effort and bases-of-power passages prove that directed desire/enthusiasm can function wholesomely on the path, so "all desire is forbidden" is false.

## Guardrails

- Do not translate every occurrence of `chanda` as spiritually wholesome desire.
- Do not call every intention `taṇhā`.
- Do not infer that strong motivation is attachment merely because it is strong.
- Do not use "wholesome motivation" to excuse greed, status-seeking, or identity-building in religious form.
- Milindapañha remains a later dialectical layer.
