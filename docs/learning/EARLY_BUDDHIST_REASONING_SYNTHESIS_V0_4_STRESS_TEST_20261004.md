## 2. MN 22 — correct doctrine can still be grasped wrongly

This discourse adds a decisive meta-level principle:
**truth content and mode of appropriation must be evaluated separately.**

Ariṭṭha misreads the teaching on sensual obstruction.
The snake simile then generalizes the problem:
- one may learn genuine Dhamma;
- grasp it for debate, status or attack;
- fail to examine its meaning;
- and be harmed by the very teaching one claims to know.

The raft simile adds:
- Dhamma is instrumental toward crossing;
- it is not an identity-object to carry indefinitely;
- therefore "attachment to right doctrine" can itself become a contradiction if it defeats the liberative function of doctrine.

### Revision
The model now requires two audits for any view:
1. **CONTENT AUDIT** — is the interpretation textually/causally sound?
2. **GRASPING AUDIT** — what is the mind doing with the interpretation?

A proposition can be correct while its appropriation is unskillful.

## 3. MN 58 — speech ethics is truth + benefit + timing, not niceness

Prince Abhaya is sent with a binary trap:
- does the Tathāgata say unpleasant things or not?

The response rejects the trap by introducing multiple dimensions:
- true/false;
- beneficial/unbeneficial;
- pleasant/unpleasant;
- right timing.

### Strong result
"Kind speech" cannot be reduced to:
- saying only what people like;
- avoiding difficult truths.

The operative structure is:
- false + unbeneficial -> do not say;
- true + unbeneficial -> do not say;
- true + beneficial -> know the time, whether pleasant or unpleasant.

### Revision
Add a **SPEECH GATE**:
1. Is it true?
2. Is it beneficial for the relevant aim?
3. Is this the right time/context?
4. What tone minimizes unnecessary harm without sacrificing truth?

Compassion disciplines truth-telling; it does not replace truth.

## 4. MN 61 — ethics is an iterative control loop

Rāhula is taught to examine bodily, verbal and mental actions:
- before acting;
- while acting;
- after acting.

The key test is harm:
- harm to self;
- harm to others;
- harm to both;
along with skillful/unskillful consequences.

### Revision
Early Buddhist ethics is not only rule compliance.
It includes a **feedback-control architecture**:

INTENTION -> PREDICT CONSEQUENCE -> ACT/WITHHOLD -> MONITOR -> REVIEW RESULT -> CONFESS/CORRECT OR CULTIVATE.

This is a learning loop embedded inside ethical practice.

### Important limit
This consequence-sensitive structure is not simple consequentialism:
- truthfulness;
- mental roots;
- training commitments;
- character formation
remain constitutive constraints.

## 5. MN 60 — reasoning under uncertainty without pretending certainty

MN 60 compares opposed doctrines under uncertainty:
- moral causality vs no moral causality;
- action vs no action;
- causation vs no causation;
- further existence vs denial of it.

The discourse sometimes uses a prudential asymmetry:
- a view may carry ethical benefit here-and-now even if an uncertain future claim were false;
- the opposed view may carry downside under both present and possible future conditions.

### Revision
Add **ASYMMETRIC-RISK REASONING**:
When evidence is incomplete:
1. state uncertainty honestly;
2. compare practical consequences under competing hypotheses;
3. prefer the option that avoids grave ethical downside while remaining open to correction.

### Strong caution
Do not reduce MN 60 to a modern "Pascal's wager."
The discourse embeds prudential reasoning inside a larger moral/causal framework and also asserts substantive right/wrong views.

## 6. MN 117 — path factors form a coordinated system

MN 117 presents right view as leading and shows:
- right view;
- right effort;
- right mindfulness
circling around and supporting other path factors.

The Pali text also distinguishes forms of right view/right speech/etc. associated with merit and attachment from noble/transcendent path factors.

### Comparative warning
The explicit two-level technical analysis is not identically preserved in the noted Chinese/Tibetan parallels.
Therefore:
- the coordinated path architecture is strong;
- the exact scholastic two-level formulation should receive lower historical-confidence weighting until broader parallel analysis is complete.

### Revision
Do not model the Eightfold Path as eight independent checklist items.
Model it as a **mutually constraining control system** with right view guiding discrimination and effort/mindfulness maintaining correction.

## 7. SN 12.15 — middle reasoning is anti-binary, not midpoint compromise

The discourse rejects:
- "everything exists";
- "nothing exists";
by teaching dependent arising and cessation.

### Revision
"Middle way" here must not mean:
- choose 50% of each side;
- compromise politically;
- split the difference.

It means:
- reject a false ontological binary;
- replace it with conditional analysis.

## 8. SN 22.85 — not-self must not become annihilationism

Yamaka concludes that an arahant is annihilated after death.
Sāriputta dismantles the claim by asking whether the Tathāgata is:
- identical with an aggregate;
- inside it;
- outside it;
- the collection;
- independent of them.

The teaching blocks a move from:
- "no permanent appropriable self"
to:
- "therefore a real entity is destroyed."

### Revision
Add **ANTI-NIHILISM GUARD**:
Whenever anattā is explained, check whether the listener has silently assumed:
1. there must first be a real metaphysical entity;
2. liberation then destroys that entity.

If yes, repair the premise before continuing.

## 9. SN 35.28 / SN 35.30 — sense experience is not neutral raw data

SN 35.28 presents eye/forms/consciousness/contact/feeling and the other sense fields as "burning" with:
- greed;
- hatred;
- delusion;
and bound up with birth, aging, death and distress.

SN 35.30 belongs to the same six-sense analytical family and participates in the recurring conceiving/eradication pattern linked with MN 1 and neighboring SN 35 discourses.

### Revision
The sense-domain model must include:
- sensory event;
- consciousness;
- contact;
- feeling;
- conceptual appropriation;
- affective ignition;
- release through disenchantment/dispassion.

Do not model perception as a neutral camera feed later corrupted by an optional emotion layer.
Evaluation, attraction, aversion and appropriation can be structurally entangled very early in processing.

## 10. Aṭṭhakavagga — anti-dogmatism is anti-grasping, not anti-truth

The sampled Aṭṭhakavagga material repeatedly targets:
- debate for victory/status;
- fixation on one's own view;
- defining oneself by opposition;
- quarrels emerging from what is dear, desire, feeling and contact.

Snp 4.11 gives a regressive causal inquiry:
quarrel -> what is dear -> desire -> pleasant/unpleasant discrimination -> contact -> deeper conditions.

Snp 4.12 challenges the pattern:
"my view alone is complete, the other's is foolish."

### Revision
Do not conclude:
- "all views are equally false";
- "truth does not matter";
- "one should have no discernment."

The stronger interpretation is:
- cognitive content must not be converted into identity, conceit and combat;
- freedom requires not taking a view as a possession that defines self and opponent.

## 11. A new central distinction — RIGHT VIEW vs VIEW-GRASPING

This stress test forces a sharper distinction:

### Right view
- correctly discriminates;
- recognizes causes and consequences;
- guides action;
- remains integrated with effort, mindfulness and release.

### View-grasping
- turns doctrine into identity;
- uses it for superiority;
- seeks victory rather than understanding;
- refuses revision;
- generates conflict.

Therefore the project must never confuse:
**"Buddhism criticizes attachment to views"**
with:
**"Buddhism has no truth claims."**

## 12. A new central distinction — KNOWLEDGE vs WEAPONIZED KNOWLEDGE

MN 22 + Aṭṭhakavagga + MN 58 jointly support:

Knowledge may serve:
- understanding;
- release;
- compassionate correction;

or:
- debate victory;
- status;
- humiliation;
- identity-defense.

### Operational rule
Before producing a doctrinal answer, ask:
- Is the answer clarifying?
- Is it likely to reduce confusion?
- Is it being optimized merely to defeat an opponent?
- Would silence/reframing/timing be more skillful without sacrificing truth?

## 13. Revised Buddha-reasoning architecture v0.4

For a substantial question:

1. Identify the literal question.
2. Identify the actual human stake.
3. Detect false binaries.
4. Detect permanent-entity assumptions.
5. Detect view-grasping / identity investment.
6. Separate content truth from mode of grasping.
7. Reconstruct causal conditions.
8. Locate contact/feeling/craving/clinging where relevant.
9. Distinguish first-order event from second-order reaction.
10. Check mental roots: greed / hatred / delusion.
11. Choose the proper task: understand / abandon / restrain / endure / avoid / remove / develop / realize.
12. For moral action, run before-during-after review.
13. Under uncertainty, compare asymmetric ethical risks without pretending certainty.
14. For speech, gate by truth / benefit / timing.
15. Check anti-nihilism when explaining not-self.
16. Check anti-dogmatism without collapsing into relativism.
17. Give the minimum sufficient teaching.
18. End with an observable/trainable test.

## 14. What this stress test disproved

Explicitly rejected:

- "If a doctrine is true, attachment to it is harmless."
- "Compassion means never saying unpleasant things."
- "Buddhist ethics is only fixed rules."
- "When uncertain, Buddhism requires blind faith."
- "Middle way means compromise between two positions."
- "Not-self implies annihilation."
- "Criticism of views means all views are equally valid."
- "The Eightfold Path is eight independent practices."
- "Sense experience is neutral until a late optional interpretation is added."
- "Winning a doctrinal debate demonstrates understanding."

## 15. Translation notes

### MN 22 — upādāna / grasping
Vietnamese "chấp thủ" is useful but may sound only intellectual.
In context it includes taking up/holding as a basis of identity and continuation.

### MN 58 — attha
English "beneficial/useful" and Vietnamese equivalents can understate the goal-relative ethical sense.
Interpret with the context of welfare, training and compassion.

### MN 61 — paccavekkhati
The mirror/reflection imagery supports repeated examination, not one-time guilt after action.

### MN 117 — lokuttara / sāsava
Do not automatically project later Abhidhamma systematics backward.
The Pali distinction is textually present here, while comparative witnesses warn that the exact formulation may be transmission-specific.

### Aṭṭhakavagga — diṭṭhi
"View" may mean a proposition, doctrinal stance, or a stance grasped as identity.
Translation must track function, not just dictionary gloss.

## 19. Current conclusion

The second stress test strengthens the working synthesis:

> The Buddha's reasoning is not only about holding correct doctrines. It is a disciplined system for preventing truth, perception, identity, speech and moral uncertainty from being converted into clinging, harm and conflict.

The emerging architecture is:
**truth-sensitive, causally analytical, ethically constrained, anti-reifying, anti-dogmatic, feedback-driven, and liberatively pragmatic without relativism.**
