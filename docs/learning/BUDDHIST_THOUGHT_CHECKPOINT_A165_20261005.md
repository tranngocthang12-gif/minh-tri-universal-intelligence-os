## Central question

How can Buddhist thought distinguish confidence, certainty, preservation of truth, direct knowing, and attachment to being right?

## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. MN 95 — preserving truth is not the same as final realization

MN 95 distinguishes several bases on which a person may accept a proposition—faith, preference, oral tradition, reasoning, and reflective acceptance—and warns that what is accepted on such bases may turn out true or false.

The discourse therefore separates:

- honestly stating what one currently accepts;
- claiming exclusive truth;
- direct realization.

A person preserves truth by not overstating beyond the actual epistemic basis.

**Guardrail:** MN 95 does not teach that faith or reasoning are useless. It places them below direct realization when the claim is “this alone is true”.

### 2. MN 95 — confidence can be part of a path toward direct knowing

The discourse also gives a progression in which confidence in a teacher leads to approaching, listening, remembering, examining meaning, accepting reflectively, generating desire, applying effort, weighing, striving, and finally directly realizing truth.

This blocks the false opposition:

`faith vs investigation`.

Faith can function as a provisional support for inquiry.

### 3. MN 47 — the teacher is to be examined

MN 47 authorizes investigation of the Tathāgata through observable conduct and qualities, including whether wholesome qualities are long-standing or recently appearing.

This supports the project rule:

confidence in a teacher should not cancel examination.

The discourse culminates in a form of confidence grounded in reasons/seeing, not mere deference.

### 4. MN 22 — Dhamma is a means, not a possession

The raft simile distinguishes using Dhamma for crossing from grasping it afterward.

MN 22 also treats `diṭṭhi-nissaya` as a harmful dependence/support on view.

A165 therefore preserves:

- a teaching can be right and useful;
- using it does not require turning it into a permanent identity;
- correct view can be instrumental without becoming appropriative dependence.

### 5. Aṭṭhakavagga — dispute often appears where truth-claims become positions

Snp 4.8, 4.9, and 4.12 repeatedly connect exclusive truth-claims, comparison, fixed positions, and dispute.

**Guardrail:** this does not imply relativism or that truth does not exist.

The texts criticize fixation and competitive positioning, not truthfulness itself.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Confidence is not the same as attachment to being right

A165 uses the following distinction:

**confidence:** “this is well-supported enough to act on / continue investigating.”

**attachment to being right:** “if this claim changes, my status, identity, or worth is threatened.”

This wording is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS, not a canonical definition.

### B. Corrigibility does not require permanent doubt

A person can be strongly confident while remaining corrigible.

The key is whether confidence tracks evidence and direct seeing, or whether evidence is subordinated to defending a self-position.

Thus:

- humility != indecision;
- confidence != infallibility;
- revisability != lack of commitment.

This is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS.

### C. Three practical epistemic levels

A165 distinguishes:

1. **provisional acceptance** — belief, reasoning, tradition, trusted testimony;
2. **examined confidence** — belief strengthened through investigation and consistency;
3. **direct realization** — what is personally known/seen in the relevant domain.

This three-level model is a project synthesis from MN 95 + MN 47, not a canonical numbered taxonomy.

### D. “Only this is true” needs evidence-class control

When one moves from:

“this is what I currently accept”

to:

“only this is true and anything else is false”,

the evidential burden rises.

MN 95 is used as the principal guardrail against overstating one’s epistemic position.

### E. Being corrected need not become identity injury

A165 applies prior work on `māna`, `maññati`, and view-clinging:

a correction can be processed as:

- information about a claim;

rather than:

- defeat of the self.

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION.
