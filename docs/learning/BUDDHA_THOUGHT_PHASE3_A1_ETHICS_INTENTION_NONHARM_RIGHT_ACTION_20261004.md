## Research question

Why is ethics structurally necessary in the Buddha’s thought rather than merely a social convention? How do intention, non-harm, non-ill-will, truthfulness, right speech and right action connect to wisdom, concentration and liberation?

This pass stays inside the internal logic of the early Buddhist texts. Modern moral philosophy/science is not used as the interpretive frame.

## 1. MN 19 — ethical discrimination begins inside thought

MN 19 divides thought into two families:

### Unskillful
- sensuality,
- ill will,
- harmfulness.

### Skillful
- renunciation,
- non-ill-will,
- harmlessness.

The Buddha says the first group leads to:
- one’s own affliction,
- others’ affliction,
- both,
- obstruction of discernment,
- vexation,
- and does not lead to Nibbāna.

The second group does not lead to such harm and supports discernment and Nibbāna.

This is crucial:
ethics does not begin only at outward rules.
It begins at the direction of thought and intention.

## 2. Ethics is linked directly to wisdom and liberation

MN 19 does not say harmful thought is wrong only because society forbids it.

It says harmful/ill-willed thought:
- harms self/others,
- obstructs paññā/discernment,
- increases distress,
- does not lead to Nibbāna.

Thus early Buddhist ethics is structurally connected to the path:
**a harmful mind is also a mind moving away from liberating knowledge.**

This is one reason virtue is not an optional social layer added onto meditation.

## 3. MN 61 — the Buddha teaches an ethical method of repeated reflection

The mirror simile instructs Rāhula to reflect on bodily, verbal and mental actions:

### Before action
Will this action afflict:
- myself,
- others,
- both?

If yes, do not do it.

### During action
Is it actually producing such harm?

If yes, stop.

### After action
Did it produce harm?

If yes:
- acknowledge/reveal it where appropriate,
- learn from it,
- restrain oneself in future.

If it did not produce harm and was skillful:
- remain glad,
- continue developing skillful qualities.

This shows ethics as **training in truthful self-examination**, not merely obedience to a fixed list.

## 4. Truthfulness is foundational in MN 61

Before the mirror teaching, the Buddha warns Rāhula against deliberate lying, even in jest.

Why is this structurally important?

If one cannot truthfully acknowledge:
- intention,
- action,
- consequence,
then the whole before/during/after reflection system collapses.

Therefore truthfulness is not only social courtesy.
It is required for self-correction and wisdom.

Project synthesis:
**without truthfulness, one cannot reliably train kamma.**

## 5. AN 3.71 — greed, hate and delusion are ethically bad because they deform action and understanding

AN 3.71 explains the drawbacks of greed, hate and delusion.

When overcome by them, a person:
- intends harm to self,
- intends harm to others,
- intends harm to both,
- acts badly by body, speech and mind,
- does not understand what benefits self, others or both,
- blocks wisdom,
- moves toward distress rather than Nibbāna.

When greed, hate and delusion are abandoned, these patterns cease.

This connects the Nibbāna module directly with ethics:
**the very defilements whose destruction defines Nibbāna are also the roots of harmful conduct.**

Thus ethics and liberation are not separate projects.

## 6. MN 21 — non-hatred is required even under severe provocation

MN 21 trains monks so that under:
- blame,
- harsh speech,
- physical attack,
even the extreme saw simile,

they should not let inner hate take over, should not speak evil words, and should maintain goodwill.

This does not mean:
- pain is denied,
- injustice is declared good,
- discernment between right/wrong disappears.

The focus is:
**do not allow another person’s harmful act to reproduce hatred inside one’s own mind.**

The ethical danger is not only what the attacker does.
It is also what one’s own mind becomes in response.

## 7. Sn 1.8 — mettā is universalized beyond preference

Sn 1.8 asks for goodwill toward:
- weak and strong,
- large and small,
- seen and unseen,
- near and far,
- born and seeking birth.

It also says:
- do not deceive,
- do not despise,
- do not wish suffering on another through anger/resistance.

The mother-and-only-child comparison then expands the required heart toward all beings.

Important:
mettā here is not mere affection for people one likes.
It is a trained orientation free of hostility and ill will.

## 8. Right intention, right speech and right action form one ethical movement

MN 19 / path texts give:
### Right resolve/intention
- renunciation,
- non-ill-will,
- harmlessness.

MN 117 / SN 45.8 give:
### Right speech
abstaining from:
- lying,
- divisive speech,
- harsh speech,
- idle/frivolous speech.

### Right action
abstaining from:
- killing,
- taking what is not given,
- sexual misconduct / improper sexual conduct depending on audience/context.

These are not isolated commandments.

They line up as:
**mind orientation → speech → bodily action.**

The inner and outer dimensions of ethics are therefore continuous.

## 9. Cetanā and ethics

From the previous kamma module:
AN 6.63 places cetanā/intention at the center of kamma.

Now MN 19 and MN 61 show how intention is trained:
- notice what kind of thought/intention is arising,
- assess harm and consequence,
- abandon what leads to affliction,
- cultivate non-ill-will and harmlessness.

Thus:
**ethical training is the training of the causal source from which karmic action proceeds.**

This is an internal Buddhist connection, not a modern moral theory.

## 10. Ethics is not merely rule-following

The early material contains rules and abstentions, but the deeper structure is:

- intention matters,
- consequence matters,
- harm to self matters,
- harm to others matters,
- truthfulness matters,
- mental roots matter,
- repeated review matters,
- whether the action leads toward or away from Nibbāna matters.

Therefore sīla cannot be reduced to:
“obey social rules because authority says so.”

Nor should it be reduced to:
“do whatever feels compassionate.”

There is disciplined examination of intention, action and result.

## 11. Self-harm / other-harm / harm to both

MN 19, MN 61 and AN 3.71 repeatedly use the threefold frame:
- self,
- others,
- both.

This is philosophically important.

Early Buddhist ethics does not treat:
- self-interest,
- other-interest,
as necessarily opposed.

Greed/hate/delusion can damage all three:
- oneself,
- others,
- the relation between them.

Skillful action is evaluated in a field wider than one isolated agent.

## 12. Non-harm and wisdom are mutually linked

AN 3.71 says greed/hate/delusion obscure what is good for self, other and both and block wisdom.

MN 19 says harmful thought obstructs discernment.

Thus non-harm is not just a sentimental virtue.
It is a condition for seeing clearly.

Conversely, wisdom helps recognize:
- what harms,
- what causes harm,
- what should be abandoned.

So:
**ethics supports wisdom; wisdom guides ethics.**

This fits the project’s earlier finding that the path factors are mutually supporting.

## 13. Mettā is not a replacement for discernment

MN 21 teaches goodwill even toward an aggressor.

But early right view/right action texts still distinguish:
- killing from non-killing,
- lying from truthfulness,
- harmfulness from harmlessness.

Therefore mettā does not mean:
“everything anyone does is acceptable.”

The early structure is:
- recognize harmful conduct as harmful,
- do not reproduce hatred in response,
- act without ill will.

This distinction will be important in later compassion/social-thought passes.

## 14. Ethical speech as anti-papañca / anti-conflict practice

Phase 2A showed how:
- self-positioning,
- view-clinging,
- comparison,
- papañca
can end in:
- accusation,
- divisive speech,
- quarrel.

Right speech now gives the practical counter-movement:
- no lie,
- no divisive speech,
- no harsh speech,
- no empty speech that feeds confusion.

Thus right speech is not merely etiquette.
It is one place where the conflict-generating inner process is prevented from becoming social action.

This is a project synthesis grounded in MN 18 + right-speech texts.

## 15. Milindapañha stress-test — virtue is the ground

Milindapañha 3.1.9 says virtue/good conduct is the foundation on which:
- faith,
- energy,
- mindfulness,
- concentration,
- wisdom,
- awakening factors,
develop.

Nāgasena uses the earth/foundation analogy:
just as growth and construction require ground, development of the path depends on virtue.

This is strongly compatible with early path structure.

**MILINDA_COMPATIBLE.**

But the neat systematization of all qualities as resting on sīla is later explanatory organization and should not replace the earlier texts.

## 16. The first Phase-3 synthesis

The current internal-Buddhist model is:

greed / hate / delusion
→ harmful intention
→ harmful body/speech/mind action
→ affliction to self / others / both
→ agitation, remorse, obscured discernment
→ movement away from liberation.

Conversely:

non-greed / non-hate / non-delusion
+ renunciation / non-ill-will / harmlessness
→ right speech / right action
→ less affliction
→ less remorse/agitation
→ clearer mind
→ stronger concentration/discernment
→ movement toward release.

This is a SYNTHESIS, not a single canonical chain.

## 17. COUNTEREVIDENCE / GUARDRAILS

Reject:
1. “Buddhist ethics is only obeying rules.” — MN 61 emphasizes reflective examination before/during/after action.
2. “Only intention matters; consequences do not.” — MN 61 explicitly checks consequences.
3. “Only consequences matter; intention does not.” — kamma is centered on cetanā and MN 19 begins with thought/intention.
4. “Non-harm is separate from wisdom.” — MN 19 and AN 3.71 connect harm directly with obstruction of discernment.
5. “Mettā means approving harmful behavior.” — right view/right action still distinguish harmful conduct; mettā concerns the absence of hatred.
6. “Ethics is only for society; meditation/wisdom are the real path.” — ethics directly conditions mind, concentration and discernment.
7. “An arahant becomes morally indifferent because there is no self.” — the path explicitly removes greed/hate/delusion, the roots of harm.

## 18. CONFIDENCE

**EBT_STRONG / PĀLI TEXTUALLY STRONG**
- harmful thought is judged by harm to self/others/both and by obstruction of wisdom/liberation.
- bodily/verbal/mental action should be repeatedly reflected on for harm and result.
- greed/hate/delusion produce harmful intention/action and obscure wisdom.
- non-ill-will and harmlessness are positive path factors.
- right speech and right action are integral path factors.
- mettā is cultivated beyond personal preference.

**PARALLEL AUDIT PENDING**
- exact Chinese/Āgama parallels for MN 19, MN 21, MN 61 and AN 3.71 were not line-audited in this pass.

**MILINDA_COMPATIBLE**
- virtue as the ground for higher development.

## 19. Core synthesis

> In the Buddha’s thought, ethics is not a separate social code added onto liberation. Greed, hatred and delusion are simultaneously roots of suffering, distorted understanding, harmful intention and harmful action. Training thought, speech and action in non-ill-will, harmlessness and truthfulness therefore directly changes the same causal structure that the path is trying to end. Sīla protects others, but it also makes the practitioner’s own mind more truthful, less agitated and more capable of concentration and wisdom.

This is a SYNTHESIS.
