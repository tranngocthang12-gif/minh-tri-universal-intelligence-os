# PHASE 4 — A14 — RIGHT VIEW WITHOUT VIEW-CLINGING

Status: CHAT_TAUGHT / PARTIAL_SOURCE_AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-04

Question: If the Aṭṭhakavagga warns against clinging to views, why does the Noble Eightfold Path begin with Right View?

## MN 117
- TEXT_ATTESTED: right view comes first because it recognizes wrong view as wrong and right view as right; right view guides the other path factors.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 117 distinguishes a right view still accompanied by defilements/attachments from a noble, undefiled, transcendent right view functioning as wisdom and a path factor.
- SOURCE CAUTION: this two-level formulation is not found in all Chinese/Tibetan parallels in the same form; use it as attested in the Pāli MN 117 and avoid overgeneralizing its exact formulation as pan-canonical without parallel audit.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: right view is not merely a proposition to defend; in the noble path it becomes a mode of discernment that coordinates effort and mindfulness and leads toward freedom.

## MN 22 and MN 38
- TEXT_ATTESTED: Dhamma is a raft for crossing, not for holding on.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: MN 38 explicitly asks whether one would understand the raft simile if one cherished, treasured, adhered to, and regarded as "mine" even a pure and bright view; the correct answer is no.
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: a view can be epistemically correct yet used wrongly if appropriated as identity.

## SN 12.15
- TEXT_ATTESTED: right view avoids the dual extremes "everything exists" and "nothing exists".
- TEXT_ATTESTED: right view is connected with seeing arising and cessation accurately and with not clinging to the thought "my self".
- CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS: right view in this discourse functions by loosening ontological fixation rather than replacing one absolute dogma with another.

## Aṭṭhakavagga bounded lexical audit — Snp 4.12
- TEXT_ATTESTED / lexical: `sacca` occurs in the claim that "truth is one" and in the dispute where different groups make their own views into truths.
- TEXT_ATTESTED / lexical: `saññā` occurs in the statement that apart from perception/construal, there are not many different permanent truths in the world.
- TEXT_ATTESTED: the discourse criticizes dependence on what is seen/heard/sensed, practices, and self-authorized decisions when these become grounds for conceit, denigration, and dispute.
- GUARDRAIL: this does not license the claim "there is no truth" or "all views are equally false". The discourse itself preserves a truth/falsehood distinction while attacking appropriation and entrenchment.
- AUDIT BOUNDARY: this closes only a bounded `sacca`/`saññā` slice in Snp 4.12. Wider lexical coverage remains open.

## Reconciliation
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
Right View and non-clinging to views are not opposites. Right View is the discriminative, path-guiding capacity to see what is wrong/right, suffering/origin/cessation/path, and arising/cessation. View-clinging is the appropriation of a cognitive position as "mine", using it for identity, conceit, or dispute. The path therefore begins with Right View precisely because one needs an orientation that can diagnose and eventually dismantle the clinging mechanism, including clinging to views.

A useful learning distinction:
1. orientation: "this is skillful / unskillful; this leads to suffering / cessation";
2. verification: see arising and cessation directly;
3. non-appropriation: do not convert even correct insight into "my doctrine / my superiority / my self";
4. release: the cognitive tool has done its work when it no longer feeds clinging.

## Milindapañha dialectical layer
- MILINDAPANHA_DIALECTIC: Nāgasena's question-classification method and chariot/person reasoning illustrate how distinctions can be used rigorously without reifying them into permanent entities.
- PROJECT_INTERPRETATION: Mi Tiên is especially useful here as a discipline of argument: test a proposition, expose hidden assumptions, preserve valid distinctions, and avoid turning the successful distinction into identity.
- Guardrail: this is a methodological use of Milindapañha; do not claim an exact Milinda passage says "Right View is not view-clinging" unless directly audited.

## Main conclusion
CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS:
The apparent tension disappears once "view" is separated from "clinging to a view". Early Buddhist practice requires accurate orientation and discernment; what it rejects is appropriation, absolutization, conceit, and identity built from views. Right View matures not into a stronger ideological possession but into seeing that no longer needs to say "this insight is mine".
