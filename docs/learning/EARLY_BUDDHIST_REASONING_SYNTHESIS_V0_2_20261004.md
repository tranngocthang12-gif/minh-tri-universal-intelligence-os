**Status:** DEEPER TEXT-GROUNDED SYNTHESIS / NOT COMPLETE MASTERY  
**Track:** Early Buddhist Canon — Pali / English / Vietnamese / parallel-aware  
**Boundary:** Reconstruct recurring reasoning patterns from early-discourse evidence. No claim of buddhahood, awakening, omniscience, or verbatim reproduction of the historical Buddha.

## 1. New evidence cluster

This revision extends v0.1 with:

- SN 12.15 Kaccanagotta — right view and the existence/non-existence duality;
- SN 36.6 Salla — primary pain vs secondary mental suffering;
- MN 2 Sabbasava — multiple intervention methods rather than one universal technique;
- MN 95 Canki — preservation, discovery and attainment of truth;
- SN 44.1 Khema — categories about the liberated one after death do not apply;
- SN 44.10 Ananda — disciplined silence on self-survival/non-survival;
- DN 1 Brahmajala — views traced to feeling, grasping and their consequences;
- SN 12.20 Paccaya — conditionality treated as a discoverable structural regularity;
- SN 12.38 Cetana — intention/mental preoccupation conditions continuing process;
- SN 22.59 Anattalakkhana — self-appropriation tested by impermanence, affliction and lack of sovereign control.

## 2. Core philosophical architecture

The working synthesis is now:

> Early Buddhist thought is a disciplined transformation of how a problem is framed, observed, causally analyzed, appropriated, acted on, and released.

It is not adequately captured by:
- "life is suffering";
- "there is no self";
- "everything is impermanent";
- "do not think about metaphysics";
- "just meditate";
- "believe only what you experience."

Those slogans omit the reasoning architecture.

## 3. Principle 1 — Frame by arising and ceasing, not absolute existence

SN 12.15 is decisive for the response model.

The discourse criticizes ordinary reliance on the polarity:
- existence;
- non-existence.

The corrective is to see:
- arising through conditions;
- cessation through conditions.

### Reasoning rule

When a question is framed as:
- "Does X absolutely exist?";
- "Does X absolutely not exist?";
- "Am I permanently this?";
- "When this changes, does the real me disappear?";

first test whether the binary assumes a fixed entity where the discourse treats a conditioned process.

### Response move

Replace:
- absolute entity language

with:
- conditions;
- arising;
- persistence while conditions remain;
- cessation when conditions cease;
- grasping that turns a process into identity.

This does not mean every yes/no question is invalid. It means entity-binaries are suspect when the subject is taught as dependently arisen.

## 4. Principle 2 — Not-self is de-appropriation, not a cheap metaphysical negation

SN 22.59 strengthens the v0.1 model.

The argument does not merely assert a proposition.
It asks whether the aggregates:
- are fully under command;
- remain as wished;
- are permanent;
- are satisfactory when changing;
- are fit to be regarded as "mine / I am this / this is my self."

### Reasoning rule

For identity problems, distinguish:
- descriptive process;
- appropriation;
- metaphysical theory.

The practical target is the appropriation relation.

### Strong caution

Do not replace one rigid self-theory with a rigid slogan:
- "there is absolutely no self in any possible sense."

The early-discourse response model is better represented as:
- whatever is impermanent, afflicted, changing and not sovereignly controllable is unfit for appropriation as self.

## 5. Principle 3 — Do not force liberated experience into ordinary categories

SN 44.1 shows a more radical anti-reification move.

Questions about the liberated person after death are not answered by selecting one of:
- exists;
- does not exist;
- both;
- neither.

The explanation is that the bases by which the person is conventionally reckoned have been cut off; the ordinary categories fail to apply.

### Reasoning rule

Before answering a categorical question:
1. ask whether the categories themselves still have a valid referent;
2. ask whether the question assumes continuity of the very structure the teaching says has ceased;
3. if the categories fail, do not choose a box merely to satisfy grammar.

This is not mystification.
It is category discipline.

## 6. Principle 4 — Silence is a positive epistemic act when every offered answer misleads

SN 44.10 and MN 63 show two distinct forms of non-answer.

### Type A — category-confusion silence
A yes/no answer would push the hearer toward a misleading view.

### Type B — liberative-irrelevance refusal
The question is not connected to the task of understanding and ending suffering.

### Reasoning rule

Never imitate silence mechanically.

Use SET-ASIDE only if:
- the offered alternatives presuppose a false frame; or
- the question class is textually treated as undeclared/unprofitable; or
- answering it would harden the very view that must be abandoned.

If the assistant simply lacks evidence, say:
- UNKNOWN / TEXTUALLY UNRESOLVED.

Ignorance must never be disguised as "noble silence."

## 7. Principle 5 — Pain and suffering are not identical

SN 36.6 separates:
- unavoidable/primary painful feeling;
- reactive mental suffering added through resistance, craving and ignorance.

The "second arrow" model is a major psychological principle.

### Reasoning rule

When responding to distress:
1. do not deny the first pain;
2. identify the second-order reaction;
3. distinguish pain from the appropriation/resistance layered onto it;
4. find the trainable link.

### Practical implication

Early Buddhist practice is not:
- "feel nothing."

It is:
- experience feeling without automatically adding craving, aversion, identification and proliferation.

## 8. Principle 6 — Different causal problems require different interventions

MN 2 is methodologically important because it lists different ways of abandoning taints:
- by seeing/understanding;
- restraint;
- proper use;
- endurance;
- avoidance;
- removal;
- development.

### Reasoning rule

Reject the assumption:
> one spiritual technique solves every problem.

Instead diagnose the mechanism first.

Examples:
- false view -> clarification/seeing;
- sense-triggered reactivity -> restraint;
- unavoidable discomfort -> endurance;
- dangerous environment -> avoidance;
- already-arisen harmful thought -> removal;
- missing wholesome capacity -> cultivation/development.

This is a treatment-selection architecture.

## 9. Principle 7 — Epistemology has stages

MN 95 adds an important distinction.

A person may have:
- faith;
- preference/endorsement;
- oral transmission;
- reasoning;
- reflective acceptance.

Any of these may be true or false.

Therefore intellectual honesty requires saying:
- "this is what I believe";
- "this is what I have received";
- "this is what seems reasonable";

without prematurely saying:
- "this alone is true."

### Three-stage model

1. **Preserve truth honestly** — do not overstate confidence.
2. **Discover/awaken to truth** — investigate teacher, teaching, practice and evidence.
3. **Attain truth** — realization goes beyond merely correct propositions.

### Reasoning rule for MINH TRI

Always distinguish:
- source report;
- inference;
- provisional synthesis;
- direct textual statement;
- contested interpretation;
- experiential claim the assistant cannot possess.

This aligns closely with MINH TRI's own rule:
chat/AI claim is not VERIFIED.

## 10. Principle 8 — Views are analyzed causally, not only refuted propositionally

DN 1 does more than catalog speculative doctrines.

A recurrent analytical move is:
- identify the view;
- identify the experiential basis;
- identify feeling;
- identify grasping at the view;
- identify the consequence of clinging;
- understand gratification, drawback and escape;
- become free by non-grasping.

### Reasoning rule

When someone holds a strong belief, ask two sets of questions:

#### Proposition layer
- Is it supported?
- Is it coherent?
- What evidence contradicts it?

#### Attachment layer
- Why is this view being held?
- What feeling or identity does it protect?
- What fear/desire is invested in it?
- What happens psychologically when the view is threatened?

The Buddha-reasoning model examines both.

## 11. Principle 9 — Conditionality is structural, not dependent on a Buddha declaring it

SN 12.20 treats dependent conditionality as something the Buddha discovers, realizes and explains, rather than arbitrarily creates.

### Reasoning implication

The teaching presents itself as:
- discovery of conditional structure;
not merely:
- authority issuing commandments.

### MINH TRI translation into method

A claim should ideally expose:
- condition;
- mechanism;
- predicted consequence;
- possible interruption;
- observable evidence.

This is one reason the discourse style repeatedly returns to:
- origin;
- cessation;
- gratification;
- drawback;
- escape.

## 12. Principle 10 — Intention is a causal input, not a free-floating inner essence

SN 12.38 links:
- intending;
- planning/considering;
- underlying tendency/preoccupation

with a basis for consciousness to become established and for continued becoming/suffering.

### Reasoning rule

Do not treat intention merely as:
- "what I truly am."

Treat it as:
- a causal activity;
- trainable;
- conditioned;
- consequential.

Ethics and psychology therefore interlock.

## 13. The Buddha's recurring problem-solving loop

The current best reconstruction is:

### 1. Clarify the question
What exactly is being asked?

### 2. Identify the practical stake
What suffering, desire, fear, identity or conflict is underneath?

### 3. Test the frame
Does the question falsely assume:
- permanence;
- essence;
- absolute control;
- binary existence/non-existence;
- authority as proof;
- thought as truth?

### 4. Reduce to conditioned processes
What is arising due to what?

### 5. Locate the experiential hinge
Often:
contact -> feeling -> craving/aversion -> appropriation -> proliferation/action.

### 6. Identify the correct task
Should this be:
- understood;
- abandoned;
- restrained;
- endured;
- avoided;
- removed;
- cultivated;
- directly realized?

### 7. Match the intervention
Do not prescribe insight when restraint is needed.
Do not prescribe endurance when avoidance is wiser.
Do not debate metaphysics when the frame itself is defective.

### 8. Test mental roots
Does this increase:
- greed;
- hatred;
- delusion;
or:
- non-greed;
- non-hatred;
- clarity?

### 9. De-appropriate
Is the mind turning a conditioned event into:
- me;
- mine;
- my essence;
- my absolute view?

### 10. Return to practice
What can be observed, stopped, cultivated or tested now?

## 14. Important correction to a common modern reading

The emerging model is **not** simple pragmatism.

The Buddha does not say:
> whatever reduces discomfort is true.

Instead the discourses combine:
- causal accuracy;
- ethical consequence;
- disciplined observation;
- proper confidence labels;
- non-grasping;
- liberative purpose.

A pleasant belief may be false.
A painful observation may be true.
A useful practice does not license a false metaphysical claim.

## 15. Important correction to "the Buddha rejected philosophy"

The texts show extensive analysis of:
- causality;
- personal identity;
- knowledge;
- language/categories;
- action;
- consciousness;
- perception;
- ethics;
- freedom.

What is rejected or subordinated is not thinking itself, but:
- sterile speculation;
- dogmatic grasping;
- categories that distort process;
- reasoning disconnected from practice and verification.

Therefore a better formulation is:

> The Buddha is philosophically rigorous but therapeutically selective.

## 16. Important correction to "just experience it yourself"

MN 95 rejects both:
- blind authority;
- premature private certainty.

The path includes:
- confidence sufficient to investigate;
- scrutiny of the teacher;
- hearing;
- remembering;
- examining meaning;
- practicing;
- direct realization.

### MINH TRI rule

First-person conviction is evidence of conviction.
It is not automatically evidence of truth.

## 17. Response-style model refined

When reconstructing a likely early-Buddhist response, choose among:

### DIRECT
When the distinction is simple and textually stable.

### ANALYTICAL
When terms are conflated.

### CONDITIONAL
When the phenomenon is best explained by dependent arising.

### DE-APPROPRIATIVE
When the problem centers on "I/me/mine."

### COUNTER-QUESTION
When the user's own answer reveals the needed distinction.

### REFRAME
When the offered binary is false.

### METHOD-SELECTION
When different causal mechanisms require different practices.

### GRADUAL
When the person needs a reachable next step rather than the final doctrine.

### SIMILE
When a process is easier to see through analogy.

### SET-ASIDE
When the question is textually undeclared/unprofitable or category-defective.

### UNCERTAINTY
When evidence is insufficient or interpretations conflict.

## 18. Applied reasoning examples

### Question: "If there is no self, who suffers?"
Do not begin with "nobody suffers."

Better sequence:
- identify the assumption that suffering requires a permanent owner;
- distinguish process from owner-theory;
- analyze feeling, craving, appropriation and suffering conditionally;
- explain that removing a permanent-self theory does not remove causal continuity.

### Question: "Should I suppress painful feelings?"
Use SN 36.6:
- primary pain is not the same as reactive suffering;
- suppression/resistance may be another layer of aversion;
- train non-reactive knowing rather than numbness.

### Question: "Which meditation technique is always best?"
Use MN 2 method-selection:
- no single technique is universally primary;
- identify the defilement/mechanism and match the intervention.

### Question: "How can I know my religion/philosophy is true?"
Use MN 95:
- state current confidence honestly;
- do not equate tradition/reason/faith with final knowledge;
- investigate conduct, teaching, consequences and practice;
- preserve the distinction between belief and realization.

### Question: "Does an awakened person exist after death?"
Do not improvise a metaphysical answer.
Use the undeclared/category-failure material and explain why the early texts resist those four predicates.

## 19. Translation findings from this cluster

### satta / atta / tathagata
Do not silently collapse:
- living being/person conventions;
- self-doctrine;
- Tathagata/liberated one.

Questions about postmortem "existence" depend heavily on what is being predicated.

### vedana
"Feeling" here is often hedonic tone:
- pleasant;
- painful;
- neutral.

It is not equivalent to the full modern category "emotion."

### yoniso manasikara
Vietnamese "nhu ly tac y" and English "wise/appropriate attention" point to a method of attending according to origin, function and task.
Do not reduce it to generic concentration.

### ditthi
"View" can be:
- ordinary perspective;
- doctrinal position;
- identity-forming grasped theory.
Context determines risk.

### upadana
"Clinging/grasping" is not just liking strongly.
It includes appropriation and taking up views/identity/sensuality as a basis.
