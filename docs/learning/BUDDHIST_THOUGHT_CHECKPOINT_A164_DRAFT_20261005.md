**Topic:** Knowing without possession — direct knowing, conceptual knowledge, confidence, and the danger of turning knowledge into identity.

## Central question

If the Buddha teaches right view, knowing, and direct realization, how can one know something as true without converting that knowing into "I am the knower", superiority, or a new object of clinging?

## TEXT_ATTESTED

### 1. MN 95 — belief and reasoning are not yet realization

MN 95 distinguishes several grounds on which a person may hold a conviction:
- faith;
- preference;
- oral tradition;
- reasoning;
- reflective acceptance of a view.

These may be true or false.

The discourse then lays out a gradual process in which trust, listening, examination, practice, and direct realization culminate in knowledge of truth.

A164 therefore preserves:

**being convinced is not identical to knowing directly.**

### 2. MN 1 — direct knowing is distinguished from conceiving

MN 1 contrasts the uninstructed person's pattern:
- perceiving;
- conceiving (`maññati`);
- conceiving in relation to;
- conceiving "mine";
- delighting;

with the instructed/arahant/Tathāgata pattern of direct knowledge and non-conceiving.

The crucial point for A164:

**direct knowing does not require adding possessive or self-referential conceiving to the known object.**

### 3. SN 12.15 — right seeing without "my self" fixation

SN 12.15 describes right view in terms of seeing arising and cessation with right wisdom while not being bound by attachment, fixation, or "my self".

It therefore gives a model in which:
- something can be seen correctly;
- yet the seeing need not be organized around self-possession.

### 4. SN 22.89 — removal of explicit self-view does not immediately remove all "I am" conceit

Khemaka does not regard any aggregate as self or belonging to self, yet a residual "I am" conceit/tendency remains.

This is vital for A164:

A person may have corrected explicit doctrine and still retain a subtler self-positioning structure.

Therefore:
`correct view != complete absence of selfing`.

### 5. Snp 4.9 — knowledge itself is not to become a basis for comparison

Snp 4.9 says the peaceful person does not construct superiority, inferiority, or equality from what has been seen, heard, cognized, or otherwise known.

This directly constrains epistemic pride:

**knowledge is not supposed to become a platform for rank.**

### 6. Snp 4.14 — do not conceive oneself superior, inferior, or equal because of what is known

Snp 4.14 likewise warns against conceiving oneself through comparative rank on the basis of knowledge.

This is an especially direct bridge between:
- knowledge;
- `maññeyya`;
- comparison;
- self-positioning.

## CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS

### A. Four layers should be separated

A164 distinguishes:

1. **information received** — what one has heard or learned;
2. **conceptual judgment** — what one currently thinks is true;
3. **direct knowing/realization** — what has been personally verified in the relevant sense;
4. **identity built around knowing** — "I am knowledgeable", "I understand better", "I am the one who sees".

The first three can be epistemically legitimate.
The fourth is a new site of appropriation.

This four-layer model is CROSS_TEXT_SYNTHESIS, not a canonical taxonomy.

### B. Knowledge can be correct while self-positioning around knowledge is still unwholesome

SN 22.89 shows that explicit doctrinal correction and residual "I am" can coexist.

Snp 4.9/4.14 show that knowledge can become fuel for superiority/inferiority/equality comparison.

Therefore the relevant danger is not knowledge itself.

The danger is:
`knowledge -> mine -> me -> rank -> defense -> conflict`.

This chain is CROSS_TEXT SYNTHESIS, not one canonical sequence.

### C. Epistemic humility is not uncertainty about everything

A164 rejects two extremes:

- **dogmatic possession:** "I know, therefore I am superior and beyond correction";
- **performative uncertainty:** "I must never say anything is true."

MN 95 allows graded confidence and eventual realization.
SN 12.15 affirms right view.

Humility therefore means:
- say what you know;
- say how you know it;
- do not claim more than the evidence;
- do not turn correctness into self-worth.

### D. The strongest knowing reduces, rather than strengthens, self-appropriation

This is a major cross-text inference:

If direct knowing in MN 1 is paired with non-conceiving,
and right view in SN 12.15 is paired with absence of self-fixation,
then increasing insight should not increase identity possession.

A claim such as:
"the more awakened I am, the more important my identity as a knower becomes"

runs opposite to the direction of these texts.

## LATER/PARACANONICAL — MILINDAPAÑHA

- reasoning that apprehends;
- wisdom that cuts through defilement and illuminates.

Role in A164:
- supports the distinction between conceptual correctness and transformative insight;
- emphasizes that wisdom is judged partly by what it removes, not merely by how many propositions one can state.

## Practical understanding

When learning something deeply, ask:

1. What exactly do I know?
2. What is merely plausible or inferred?
3. What have I personally verified, and in what sense?
4. Am I using knowledge to reduce confusion and suffering, or to build rank?
5. If corrected, does the correction threaten the claim — or threaten "me"?
6. Can I say "I was wrong" without feeling that my worth has collapsed?
7. Does deeper understanding make me less defensive, less comparative, and less possessive?

This is CROSS_TEXT APPLICATION.
